Home › Articles › Climate in Santa Cruz: when to travel, wind and temperatures

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-12

The climate in Santa Cruz shapes a trip more than anything else. It decides which routes to take, how many daylight hours you will have to drive and what clothes to pack. The province is huge and the climate changes a lot between the Atlantic coast, the central plateau and the Andes.

Three traits repeat across almost the whole territory: it is dry, it is cold for much of the year and it is windy. Most of the province has a cold semi-arid (steppe) climate. On the coast and the plateau it rains little: between 150 and 280 mm a year depending on the town. The exception is the Andean edge, where rainfall rises a lot.

The three climate zones of Santa Cruz

Atlantic coast

It includes Caleta Olivia, Puerto Deseado, Puerto San Julián, Puerto Santa Cruz and Río Gallegos. The sea softens the extremes a little, but the wind is constant. In Río Gallegos the average January high is 20 °C and the average July low is −2.3 °C (1991-2020 normals). It rains about 277 mm a year, spread over around 97 days with precipitation. There are on average 14.6 days of snow a year, almost all between June and August. For Puerto Deseado, see Climate in Puerto Deseado: when to go.

Central plateau

This is the area of Gobernador Gregores, Bajo Caracoles and Las Heras. It is the driest zone and the one with the widest temperature range. In Gobernador Gregores the average January high is around 21.6 °C and the average June low drops to −4.1 °C. Annual precipitation is about 155 mm. Note that these values come from old series (the 1950s to the 1980s), so take them as a general reference.

Andes and lakes

It covers El Chaltén, El Calafate, Lago Posadas, Perito Moreno and Los Antiguos. Along the lakes the climate is still dry: El Calafate gets about 161 mm a year and Perito Moreno about 163 mm. But in El Chaltén, right against the mountains, the historical average is about 808 mm a year, with frequent bad weather and rain spread over many days.

Wind: the factor that weighs the most

In Santa Cruz the wind changes the wind chill and also the way you drive. In Río Gallegos the average monthly speed goes from about 19 km/h in June and July to about 27.6 km/h in December. The windiest months are November, December and January, right in high season. Gusts can exceed 100 km/h, even in summer.

  • An 18 °C day with strong wind feels much colder. Always bring a windbreaker.
  • On the road, crosswinds push the car, especially on open plateau stretches. Slow down and hold the wheel firmly.

We did not find monthly average wind data for the plateau or the Andes in official sources, so we do not give any. For the day's forecast, use the Servicio Meteorológico Nacional.

In summer you can drive long stretches and take excursions until late. In winter you get less than 8 hours of light: plan short legs and do not drive at night on gravel roads.

When to go to Santa Cruz: season by season

Summer (December to February)

It is the mildest time, high season and the season of the longest days. Parques Nacionales says the ideal season to visit Monte León National Park is from mid-December to February. The downside: it is the windiest time on the coast and the one with the most demand for lodging.

Autumn (March to May)

In March the wind starts to ease. From May, frosts are frequent inland.

Winter (June to August)

It is the coldest time and the one with the least light. Average July highs are around 5 to 7 °C and average lows between −1.5 and −4.4 °C. There is snowfall, mostly in the Andes and on the plateau. Some road stretches can ice over: in May 2025 Route 40 between La Esperanza and El Calafate was left with frost and snow and could only be driven with chains, according to Diario Jornada. If you travel in winter, check road conditions with Vialidad Nacional and Vialidad Provincial before leaving. More on the road: Route 40 in Santa Cruz: fuel, gravel and distances.

Spring (September to November)

Temperatures rise little by little, it rains little and the days lengthen quickly. The wind picks up again toward November. Some tourist services in the Andes only open in high season: check before you travel.

Which month to pick for your plan

  • Trekking in El Chaltén: summer is high season because of the light and the temperatures. More ideas in What to do in El Chaltén besides Fitz Roy.
  • Full Route 40: late spring and summer are best, to avoid ice and make the most of the daylight. See Route 40 north.
  • Coastal wildlife: the Magellanic penguin colony at Monte León has more than 40,000 breeding pairs, according to Parques Nacionales. Dates for each penguin colony are in Wildlife watching on the Santa Cruz coast.

What to pack

  • Layered clothing: thermal shirt, sweater or fleece and a windproof, waterproof jacket.
  • Hat, gloves and neck warmer even in summer.
  • Sunscreen and sunglasses: the air is dry and in summer there are many hours of sun. Río Gallegos gets about 2,067 hours of sun a year.
  • If you will drive in winter: chains and extra warm clothes in the car.

If you are driving from the coast, also see Route 3 in Santa Cruz: stretches, distances and stops.

Frequently asked questions

Is it very cold in Santa Cruz in summer?

Not so much by day, but yes at night. Average January highs are between 19 and 22 °C and lows between 7 and 9 °C. With wind, the wind chill drops a lot.

Which is the windiest month?

In Río Gallegos, December, with an average of 27.6 km/h, followed by November and January. The least windy are June and July, at about 19 km/h.

Does it rain a lot in Santa Cruz?

In most of the province, no: between 150 and 280 mm a year. The exception is the El Chaltén area, with a historical average of about 808 mm a year.

Does it snow in Santa Cruz?

Yes, mostly from June to August. Río Gallegos gets about 14.6 days of snow a year and El Calafate about 20.4.
