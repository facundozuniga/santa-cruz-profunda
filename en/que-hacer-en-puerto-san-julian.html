Home › Articles › Puerto San Julián guide: things to do, how to get there and how many days to stay

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-09

Puerto San Julián is a town on the Atlantic coast of Santa Cruz, on the bay of the same name, which according to Argentina's Federal System of Protected Areas opens between Cabo Curioso and Punta Desengaño. For many it's just a stop on Route 3, but this is where Magellan's expedition spent the winter of 1520 and where the first Mass in what is now Argentina was celebrated.

Today the town combines that history with a bay where Magellanic penguins nest and Commerson's dolphins swim, beaches and cliffs to the north, a provincial reserve on the peninsula and, inland, rock art thousands of years old and the lowest point in the Americas.

This Puerto San Julián guide brings together what you need to plan the trip. For places to stay and eat, see the San Julián directory; the general overview is at Puerto San Julián.

Where it is and how to get there

San Julián is in east-central Santa Cruz, on Ruta Nacional 3. Distances vary a little depending on the source:

FromApproximate distance
Río Gallegos350 to 360 km
Comodoro Rivadavia430 to 461 km
Buenos Airesabout 2,250 km (Welcome Argentina)

More details in Where is Puerto San Julián?

By car

From Buenos Aires it's two or three days of driving, almost all on Route 3; Bahía Blanca and Puerto Madryn are good places to spend the night. For the Santa Cruz stretch see The coast along Ruta 3.

By bus

There are long-distance services from Retiro, a trip of more than 30 hours, and the companies that run along Route 3 stop in San Julián. We found no up-to-date official information on companies and frequencies: check with the Tourism Office before you travel.

By air

The town has an airport, but as of November 3, 2026 we found no scheduled flights in operation. The province is working with the airline American Jet to reactivate it, first for mining-related flights (Prensa Santa Cruz, October 2025), and in June 2026 the company said it is studying extending the scheme to San Julián and Puerto Deseado, with no date. The provincial website lists Comodoro Rivadavia as the nearest airport; you can also fly to Río Gallegos and continue overland.

A bit of history

DateEvent
31/3/1520Magellan's expedition arrives and names the bay.
1/4/1520Palm Sunday: first Mass in what is now Argentina.
1520Mutiny against Magellan, who puts it down with executions. When leaving, the expedition maroons the mutinous chaplain on the islet that has since been called Isla Justicia, according to Wikipedia.
1780Antonio de Viedma founds the Spanish colony of Floridablanca, about 10 km northwest of the present-day town.
1783–1784A Royal Order of August 1, 1783 decides to abandon the colony; it is dismantled in 1784.
1834Charles Darwin stops at the port aboard the Beagle.
17/9/1901Founding date of San Julián as a town.
1912–1967The Swift meatpacking plant operates and drives the town's economy, according to the local guide San Julián Patagonia (other sources give 1910 or 1911 as the start).

According to the province, the expedition members called the Tehuelche people they met here “patagones” because of their great stature, and that is where the name Patagonia comes from.

Things to do in the city

Nao Victoria Theme Museum

It's a full-scale replica of the Nao Victoria, the ship in Magellan's fleet that completed the first circumnavigation of the globe. Inside, surround sound, replica navigation instruments and crew figures show what sailors' lives were like in the 16th century. It's at Av. San Martín and Costanera.

Heads up: as of November 3, 2026, the municipal tourism website says the museum remains closed for maintenance until further notice. You can still see the replica from the waterfront; to find out whether it has reopened, and its hours and admission, check before you travel at Turismo Puerto San Julián (municipal) – ¿Qué visitar?.

Punto Cero, the First Mass and Malvinas memorials

At the foot of the Nao Victoria is Punto Cero, which symbolically marks the start of Patagonia and has at its center a map by the chronicler Antonio Pigafetta. Nearby are the First Mass Monument, with its altar and great cross, and the Mirage Dagger aircraft that commemorates the first mission over the islands from the San Julián air base, on May 1, 1982.

Rosa Novak Museum (Regional and Marine Art Museum)

According to the municipal website, the museum opened on September 17, 1972 with the collection donated by Rosa Novak de Hoffman and her family, with stone tools and objects of the Tehuelche people. In 1980 a marine art collection was added and it was renamed the Regional and Marine Art Museum. InterPatagonia mentions remains from the Floridablanca colony, a pioneers' room and Indigenous objects. It's at Av. Hernando de Magallanes and Rivadavia, tel. (02962) 41-4384; opening hours: check before you travel at Turismo Puerto San Julián (municipal) – ¿Qué visitar?.

Museo del Campo and Magellanic-style houses

The Museo del Campo (Belgrano 1062), opened in 2013, tells the story of the pioneer families and the sheep farming that made the town grow. In the center there are still old Magellanic-style houses, which InterPatagonia lists among the town's attractions.

Camino de la Luz (Via Lucis)

It's a theme park with a trail of fourteen stations devoted to the resurrection of Jesus, with bas-reliefs decorated with seashells and stones from the coast and plaques in braille. According to the municipal website, admission is free, and the path ends at a viewpoint over the town and the beaches. Welcome Argentina places it 8 km from San Julián, in the Coastal Circuit area.

Boat trip on the bay: penguins and Commerson's dolphins

The boat excursion is the highlight of San Julián. According to the province, the trip visits the islets of Banco Cormorán, Isla de los Pingüinos and Banco Justicia:

  • Isla (or Banco) Justicia: the islet linked to the 1520 mutiny, today a refuge for birds such as imperial cormorants, gulls and neotropic cormorants.
  • Isla Cormorán, also called Penguin Island: it has a colony of more than 37,000 pairs of Magellanic penguins, according to SiFAP (InterPatagonia speaks of more than 120,000 penguins). Some trips include a walk among the penguins.

During the trip it's common to see Commerson's dolphins, the black-and-white dolphins that swim and leap alongside the boats. SiFAP records a population of at least 42 of them in the bay. There are also cormorants, terns, oystercatchers, skuas and petrels.

  • Duration: about an hour and a quarter on average, according to InterPatagonia.
  • Frequency: the same source says that in January and February there are departures every day, every two hours.
  • Season: penguins from October to April; dolphins all year round (InterPatagonia).
  • Weather: trips are cancelled in bad weather. Confirm even if you have a booking.

The operator listed on the municipal website appears in the adventure and sports section; check this season's schedules and prices before you travel.

Exploring the coast

Coastal Circuit (to the north)

According to the municipal website, it runs 27 km north of town, and the first 10 km follow the northern shore of the bay. The province has a free official audio guide: the route ends at Playa La Mina, near Route 3, and takes about an hour and a half at around 30 km/h. Download the tracks before you leave, because the signal drops around the Sholl Tomb. Some stops:

  • Sholl Tomb: that of Lieutenant Robert Sholl, who died aboard the Beagle in 1828, according to the local guide.
  • Ruins of the Swift meatpacking plant and the Swift 12 tugboat, beached next to it.
  • Playa Los Caracoles: the shore is covered in shells.
  • Punta Caldera and Cabo Curioso: wide beaches with a lighthouse. According to SiFAP, Cabo Curioso has the largest breeding colony of red-legged cormorants in Argentina.
  • La Lobería: a cliff with a South American sea lion haul-out.
  • Playa Garganta del Diablo: walking access of moderate difficulty, according to National Parks.
  • Playa La Mina: where a coal mine once operated; today it's a favorite for fishing and water sports.

These beaches are part of the northern zone of the Makenke Interjurisdictional Marine Park. National Parks warns that they have no services or lifeguards, that swimming is not allowed, and that tides are very large: check them before heading down to the beach. With stops, allow half a day; sunset over the cliffs is the best time. Don't take fossils or rocks containing remains: paleontological heritage is protected by Law 25,743.

Península San Julián Provincial Reserve

The peninsula that closes the bay to the east is a provincial reserve created in 1986 (Law 1,821), covering 10,400 hectares, according to SiFAP. It is an Important Bird Area, and InterPatagonia mentions penguins, cormorants, rheas, foxes, armadillos and guanacos. National Parks gives the access at km 2,262 of Route 3, 12 km south of town, followed by 17 km of gravel. According to SiFAP, fishing is allowed from late spring to mid-autumn and the visiting season runs from October to early April. Road conditions and visiting conditions: check with the Tourism Office before you go.

Full-day trips

Laguna del Carbón and the Gran Bajo de San Julián

Inside the Gran Bajo de San Julián lies Laguna del Carbón, the lowest point in the Americas: about 105 m below sea level according to SEGEMAR (107 m according to the municipality). The easiest option is the natural viewpoint on Route 3, 48 km south of town, according to the municipal website. Going down to the bottom is another story: the track is unmarked, crosses private land and we found no up-to-date official information on whether access is open. There are no services and no signal. Check with the Tourism Office before setting out. Full guide: Laguna del Carbón and Gran Bajo de San Julián.

Estancia La María: rock paintings

About 140 to 150 km northwest of town, depending on the source, along provincial routes 25 and 77, both gravel, lies Estancia La María. The municipal website describes it as a 22,000-hectare archaeological locality with 87 caves and rock shelters with rock art, often with four or five colors in a single drawing, plus quarries, stone-tool workshops and a petrified forest. The local guide San Julián Patagonia speaks of paintings more than 12,600 years old, with human figures, animals, hands and geometric shapes in red, white, black and yellow, and mentions guided visits, lodging and a restaurant. Season, bookings and road conditions: check with the contacts on the municipal tourism website before setting out.

When to go

The climate is cold and dry, with strong wind all year and big temperature swings. Summers can be pleasant for the beach and winters are harsh, with sub-zero temperatures.

  • October to March: the best time. There are penguins, more boat trips and long days.
  • January and February: high season, with more trips on the bay.
  • Winter: quiet and without penguins. Check which excursions are running.

How many days to stay

  • 1 day: the Nao Victoria (from outside, if it's still closed), the waterfront, the museums and the Coastal Circuit.
  • 2 days: add the boat trip on the bay.
  • 3 or 4 days: add the Gran Bajo viewpoint, the Península San Julián Reserve and La María, if you confirm the visit and lodging.

These are our own suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Wind: bring a windbreaker and warm layers, even in summer.
  • Fuel: fill up in town before heading to the Gran Bajo or La María.
  • Spare day: boat trips depend on the wind and the sea. Don't leave the boat for your last day.
  • Signal: outside town there are stretches with no coverage. Download maps and the audio guide for offline use.
  • Puerto San Julián Tourism Office: Av. San Martín 1570, tel. +54 2962 454396 / +54 2962 414221, centur@sanjulian.gov.ar. According to the provincial website it is open Monday to Friday from 8 am to 8 pm; check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Puerto San Julián.

To continue your trip: Puerto Santa Cruz, Piedra Buena and Monte León, Bosques Petrificados de Jaramillo and Puerto Deseado.

Frequently asked questions

Is San Julián worth a stop if I'm driving Route 3?

Yes. In half a day you can see the waterfront, the Nao Victoria and the museums. With a full day you can add the Coastal Circuit or the boat trip on the bay.

When can you see penguins in San Julián?

From October to April. You see them on the boat trip around the bay, which visits the Magellanic penguin colony on Isla Cormorán, also known as Penguin Island.

Is the Nao Victoria Museum open?

As of November 3, 2026, the municipal tourism website says it is closed for maintenance until further notice. Check with the Tourism Office before you go; you can still see the replica from the waterfront.

Are there flights to Puerto San Julián?

As of November 3, 2026 we found no scheduled flights in operation. The usual option is to fly to Comodoro Rivadavia or Río Gallegos and continue along Route 3.

Can you visit Laguna del Carbón on your own?

The viewpoint on Route 3, 48 km south of town, yes. For going down to the bottom we found no up-to-date official information on access, which crosses private land: check with the Tourism Office before setting out.
