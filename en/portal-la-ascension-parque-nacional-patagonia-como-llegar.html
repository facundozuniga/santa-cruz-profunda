Home › Articles › Portal La Ascensión: how to get there and what to do in Patagonia National Park

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-26

On the shores of Lake Buenos Aires, between Los Antiguos and Perito Moreno, an old sheep ranch became the northern gateway to Patagonia National Park. This is La Ascensión: trails climbing from the lakeshore to the plateau, a camping area and the buildings of a ranch founded in 1912 that you can walk around.

Entry is free and you get there on a paved road. If you are traveling through northwestern Santa Cruz, it is worth stopping for at least half a day. This guide covers how to get there, which trails there are, where to camp, the best time to go and what to bring. For the rest of the park, see our Parque Patagonia page and the Cañadón Pinturas guide.

Where it is and how to get there

La Ascensión is on Provincial Route 43, on the south shore of Lake Buenos Aires. According to National Parks, it is 17 km from Los Antiguos and 38 km from Perito Moreno. Diario Río Negro notes that RP 43 is paved, so any car will do.

If you are coming from the Atlantic coast, the usual route is to drive to Perito Moreno and continue west on RP 43. Without a car, you can take a long-distance bus to Los Antiguos or Perito Moreno (see How to get to Santa Cruz); Río Negro mentions that you can also reach the gateway on organized tours. Ask about options at the tourist offices in each town.

A bit of history

According to National Parks, La Ascensión was a ranch founded in 1912 that raised sheep for wool and meat. Hundreds of rural workers lived there. Wikipedia says that in 2015 the Fundación Flora y Fauna Argentina bought the ranch, about 20,000 hectares, and donated part of it to National Parks. In 2019, under Decree 326/2019, that land became the La Ascensión Wild Nature Reserve, next to the national park and managed by National Parks.

Patagonia National Park was created in 2014 by Law 27,081 and today protects more than 106,000 hectares of steppe. It is the first national park to protect the high plateaus of Santa Cruz. Its flagship species is the hooded grebe (macá tobiano), a critically endangered bird found only in this province; the Lake Buenos Aires Plateau is its habitat.

What to do at La Ascensión

According to National Parks, the La Ascensión Wild Nature Reserve covers about 20,000 hectares, from the lakeshore up to the plateau. When you arrive you must check in at the Visitor Information Center: the rangers register you and give you the information for your visit.

Explore the old ranch buildings

The ranch keeps its buildings with their original façades: the shearing shed, the corrals, farm machinery and the matera (the workers' common room). According to Río Negro, the old school was turned into a visitor center and the shearing shed houses a cultural center. It is a short visit, ideal to start with.

Trek to the Lake Buenos Aires Plateau

This is the most demanding hike. It climbs to the highest part of the reserve, with 360° views over the plateau, the lake, the steppe and the Andes. Up there lives the hooded grebe, along with high-altitude plants, lichens and endemic reptiles.

Wikipedia splits the trail into stages from the ranch buildings: Mirador Roca Guacha (4.5 km), Puesto Amarillo (6 km), Puente Viejo (8.5 km), Mirador Cóndor (10.7 km), Puesto El Rincón (12 km) and the rim of the plateau (16 km). Prensa Santa Cruz describes it as the most difficult trail, 16 km long and about 9 h 30 min, with steep, rocky ups and downs in the last 4 km from El Rincón. National Parks sets aside the El Rincón area as a resting place for hikers on this trek, so you can split it over two days. Always tell the rangers before you set out.

Horseback riding

National Parks has authorized one horseback riding operator, El Buen Samaritano (phone 2975 404155), with rides through the reserve. According to the Perito Moreno tourism website, rides run year-round with advance booking. Ask the operator about routes and rates.

Wildlife watching

On the steppe it is common to see guanacos, rheas (choiques), foxes and condors, according to Crónica. Waterbirds appear on the lagoons and the lakeshore. Bring binoculars.

Where to camp

National Parks lists two camping sites at La Ascensión:

  • Caburé area: picnic and camping area with fire pits, grills, running water, toilets and parking for motorhomes.
  • El Rincón area: resting place for hikers on the plateau trek, next to an old, disused summer shepherd's hut. Only camping stoves are allowed and you need proper gear. No booking is required, but the park suggests getting in touch beforehand.

For a roof over your head, National Parks points to Los Antiguos, Perito Moreno, Lago Posadas and Bajo Caracoles. Los Antiguos and Perito Moreno are less than 40 km away. See the Los Antiguos directory and the Perito Moreno one.

Opening hours and admission

  • Entry: free, according to the National Parks fees page.
  • High season (October 1 to the last working day after Easter Week): entry from 8 am to 8 pm.
  • Low season (first working day after Easter Week to September 30): entry from 10 am to 5 pm.

Those are the National Parks opening hours. The provincial tourism website publishes a different schedule, with a mid season from 9 am to 6 pm (October 1 to December 14 and March 1 to Easter Week) and 8 am to 8 pm only from December 15 to the end of February. Given the difference, check before you travel at Parques Nacionales – Parque Nacional Patagonia: horarios y cómo llegar.

When to go

According to Río Negro, the best time to go is October to April. Days are long, and in January and February you have more daylight for the plateau. Expect wind in any season. The park is open year-round; in the low season entry hours are shorter. Before you set out, the province recommends checking the National Weather Service (SMN) forecast for alerts and trail closures.

How many days to stay

  • Half a day: the ranch buildings, the lakeshore and a short trail.
  • A full day: add Cerro Negro or Cerro de la Calle.
  • Two days: the plateau trek with a night at El Rincón.

These are our suggestions. If you are planning a trip through the northwest, combine it with Los Antiguos, Perito Moreno, the Cueva de las Manos, Cañadón Pinturas and Route 41.

Travel tips

  • Fuel and supplies: fill up in Los Antiguos or Perito Moreno. National Parks says there is a small shop near the Visitor Information Center where you can buy drinking water; according to the Perito Moreno tourism website, food service and the store only operate in high season.
  • Water: the park recommends not drinking from natural streams and bringing your own water.
  • Clothing: sunscreen, a hat and a windbreaker even if it is warm. For the plateau, hiking boots, warm layers, a camping stove and full camping gear.
  • Check-in: always register at the Visitor Information Center when you arrive and let them know before you head out on a long trail.
  • Fire: only in the designated fire pits of the Caburé area; at El Rincón, camping stoves only.
  • Pets: not allowed, according to the Perito Moreno tourism website.
  • Signal: National Parks warns that there is no cell signal in the protected area. There is wifi at the Visitor Information Center.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to enter Portal La Ascensión?

Nothing. According to National Parks, entry to Patagonia National Park is free.

Can I get there in a regular car?

Yes. La Ascensión is on paved RP 43, 17 km from Los Antiguos and 38 km from Perito Moreno.

Do I need to book to camp?

National Parks doesn't require a booking for the El Rincón area, on the way to the plateau, but suggests getting in touch beforehand. For the Caburé area, ask at the Visitor Information Center.

Is it the same as Cañadón Pinturas?

No. They are two different gateways. La Ascensión is a National Parks sector on Lake Buenos Aires; Cañadón Pinturas is run by Parque Patagonia and sits on RN 40, about 92 km away via Perito Moreno.

Can I bring pets?

No. According to the Perito Moreno tourism website, pets are not allowed in the reserve.

Where can I get information?

At the park office in the Los Antiguos bus terminal (Av. Tehuelches 157), by phone at 297 423-9441 or by email at pnpatagonia@apn.gob.ar.
