Home › Articles › Laguna Azul near Río Gallegos: how to reach the volcanic crater and what to know before you go

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-22

About 60 km from Río Gallegos, out on the steppe and almost on the Chilean border, a lake sits inside the crater of an extinct volcano. This is Laguna Azul, the closest nature outing to the capital of Santa Cruz, where you can walk along the crater rim and look down at the water.

It is not a park with facilities: there are no toilets, no drinking water and no permanent rangers. What you do get is an unusual volcanic landscape and birds nesting on the cliffs. Here is how to get there from Río Gallegos, what you will find, the reserve rules and what to bring.

Where Laguna Azul is

The Laguna Azul Provincial Geological Reserve is in the Güer Aike department. According to the Santa Cruz government, it lies 60 km from Río Gallegos and 6 km from the Argentina–Chile border. It covers 60 hectares and was created in 2005 by provincial law 2828.

The lake is part of the Pali Aike volcanic field, which straddles the border and is crossed by National Route 3 on the way to the Integración Austral border crossing and Tierra del Fuego.

The Santa Cruz government and SiFAP give 60 km; the provincial audio guide, 62 km. The 3 km internal road figure comes from the Provincial Directorate of Protected Areas.

How to get to Laguna Azul

By car

Leave Río Gallegos heading south on National Route 3 towards the Integración Austral border crossing. Before the border, the access road to the reserve branches off. To make sure you do not miss the turnoff, use the official audio guide, which marks each stretch by RN 3 kilometre.

At the entrance there is a vehicle gate, and from there it is about 3 km to the crater. In winter the gate may be closed because of ice, but the reserve stays open: if you open it to drive through, close it behind you, as Protected Areas has asked.

With the audio guide

The provincial Tourism Department offers a free driving audio guide, “Río Gallegos a Laguna Azul”, part of the Santa Cruz Sonora project. It starts at the tourism information office (Almacén de Informes), covers about 65 km in around two hours and includes optional tracks for detours to Punta Loyola and Cabo Vírgenes, plus information on steppe wildlife and music by local artists. Download it for free from Turismo Santa Cruz – Audioguía Río Gallegos a Laguna Azul.

Without a car

We could not confirm any regular transport service to the reserve. Ask the Río Gallegos tourism office about agencies or private cars that make the trip.

What Laguna Azul is: a bit of geology

The lake is a groundwater-fed body of water filling the main crater of an inactive volcano. According to the Santa Cruz government, the crater rim stands almost 200 m above sea level and the water lies below 140 m. SiFAP gives a diameter of about 560 m and attributes the intense colour to the absence of suspended material.

The Pali Aike volcanic field formed in three cycles. The oldest, about 3.8 million years ago, left a basalt lava plateau of about 4,500 km². The latest, between 4,000 and 3,000 years ago, created the Laguna Azul crater. Sediment cores from the lake bed give an age of around 3,400 years.

The whole field has about 467 volcanic vents and around 100 maars, explosion craters that form when magma meets underground water or ice. The name comes from the Tehuelche language: pale means hunger and aike, place.

Depth is the most disputed figure: SiFAP gives 295 m, but other sources give very different numbers. East of the main crater there is a second, smaller dry crater.

Things to do at Laguna Azul

Walk the crater rim

The classic outing is to circle the lake from above. From the rim you see the water below, the basalt cliffs and the steppe in every direction. The colour changes with the light.

Birds and wildlife

According to SiFAP, birds nest on the crater cliffs, mainly black-faced ibises (bandurrias): the white streaks on the rock are their guano. Around the volcano you will often see guanacos, lesser rheas and European hares. Lichens give the rocks yellowish-green, white and orange tones.

Photography

The contrast between the water and the dark basalt makes it one of the most photographed spots in the south of the province.

Reserve rules

The Provincial Agrarian Council published these rules for visitors:

  • Not allowed: pets.
  • Not allowed: camping.
  • Not allowed: fires.
  • Not allowed: going into the water.
  • Not allowed: taking volcanic rocks, fruits or anything else.
  • Take all your rubbish back with you.

In 2021 the province set visiting hours of Saturdays and Sundays from 10 am to 7 pm as part of that year’s health protocol. We could not find current published hours: in July 2025 Protected Areas said the reserve was open and anyone could go. Check with the Río Gallegos tourism office before you travel.

Services: what there is and what there isn’t

There are no services at the reserve. In August 2022 work began on a visitor interpretation centre, but it stalled and the matter is now in the courts.

In July 2025 Protected Areas reported that 30–32% of the work was still unfinished and that the building had been vandalised several times. In April 2026 the Río Gallegos municipality was still asking the province for information and requesting that the project be handed over so it could finish it.

In practice: bring water, food and everything you need, and do not count on mobile signal.

When to go

You can visit all year round, but conditions change a lot:

  • Spring and summer (October to March): long days. The best time to combine it with Cabo Vírgenes.
  • Winter (June to August): the internal road, the steps and the volcanic rock freeze. The rock has small holes where water condenses and freezes, making it very slippery. In July 2025 Protected Areas advised against going without proper footwear.

The wind blows all year: pick a day with a moderate forecast. More in Weather in Santa Cruz: when to go, wind and temperatures.

How much time to spend

It is a half-day trip from Río Gallegos: the audio guide estimates about two hours just for the drive. If you want to walk the rim at a relaxed pace, bring a packed lunch and take the afternoon.

Travel tips

  • Leave Río Gallegos with a full tank: there is no fuel at the reserve.
  • Bring a windproof jacket, a hat and sunglasses all year round.
  • Wear shoes with a good grip: loose rock and winter ice are the main hazards.
  • Stay away from the cliff edges in strong wind.
  • If you are continuing to Chile, bring the documents needed for the Integración Austral crossing. Opening hours in Border crossings from Santa Cruz to Chile.
  • Watch out for guanacos crossing the road.

Combine it with

  • Cabo Vírgenes: lighthouse, penguin colony and km 0 of Route 40, at the southern tip of the mainland. It is a full-day trip: Cabo Vírgenes: how to get to the penguin colony, the lighthouse and km 0.
  • Pali Aike National Park (Chile): since 1970 it has protected the Chilean side of the same volcanic field and has hiking trails. For access and opening hours, check with Chile’s National Forest Corporation (CONAF).

Frequently asked questions

How far is Laguna Azul from Río Gallegos?

About 60 km according to the Santa Cruz government and SiFAP; the provincial audio guide says 62 km.

Can you swim in Laguna Azul?

No. Going into the water is forbidden to protect the reserve.

Can I bring my dog?

No. Pets are not allowed.

Can you camp at Laguna Azul?

No. Fires are not allowed either.

Is there an entrance fee?

None of the official sources we checked mentions a fee. Confirm with the Río Gallegos tourism office before you go.
