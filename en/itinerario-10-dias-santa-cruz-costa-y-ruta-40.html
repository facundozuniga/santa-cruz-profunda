Home › Articles › 10-day Santa Cruz itinerary: a loop along the coast and Route 40 without going through El Calafate

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-27

Most Santa Cruz itineraries start and end in El Calafate. This one doesn't. It is a loop through the north and center of the province: it starts in Caleta Olivia, runs down the Atlantic coast to Piedra Buena, crosses the plateau to Route 40 and returns to the coast on provincial routes 43 and 12.

In 10 days you combine the estuary of Puerto Deseado, the bay of San Julián, Monte León National Park, the steppe around Gobernador Gregores, the Cave of the Hands and Los Antiguos. It adds up to about 2,070 km of driving, almost all paved, with long stretches without gas stations.

Each stop has its own guide on Santa Cruz Profunda. Here we focus on how to string them together: the day-by-day route, the distances and what you need to know before setting out.

Where the loop starts and how to get there

The starting point is Caleta Olivia, on National Route 3. The nearest airport is Comodoro Rivadavia, in Chubut, 68 km away on RN 3 according to Interpatagonia. National Parks says that airport has daily flights from Buenos Aires and that car rental is available in Comodoro. If you rent, ask the company about its conditions for driving on gravel. More options in How to get to Santa Cruz: flights, buses and roads.

The loop is designed to be driven in your own or a rented car, at your own pace and with time to spare for detours.

The itinerary, day by day

Day 1: Caleta Olivia

You arrive, pick up the car, shop for the trip and walk along the waterfront. What to see in town: Things to do in Caleta Olivia.

Days 2 and 3: Puerto Deseado

It is 227 km: 101 km on RN 3 to the junction with RN 281 and 126 km on RN 281. Ruta0 shows no need to refuel along the way. Road details in How to get to Puerto Deseado from Comodoro Rivadavia.

Two days are enough for the estuary and, in season, the boat trip to Penguin Island. According to National Parks, the island can only be visited on authorized boat tours, which run from mid-October to April, always weather permitting; the trip takes about 6 hours. More in Things to do in Puerto Deseado and Penguin Island: the tour from Puerto Deseado.

Days 4 and 5: Puerto San Julián

This is the longest leg: 382 km. You head back along RN 281 to RN 3 and drive south. Ruta0 lists no stations on the way, but notes that there may be stations it hasn't recorded: at Tres Cerros, on RN 3 (km 2114), there is a YPF station run by ENER+, which places it 143 km from San Julián. Even so, leave Deseado with a full tank.

On November 19, 2026, the National Roads Department (Vialidad Nacional) report published by Ruta0 listed the El Salado–Tres Cerros stretch as partially closed and passable with caution, and San Julián–El Salado with a deformed road surface and loose animals. Check it before you leave.

Day 5 is for the bay, the coast and the town center, or for the Gran Bajo de San Julián and Laguna del Carbón. Before heading to the Gran Bajo, read what is known about access in Laguna del Carbón and the Gran Bajo de San Julián. Town guide: Things to do in Puerto San Julián.

Day 6: Piedra Buena and Monte León National Park

It is 121 km on RN 3, all paved. According to National Parks, the Monte León entrance is 35 km south of Piedra Buena on RN 3. Visit the park and drive back to sleep in Piedra Buena, on the banks of the Santa Cruz River. Opening hours for the park and the penguin colony: check before you travel at Parques Nacionales – Monte León: horarios y cómo llegar.

On November 19, 2026, Vialidad Nacional reported pothole repairs on the Piedra Buena bridge, with traffic in a single lane, alternating, from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. More in Things to do in Piedra Buena and Monte León National Park.

Day 7: across to Gobernador Gregores

It is 234 km: 120 km on RP 288 to Estancia La Julia and 114 km on RP 27. Ruta0 lists them as paved, but in its traveler reports RP 288 got 1 out of 5 stars for road condition on November 6, 2026. Check the conditions before you leave in the provincial roads report (Vialidad Provincial).

Ruta0 lists no stations along the way: fill up in Piedra Buena. In the afternoon you can follow the Route of the 1921 Strike circuit or go to Lago Cardiel, 85 km away according to Interpatagonia. Details in Things to do in Gobernador Gregores and Tres Lagos and Lago Cardiel.

Day 8: Route 40 to Perito Moreno

The most demanding day: 351 km. You drive 70 km on RP 25 to Riera and then north on RN 40, through Las Horquetas and Bajo Caracoles.

The only fuel stop on the way is Bajo Caracoles, 228 km from Gregores according to Ruta0: Turismo Ruta 40 lists it among the Route 40 stations and warns that stations in the area sometimes run out of fuel. Leave Gregores with a full tank and fill up again there.

On November 19, 2026, Vialidad Nacional listed the Bajo Caracoles–Río Ecker stretch as open, with potholes, loose animals, unstable shoulders and shoulder-grading work. More about the road in Route 40 in Santa Cruz: sections, fuel and gravel.

Day 9: Cave of the Hands and Los Antiguos

Set off early with a full tank. The Perito Moreno tourism website lists three accesses from RN 40:

  • Via RP 97, from Bajo Caracoles: 130 km south of Perito Moreno, then 47 km of gravel in fair condition, suitable for low cars. This is the option for a regular car.
  • Via RP 101: 80 km south, then 28 km of gravel in poor condition, for 4x4s.
  • On foot from the Cañadón Pinturas Portal: 54 km from Perito Moreno, via the Bajada de los Toldos, a high-difficulty trail.

Visits are guided only. According to the official site, in high season (October to April) it is open every day from 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. For tickets, check before you travel at Cueva de las Manos (sitio oficial). Full details in How to get to the Cave of the Hands.

On the way back, continue to Los Antiguos: 58 km on RP 43. What to do there: Things to do in Los Antiguos.

Day 10: back to Caleta Olivia

It is 355 km. You return on RP 43: from Perito Moreno to Pico Truncado it is 241 km, with 160 km without stations up to Colonia Las Heras, according to Ruta0. From Pico Truncado to Caleta Olivia it is 54 km on RP 12, according to Interpatagonia. If you want to split the leg, see Las Heras and Pico Truncado.

When to do this loop

The ideal time is between mid-October and April: that is when the Penguin Island tours run, according to National Parks, and high season at the Cave of the Hands, with longer visiting hours.

Summer is also cherry season in Los Antiguos: according to the Designation of Origin protocol cited by Bichos de Campo, the harvest runs from late December to mid-February. The 36th National Cherry Festival will be on January 8 and 9, 2027, with rodeo from the 8th to the 10th, as announced by the town in November 2026. More in National Cherry Festival in Los Antiguos.

Winter is riskier: in August 2026, Vialidad Nacional reported frost, fog, potholes and wind on RN 40 between Las Horquetas and Bajo Caracoles. Drive only in daylight. More in Weather in Santa Cruz: when to travel.

Are 10 days enough?

Yes. Most days involve 2 to 5 hours of driving; the longest is day 9 if you reach the Cave via RP 97. To adjust it:

  • 7 days: drop the second day in Deseado and the free day in San Julián.
  • 14 days: add Lago Posadas and Route 41, or a night at a tourist estancia near Gregores.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: fill up in every town even if you still have half a tank. The longest stretches without stations are Piedra Buena–Gobernador Gregores (234 km) and Gobernador Gregores–Bajo Caracoles (228 km).
  • Jerry can: if your car has a short range, carry one; on Route 40, stations sometimes run out of fuel.
  • Wildlife: guanacos and rheas cross without warning. Vialidad reports flag loose animals on many stretches of the loop.
  • Road conditions: check the national and provincial roads reports (Vialidad Nacional and Vialidad Provincial) before each leg.
  • Gravel: on the loop's roads, only on the access to the Cave of the Hands. Take it slow. In Monte León, according to National Parks, the internal road can become impassable on rainy days.
  • Signal: download offline maps. According to La Nación, there is no phone signal in Bajo Caracoles and the connection is the general store's Wi-Fi.

Frequently asked questions

Can you tour Santa Cruz without going through El Calafate?

Yes. This loop does not touch El Calafate or El Chaltén. If you want to add them, head south from Gobernador Gregores on RN 40: El Chaltén is 305 km away, with 57 km of gravel according to Ruta0.

Do I need a 4x4?

Not for the roads on the loop, which Ruta0 lists as paved. The gravel is on the access to the Cave of the Hands: the Perito Moreno tourism website rates the RP 97 access as fair and suitable for low cars, and the RP 101 access as in poor condition and for 4x4s. If it has rained, check the provincial roads department (Vialidad Provincial) report.

How many kilometers is it in total?

About 2,070 km, including the round trip to the Cave of the Hands from Perito Moreno via RP 97, not counting the detour to Monte León.

Where do I get fuel between Gobernador Gregores and Perito Moreno?

In Bajo Caracoles, 228 km from Gregores according to Ruta0. It is the only station on that stretch and it sometimes runs out of fuel: leave with a full tank.
