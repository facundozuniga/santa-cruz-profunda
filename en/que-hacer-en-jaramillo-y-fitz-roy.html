Home › Articles › Things to do in Jaramillo and Fitz Roy: the Facón Grande trail, the railway and the steppe

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-18

Jaramillo and Fitz Roy are two small villages on the plateau of northern Santa Cruz, 19 km apart. According to the 2022 census, they have 807 residents between them. Most travellers drive straight past on Route 3 heading south.

It is worth stopping. The old Patagonian Railway ran through here, and this is where, in December 1921, the story of José Font, “Facón Grande”, came to an end; he was one of the leaders of the rural strikes known as the Patagonia Rebelde. Today both villages have museums in their old stations, houses of sheet metal, wood and stone, and they are the gateway to the Petrified Forests.

Where they are and how to get there

Both villages are in the Deseado department and share a local council (Comisión de Fomento). Fitz Roy is on National Route 3. Jaramillo is on National Route 281, which continues to Puerto Deseado, 7 km from Route 3 and 189 m above sea level.

Distances come from the province and InterPatagonia. According to Welcome Argentina, Routes 3 and 281 are paved. Road conditions change: before you set off, check the road report in the guide Route 3 in Santa Cruz: sections, distances and stops.

Your own car is the most practical option. The nearest airport, according to the province, is Comodoro Rivadavia (Chubut). We could not find bus timetables to these villages that we could confirm: ask at the local Tourism Office or at the bus terminals in Caleta Olivia and Puerto Deseado.

A bit of history

The railway that built the villages

Law 5559 of 1908 promoted a network of state railways for Patagonia, and one of the lines was meant to link Puerto Deseado with Lake Nahuel Huapi. Work began in 1909, but the tracks only reached Las Heras: about 283 km. Jaramillo and Fitz Roy were born as stations on the line, which opened in 1914. The service was closed on 15 January 1978, during the last military dictatorship, and after the closure much of the population left.

Facón Grande and the end of the strike

Between November and December 1921, the Army, under Lieutenant Colonel Héctor Varela, crushed the rural strikes in Santa Cruz with executions. By mid-December only José Font’s group was still holding out, and it clashed with the troops near Tehuelches station.

Afterwards, Font and other strikers were taken to Jaramillo station. He was held next to the station shed and shot on 22 December at the spot known as Cañadón de los Muertos, near the village. The exact location is still being researched: sources give different distances, and an archaeological study by CONICET is trying to pin it down.

According to the museum, several court cases over these events were opened in 2023.

Things to do in Jaramillo

Facón Grande Site Museum

It is housed in the old railway station and opened in 2021. It is devoted entirely to the rural strikes of 1920 and 1921, and offers guided visits at different times of day. Next to the platform stands the P-111 dining car from the old line.

Don’t miss the wooden cross found on an estancia near Gobernador Gregores, which passed through the hands of Osvaldo Bayer, who returned it to Jaramillo in 2009 as the museum’s first piece. Outside there is an execution scene made with sculptures. You can also tour the museum virtually with the Spatial app.

It is on José Font street (no number). Opening days and hours: ask at the Jaramillo and Fitz Roy Tourism Office, which according to the province is open at José Font s/n, Monday to Friday, 9 am to 6 pm; check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Jaramillo y Fitz Roy.

Casa Matera “Don Francisco”

It is opposite the museum, one street away. It was a general store and also the place where Font and other farmhands were held; the local council restored it in 2019. It keeps furniture and objects from the early 20th century to the 1970s.

It has a communal table where you can sit and ask about the route, drink mate and try tortas fritas or buñuelos (fried dough). Check with the Tourism Office that it is open on the day you go.

Facón Grande Monument

It was unveiled in 1998 at the junction of Route 3 and the former Provincial Route 14, now National Route 281, at the initiative of rural workers and the local council. It is the natural first stop if you come along Route 3. Scenes of the film La Patagonia rebelde, based on Bayer’s work, were shot in this area.

Village architecture

The station has been a Municipal Historic Monument since 1998. The village has colourful sheet-metal and wooden houses and stone houses built by stonemasons in the early 20th century.

The old Hotel La Paz, now Hotel Don Feliciano, was handed over by the family to the local council. In October 2026 it was still being developed to work as lodging with meals, so don’t count on it for a bed yet.

Things to do in Fitz Roy

Fitz Roy Rural Station Interpretation Centre

It opened in 2022 in the old station (Belgrano s/n, corner of General Lavalle). It tells the rural history of the area from 1902 to the 1970s: livestock drives, earmarking, shearing and the breeding of stud sheep on the wool farms. It also explains the life that revolved around the railway. The village also has a recreation of a farmhand’s house, with objects from estancia life.

Location of Historias mínimas

Part of the film Historias mínimas (2002), by Carlos Sorín, was shot in Fitz Roy: two of its characters live in the village. According to Patagonia-Argentina.com, Fitz Roy still has the spot where it was filmed; ask at the Tourism Office how to get there.

The Ice Age site

In 2007, remains of a glyptodont and a ground sloth from the last Ice Age were found in the area. The province mentions the “La Era del Hielo” (Ice Age) theme museum, and according to Patagonia-Argentina.com you can see the excavation site and a replica of the fossils. To find out where it is and how to visit, ask at the Tourism Office.

Nearby: the Petrified Forests and the audio guide

From Fitz Roy, head south on Route 3 to km 2,074, where Provincial Route 49 branches off: from there it is about 50 km of gravel to the visitor area of Bosques Petrificados de Jaramillo National Park, with araucaria trunks more than 30 m long. All the details are in the guide Bosques Petrificados de Jaramillo: how to get there and what to see.

The province offers a free driving audio guide from the Santa Cruz Sonora project, “Fitz Roy - Jaramillo al Parque Nacional Bosques Petrificados”. It starts at the Fitz Roy Tourism Office and covers about 165 km to the park gate, with an introduction and 12 tracks from A to M: you play each one when you see the sign with its letter along the road. It includes stories of the Patagonia Rebelde, fossils and music by Santa Cruz artists. Download it from Turismo Santa Cruz – Audioguía Fitz Roy - Jaramillo al Parque Nacional Bosques Petrificados. For the gravel, the province recommends high-clearance vehicles.

The train that may come back

In 2026 the province began restoring the line. The first stage, worth about ARS 497 million according to Canal 26, included surveying the route and restoring the P-111 dining car. In October 2026 the province declared that stage complete: 21 km of track restored between Jaramillo and Fitz Roy. Work is still pending, such as the crossing over Route 3, and there is no passenger service or opening date yet.

When to go

The climate is cold and dry. According to InterPatagonia, the mean annual temperature is 6 °C, with recorded extremes of 28 °C and −25 °C. For the Petrified Forests, the province suggests going from November to April to avoid the cold and snow. Our suggestion: November to March, with long days to combine the villages with the park. More in Climate in Santa Cruz: when to travel, wind and temperatures.

How many days to stay

  • Half a day: Jaramillo (monument, museum and Casa Matera) and a quick stop in Fitz Roy.
  • A full day: both villages at a relaxed pace, plus the audio guide.
  • Two days: add the Petrified Forests.

These are our own suggestions.

Where to sleep and eat

According to Jaramillo’s tourism promotion adviser (TiempoSur, October 2026), Jaramillo has a municipal campground and some shops. Fitz Roy has hotels and cabins, two grill restaurants (parrillas) and a service station with fast food. For more options, the nearest larger towns are Caleta Olivia and Puerto Deseado.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: Fitz Roy has a service station, and the province recommends filling up there before going to the Petrified Forests. There is none in the park.
  • For the Petrified Forests, bring water and food: according to National Parks, the park has no water or visitor services, only toilets.
  • Message the Tourism Office beforehand to confirm the museums are open.
  • Carry some cash: these are very small villages.
  • Watch out for guanacos and loose livestock on the road.

Frequently asked questions

How far apart are Jaramillo and Fitz Roy?

About 19 km, according to the province. Fitz Roy is on Route 3, and Jaramillo is 7 km off Route 3 along National Route 281.

Can you travel by train between Jaramillo and Fitz Roy?

Not yet. In October 2026 the province finished restoring 21 km of track between the two villages, but there is no passenger service and no opening date.

Where is the Facón Grande Museum?

In the old Jaramillo railway station, on José Font street (no number). Check opening days and hours with the local Tourism Office before you go.

Is it worth combining with the Petrified Forests?

Yes. From Fitz Roy it is about 140 km to the park, the last 50 on gravel along RP 49. Fill up in Fitz Roy.
