Home › Articles › Cabo Vírgenes: how to get to the penguin colony, the lighthouse and Route 40 km 0

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-10

Cabo Vírgenes is the southeastern tip of Santa Cruz, on the eastern mouth of the Strait of Magellan. Three landmarks sit together within a few kilometres: one of the largest Magellanic penguin colonies on the continent, the lighthouse in service since 1904 and kilometre 0 of National Route 40.

There is no town, no petrol station and no reliable cell signal. It is a full-day trip from Río Gallegos. The short destination sheet is at Cabo Vírgenes; here is the detail on the road, the season and what to bring.

Where it is and how to get there

According to SiFAP, from Río Gallegos you drive 16 km south on National Route 3 and then Provincial Route 1, with 123 km of dirt road: 139 km in total. The province has an official audio guide of 134 km and about 3 hours, and recommends 40 km/h on gravel.

Allow about 3 hours one way on the slow gravel (provincial audio-guide figure). Welcome Argentina is clear: there are no services or fuel on the way. Leave Río Gallegos with a full tank, water, food and warm clothes.

SiFAP says you can visit year-round, though in winter only in a 4x4. Before you leave, check the state of RP 1 on the Provincial Roads daily bulletin (AGVP).

If you prefer not to drive, agencies in Río Gallegos run the day trip. We do not publish lists or prices: ask about departures and season at the Río Gallegos Tourism Office. More about the city in What to do in Río Gallegos.

A bit of history

According to the Ministry of Defence (lighthouse fact sheet), Ferdinand Magellan’s expedition discovered the cape on 21 October 1520 when entering the strait. In 1584 Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa founded Ciudad Nombre de Jesús nearby, which the province describes as the first fortification of the Spanish Empire in South America; the settlement did not last. SiFAP mentions the ruins and a cemetery in the area.

Centuries later there was a gold rush in the late 19th century: Welcome Argentina and the provincial audio guide include it among the stories of the route. Today you also see oil and gas facilities along the way.

What to do at Cabo Vírgenes

Cabo Vírgenes Provincial Reserve penguin colony

The reserve was created in 1986 (Provincial Decree 1.806/86) and covers 1,230 ha, according to SiFAP. It protects Magellanic penguin habitat and oreja de cordero, an endemic plant. SiFAP speaks of about 150,000 pairs and presents it as the second-largest penguin colony in South America; C5N speaks of more than 260,000 birds.

Penguins are viewed from a 1,500 m self-guided interpretation trail (SiFAP). You also see skuas and giant petrels, and on the road it is common to cross guanacos, foxes and rheas. Welcome Argentina mentions guided visits to the nesting areas.

We found no up-to-date official information on an entrance fee or a fixed visiting schedule: check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Cabo Vírgenes Provincial Reserve audio guide or ask the Río Gallegos Tourism Office. Contact published by SiFAP: faunacap@gmail.com and 2966-44503.

Cabo Vírgenes Lighthouse

The lighthouse entered service on 15 April 1904, according to the Ministry of Defence. Welcome Argentina describes it as a truncated-pyramid tower with white and black horizontal bands, about 27 m tall, 70 m above sea level. C5N gives 26.5 m. It is a National Historic Monument (Law 26.650/2010) and part of the Centennial Lighthouses System.

Welcome Argentina mentions an interpretation centre about how it works. We found no official information on whether you can climb the tower or on visiting hours for the lighthouse or a museum: check with Río Gallegos Tourism. The lighthouse can be seen year-round in daylight, according to Welcome Argentina.

“Al fin y al Cabo” tea house

The provincial audio guide and Welcome Argentina list it as a stop on the route. C5N presents it as the southernmost in the country. We found no official opening days or hours: check before you go; do not count on finding it open outside the penguin season.

Route 40 km 0

Since 24 November 2004, by resolution of the National Roads Directorate, kilometre 0 of National Route 40 is at Cabo Vírgenes (it used to be at Punta Loyola). The route links this cape with La Quiaca, in Jujuy: argentina.gob.ar gives it 5,194 km. The marker stands next to the lighthouse. Guide to the Santa Cruz stretch: Route 40 in Santa Cruz.

Estancia Monte Dinero

It sits on RP 1 at km 100, about 120 km from Río Gallegos according to Welcome Argentina. It is a sheep ranch of the Fenton family, now in its fifth generation, and offers farm tourism: depending on the season, shearing, lamb marking, wool sorting and droving, plus sheepdog demonstrations. It has Hostería La Casa Grande and runs truck trips out to the lighthouse. Phone published by Welcome Argentina: +54 2966-428922. Season, rates and bookings: check directly with the estancia.

Punta Dúngeness

Welcome Argentina describes, a short distance from the Argentine lighthouse, the Chilean Punta Dúngenes lighthouse: red and white, active since the late 19th century, with an automatic weather station, on an unstable spit of land. We found no official information on visitor access from the Argentine side: do not build your itinerary around that point without confirming first.

When to go

  • September to March: Magellanic penguin season according to SiFAP. November to February is the safest window to see an active colony and find the tea house and tours open.
  • Chick hatching: a study published in El Hornero recorded hatchings at Cabo Vírgenes between 13 November and early December. Dates vary by year.
  • Year-round: the lighthouse and landscape can be seen in daylight. In winter SiFAP asks for a 4x4; days are short and the gravel can have ice or mud.

The wind is strong almost always and the weather changes fast, even in summer.

How many days to stay

  • 1 day: enough to go and return from Río Gallegos: about 6 hours of driving plus 3 or 4 on site.
  • With a night at Monte Dinero: if you want to take it easy, sleep at the estancia and tour the penguin colony and lighthouse at a slower pace.

These are our suggestions. Visit Argentina recommends 2 to 4 days in Río Gallegos including this outing.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: fill the tank in Río Gallegos. There is no pump on the way.
  • Gravel: drive slowly, with lights on; watch for guanacos and sheep. Check the spare tyre and bring tools.
  • Road conditions: check before you travel at AGVP Santa Cruz – Provincial roads daily bulletin.
  • Supplies: water, food and warm clothes even in summer. The Patagonian wind hits hard.
  • Phone: do not count on a signal. Tell someone your return time.
  • At the penguin colony: do not approach nests, do not leave the trail and do not pick up penguins.
  • Audio guide: the province publishes a soundtrack for the drive: check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Cabo Vírgenes Provincial Reserve audio guide.

To combine with other coastal stops: Wildlife watching on the Santa Cruz coast, Laguna Azul and The coast along Route 3.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Río Gallegos to Cabo Vírgenes take?

The provincial audio guide reckons about 3 hours for a 134 km route at 40 km/h on gravel. SiFAP gives 139 km in total. Real time depends on road conditions.

Is there an entrance fee to the penguin colony?

We found no published official fee. Billiken says entry is free; confirm with Río Gallegos Tourism or the SiFAP contact (faunacap@gmail.com).

Can you go in a regular car?

Yes in season, on gravel. SiFAP notes that in winter only in a 4x4. There are no services on the way: leave with a full tank and check the AGVP bulletin.

When are the penguins there?

According to SiFAP, from September to March. November to February is the safest window.

Where is Route 40 km 0?

Next to the Cabo Vírgenes lighthouse, since 24 November 2004, by resolution of the National Roads Directorate.
