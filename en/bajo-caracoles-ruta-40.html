Home › Articles › Bajo Caracoles: what to do and what to know about the key stop on Ruta 40

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-30

Bajo Caracoles is a tiny village in the middle of the steppe in northwestern Santa Cruz, on National Route 40. The 2022 census counted 40 residents, according to Wikipedia; La Nación counted 34 that same year. El Cronista describes it as five or six irregular blocks. Almost everything revolves around one stone building: the old general store that now works as hotel, shop, restaurant and fuel station. The short profile is at Bajo Caracoles.

If you are driving Ruta 40, this is not an optional stop: it is the only fuel pump between Perito Moreno and Gobernador Gregores. It is also the gateway to the Cave of the Hands, Parque Patagonia and the road to Lago Posadas.

Where Bajo Caracoles is

The village is in the Río Chico department, about 614 m above sea level according to Turismo Ruta 40 (Wikipedia gives 613 m). It sits on RN 40, on the stretch between Tres Lagos and Perito Moreno, a few kilometers south of the junction with RP 97, the road to the Cave of the Hands. Interpatagonia places the hotel on the banks of the Fénix Grande river.

Getting there

The practical way to get here is by road: in your own vehicle, by motorcycle, by bike or on a tour. According to Turismo Ruta 40, RN 40 is paved both northbound (Perito Moreno) and southbound (Gobernador Gregores). RP 39, toward Lago Posadas, and RP 97, toward the Cave of the Hands, are gravel.

Sources don’t agree to the kilometer. The figures for Los Antiguos, Gobernador Gregores, Tres Lagos and El Calafate come from Turismo Ruta 40, which gives 128 to 130 km to Perito Moreno and 73 km to Lago Posadas. The Provincial Roads Department report adds up to 120 km of pavement to Perito Moreno and 68 km of gravel to Lago Posadas; the province says 75 km. Toward Gobernador Gregores, Wikipedia also gives 226 km, although some press reports say 300 km or more. The gateway figure is from Parque Patagonia and the Cave figure from the Roads Department. Check the route with your own GPS before setting out. For road conditions, check before you travel at Vialidad Provincial de Santa Cruz (AGVP) – Parte diario de rutas, distrito Perito Moreno.

A bit of history

People have lived in this area for more than 10,000 years. According to Turismo Ruta 40, hunter-gatherers and later the Tehuelche moved across these plateaus following guanacos and rheas along trails called rastrilladas, and Ruta 40 follows one of those old tracks.

The current hamlet was born in the early 1920s as a country store: Francisco José Folch and the merchants Caseros and Alessanco set it up to supply travelers and ranches. The stone building that is now the hotel was commissioned by Folch and built by the Yugoslav stonemason Mateo Barak. The date 1943 is carved into the facade, according to Turismo Ruta 40 and El Cronista; La Nación says 1940.

The name comes from a “bajo” (low-lying basin) with fossils of Crioceras, an ammonite or marine “snail” (caracol) that went extinct more than 100 million years ago.

What there is in the village

Fuel

It is the most important service. Turismo Ruta 40 lists it among the towns with a gas station on Ruta 40 in Santa Cruz and says to fill the tank “no matter what”. According to La Nación (2022), it is a private station run from the general store; fuel is brought in from Perito Moreno, which makes it more expensive, and the tanks held 9,000 liters of gasoline and 4,500 of diesel. Turismo Ruta 40 warns that stations in the area sometimes run out of fuel. We found no up-to-date official information on the pump’s hours or accepted payment methods: carry cash and always arrive with a reserve.

Hotel, food and camping

The Hotel Bajo Caracoles is in the historic building. According to Interpatagonia it has simple rooms with private bathroom and hot water, breakfast, a restaurant, parking and wifi. In 2022 La Nación counted eight rooms, hot showers and a camping area; Wikipedia notes camping in season. Rates, availability and the camping season change: check directly with the hotel before you travel.

Wifi and signal

According to La Nación (2022), there is no phone signal in the village and the general store’s wifi was the only connection for hundreds of kilometers. Parque Patagonia also warns that there is no phone or data coverage on the roads in the area. Tell someone your itinerary before leaving Perito Moreno or Gobernador Gregores.

Other services

The village has Rural School No. 48, according to Turismo Ruta 40 and Wikipedia, a police post and a roads maintenance post; El Cronista adds a justice of the peace. In 2022 La Nación reported that you can get your Ruta 40 Passport stamped at the store.

What to do near Bajo Caracoles

Cueva de las Manos

It is the main reason to stop here. The Cave of the Hands lies in the Río Pinturas canyon and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999. It has rock paintings thousands of years old: hands stenciled in negative and figures of guanacos and rheas, among others. From Bajo Caracoles there are two ways to get there:

  • On RP 97: from the junction with RN 40 it is 47 km of gravel to the site, according to the Provincial Roads Department report. The Perito Moreno municipal website rates it as fair and suitable for low-clearance cars, but the road changes with the weather: check the report before you go.
  • On foot from the Cañadón Pinturas Gateway of Parque Patagonia, 75 km north on RN 40, along the Bajada de los Toldos trail, rated difficult by the municipal website.

According to the official website, it opens every day: October to April from 9 am to 7 pm and May to September from 10 am to 5 pm. Visits are only with a site guide; according to the municipal website, groups of up to 20 people leave every hour. Meteored says the visit follows a boardwalk of about 1.5 km. There is an entrance fee: for rates, check before you travel at Cueva de las Manos (sitio oficial). More detail in how to get to the Cave of the Hands.

Parque Patagonia: Cañadón Pinturas Gateway

The gateway is on RN 40, 75 km north of Bajo Caracoles and 54 km south of Perito Moreno, according to Parque Patagonia. You must register at the Information Center, there is an entrance fee and pets are not allowed. If you are staying overnight in the park, you are asked to arrive before 8 pm.

Some trails published by the park (round trip):

  • Tierra de Colores: 2.6 km, 1 h, moderate.
  • Koi: 4.8 km, 1 h 45 min, moderate.
  • La Vertiente: 2.7 km, 40 min, easy.
  • There are also La Guanaca and Bajada de los Toldos: ask for the map and the day’s recommendations at the Information Center.

To stay overnight there is the La Posta de los Toldos lodge, with nine rooms with private bathroom and a restaurant, plus several campsites. Rates and bookings: check before you travel at Parque Patagonia – Recomendaciones y preguntas frecuentes. Full guide at Parque Patagonia: Cañadón Pinturas.

Lago Posadas and Lake Pueyrredón

Gravel RP 39 takes you to the town of Lago Posadas and to lakes Posadas and Pueyrredón. That is where you’ll find the natural stone arch in the lake and, farther on, Mount San Lorenzo. In midwinter the Roads Department report has restricted the Bajo Caracoles–Lago Posadas stretch to 4x4 vehicles: check it before you go. Full guide at Lago Posadas and Route 41.

Roballos Pass

From Lago Posadas, RP 41 continues north to the Roballos Pass, which crosses into Cochrane, Chile. According to the Roads Department report it is an improved dirt road that can be closed by snow in winter. The pass’s status and hours change: check before you travel at Argentina.gob.ar – Pasos internacionales: Paso Roballos (estado y contacto) (published phone: (2963) 432033).

When to go

The climate is cold, very dry and windy. Turismo Ruta 40 gives winter lows down to −20 °C, summer highs up to 32 °C and 10 to 30 mm of rain a month, and recommends traveling between November and April. That matches the best season given by Parque Patagonia, November to March. In winter, according to La Nación, there can be more than a meter of snow and the road can stay closed for several days.

How many days to stay

  • Quick stop: 30 to 60 minutes to refuel, eat and get online on the wifi.
  • One night: enough to visit the Cave of the Hands the next day without rushing.
  • Two or three nights: if you add Parque Patagonia and a side trip to Lago Posadas.

These are our own suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: leave Perito Moreno or Gobernador Gregores with a full tank and top up again in Bajo Caracoles even if you still have half a tank. Turismo Ruta 40 recommends filling up at every station you find.
  • Cash: some stations in the area may not take cards, warns Turismo Ruta 40.
  • Jerry can: carry an extra, approved one if you are taking the detour to Lago Posadas, the Cave of the Hands or the Roballos Pass.
  • Gear: water, food, a paper map and warm clothes, even in summer, as Turismo Ruta 40 recommends.
  • Roads: especially from April to October, check before you travel at Vialidad Provincial de Santa Cruz (AGVP) – Parte diario de rutas, distrito Perito Moreno.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a gas station in Bajo Caracoles?

Yes. The pump is at the hotel’s general store. Since fuel is trucked in from Perito Moreno, some grades may run out: always arrive with a reserve and carry cash.

How far is Bajo Caracoles from the Cave of the Hands?

About 47 km of gravel on RP 97, according to the Provincial Roads Department. Driving time depends on the road conditions: check the roads report before you set out.

Can you stay overnight in Bajo Caracoles?

Yes, at the Hotel Bajo Caracoles, in the historic building. There is also space to camp in season. Check availability and rates before you travel.

Is Ruta 40 paved on this stretch?

Yes, between Perito Moreno and Gobernador Gregores, according to Turismo Ruta 40. The detours to Lago Posadas and the Cave of the Hands are gravel.

Is there cell phone signal in Bajo Caracoles?

Not in the village, according to La Nación (2022). The hotel’s wifi is the nearest connection.
