Home › Articles › Lago Posadas and Ruta 41: a guide to northwestern Santa Cruz

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-23

Lago Posadas is a small town in northwestern Santa Cruz, right next to the Chilean border. It sits near two lakes of different colors: the Posadas, turquoise green, and the Pueyrredón, deep blue, separated by a narrow isthmus. The general page is at Lago Posadas.

There are two ways in. The classic one leaves Ruta 40 at Bajo Caracoles. The other is Provincial Route 41, a 155 km gravel road that runs down from Los Antiguos along the Andes, with marked viewpoints and an official audio guide.

Where Lago Posadas is

The town is in the Río Chico department, in the northwest of the province. Until 2014 it was called Hipólito Yrigoyen, which is why some guides still use that name. It grew around the main house of a sheep ranch and today lives on sport fishing, trekking, birdwatching and 4x4 tours, according to the province. Wikipedia records 662 inhabitants in the 2022 census.

Sources differ a little: the province says 75 km of gravel from Bajo Caracoles and about 130 km between Perito Moreno and Bajo Caracoles; Interpatagonia and Welcome Argentina say 124 km and 72 km. The El Sauco figure comes from Río Negro. We found no official information on scheduled buses to the town: the practical option is your own or a rented vehicle. Road conditions: check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Estado de rutas (Vialidad).

Ruta 41: the scenic road

RP 41 links Los Antiguos with Lago Posadas inland from Ruta 40, parallel to the Andes. It's 155 km of gravel with 22 panoramic points, according to the provincial press office. According to the province, in summer it's an alternative way to reach the town; in winter some sections may be closed by snow.

Markers and the official audio guide

The road is marked with posts: red-tipped ones mark spots where you can get out of the vehicle and green-tipped ones mark points of interest. Santa Cruz Tourism publishes a free audio guide with an introduction and tracks A to N, starting at the Jeinimeni River viewpoint and covering about 115 km. The province suggests driving at around 40 km/h; at that pace, that stretch takes about two hours without stops. Download it before you leave from the official audio guide page and reset your trip odometer to zero at the viewpoint, as the province advises.

What you'll see along the way

  • Lago Buenos Aires (General Carrera on the Chilean side), at the start of the route.
  • The La Ascensión portal of Patagonia National Park, a former cattle ranch.
  • The Jeinimeni River viewpoint and the Los Cerritos viewpoint.
  • Monte Zeballos, 2,750 m above sea level according to La Capital, and the Zeballos River falls.
  • The Barda de las Toscas Bayas, with sandstone more than 15 million years old.
  • Cerro Boleadora and Laguna Escondida.
  • Views of Cerro San Lorenzo, the highest mountain in the province.

Along the way it's common to come across guanacos, foxes and condors.

Patagonia National Park: El Sauco area

El Sauco is the entrance to the southern sector of Patagonia National Park, on RP 41, 40 km from Bajo Caracoles. According to Río Negro (2023), it has a rustic camping area, two easy trails and one moderate trail that leads to a lagoon. Hours, fees and services for the season: check before you travel at Parques Nacionales – Parque Nacional Patagonia: horarios y cómo llegar.

How much time to allow

With viewpoints, photos and a short walk or two, set aside a full day for Ruta 41. It's not a road to rush.

What to do in Lago Posadas

Walk the isthmus between the two lakes

It's the main outing, part of what the province calls the Lakes Circuit. A strip of land separates the Posadas from the Pueyrredón and lets you see the two water colors, one on each side. Sources disagree on its width: Interpatagonia and Welcome Argentina say about 200 m, and Canal 26 says 100 m wide and 4 km long. The Posadas drains into the Pueyrredón through a short stretch of river, and both belong to the Pacific basin.

See the Stone Arch and La Costosa beach

In the middle part of Lake Posadas there's a bridge-shaped rock, eroded by water and wind: it's the best-known picture of the place. On the same circuit is La Costosa beach, so shallow that, according to the province, you can walk with the water at waist height.

Rock art: Cerro de los Indios and Cueva del Puma

Cerro de los Indios is one of the three archaeological sites open to the public in northwestern Santa Cruz, along with the Cueva de las Manos and Alero Charcamata. According to Noticias Ambientales, you reach it on a moderate hike and it has rock paintings more than 4,000 years old; the same outlet notes that these sites are visited with authorized guides. The province also highlights the Cueva del Puma, with paintings more than 3,000 years old. Access and guides: ask at the Lago Posadas Tourism Office.

Fishing

The lakes hold rainbow trout, perch and silverside (pejerrey). The Pueyrredón continues on the Chilean side as Lake Cochrane, and because of that connection to the Pacific, sea bass (róbalo) and salmon are also mentioned. You can fish from shore or from a boat. You need the provincial fishing permit; seasons and rules: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (provincia) – Permisos de pesca, temporada 2026/2027.

Trekking and mountaineering

Excursions from the area go to Cerro del Indio, the Río Oro gorge and the trek to Cerro San Lorenzo. The long treks involve fording glacial meltwater rivers and crossing glaciers: they're for people with experience and mountain gear, ideally with a guide.

Camping at Lake Pueyrredón

Interpatagonia and Welcome Argentina mention a campground on the shore of Lake Pueyrredón that works as a base for excursions. Services and fees: ask at the Tourism Office.

When to go

In our view, the best time is October to April, the same season Río Negro gives for Patagonia National Park. Summer brings the most visitors. According to Wikipedia, in the Lake Posadas basin the mean annual temperature is around 4.5 °C with about 200 mm of rain a year; the valley has a milder microclimate and summer highs are around 24 °C. The west wind is a constant.

The southern continuation of Ruta 41, between Lago Posadas and Perito Moreno National Park, opened in December 2019 as a high-mountain road: according to Vía País, it is open only from November to March, with 78 km suitable for 4x4 vehicles only. Before you take it, check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Estado de rutas (Vialidad).

How many days to stay

  • 1 day: the isthmus, the Stone Arch and the shore of the Pueyrredón.
  • 2 days: add the rock art with a guide and a fishing trip.
  • 3 or 4 days: arrive via Ruta 41 from Los Antiguos, stop at El Sauco and do a longer hike.

These are our own suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: leave Los Antiguos or Perito Moreno with a full tank. There are no services on Ruta 41. According to Turismo Ruta 40 there is fuel in Lago Posadas and Bajo Caracoles, but not always: arrive with some to spare.
  • Supplies: bring water and food for the day if you're heading to the lakes or the road.
  • Accommodation: there are cabins and campgrounds, but not many. Book ahead in summer.
  • Car: drive slowly on gravel and check your spare tire before you leave.
  • Offline: download maps and the audio guide before you set off.
  • Lago Posadas Tourism Office: Av. San Martín 50, phone +54 2975 062765, Monday to Friday 8 am to 7 pm, according to the province.

Frequently asked questions

Can you drive Ruta 41 in a regular car?

The Los Antiguos to Lago Posadas section is mountain gravel and you drive it slowly, at about 40 km/h as the province suggests. In winter it can close due to snow: check road conditions with Vialidad before you set off. The extension towards Perito Moreno National Park was opened for 4x4 vehicles only.

Are Lago Posadas and Hipólito Yrigoyen the same place?

Yes. It's the same town: it was called Hipólito Yrigoyen until 2014.

How far is it from Bajo Caracoles to Lago Posadas?

About 75 km of gravel according to the province; Interpatagonia says 72 km on RP 39.

Can you combine it with the Cueva de las Manos?

Yes. Both are in the northwest of the province and share Ruta 40 as their backbone. One possible loop is Perito Moreno, Cueva de las Manos, Bajo Caracoles and Lago Posadas.
