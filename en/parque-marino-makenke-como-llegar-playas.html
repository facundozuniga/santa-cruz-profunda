Home › Articles › Makenke Marine Park: how to get there, beaches and wildlife near Puerto San Julián

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-27

Makenke Interjurisdictional Marine Park is the marine protected area surrounding Puerto San Julián. It protects a strip of coast and sea in the Argentine Sea ecoregion, with beaches below cliffs, the country's main breeding colony of red-legged cormorants, elephant seals and Commerson's dolphins that you can see from shore.

There are no opening hours and no entrance fee: according to National Parks, entry is free. You explore it by car or bike on gravel roads that leave Route 3 a few kilometers from town. Here you'll find the access points, beaches, wildlife and what to know about wind and tides. For everything else in town, see the Puerto San Julián guide.

Where is Makenke Marine Park?

The park is on the Atlantic coast of Santa Cruz, around San Julián bay and peninsula. “Makenke” means “the new ones” in the Tehuelche language, according to National Parks.

It is a marine area: the founding treaty says it covers the sea surface, including the seabed and subsoil, off the coast running from Playa La Mina, north of the bay, to the south of the peninsula. The National Parks fact sheet gives it 72,673 hectares; Visit Argentina, the country's official tourism website, still lists 71,272.

It is “interjurisdictional” because it is jointly managed by the National Parks Administration and the Province of Santa Cruz. Decisions are made by an Executive Management Commission with two representatives from National Parks, one from the province and one from the Municipality of Puerto San Julián.

How to get to Makenke Marine Park

The base for visiting is Puerto San Julián, on National Route 3. Both access roads to the beaches start from there.

Distances from National Parks.

By car

There are two ways in:

  • Northern zone, via the Coastal Circuit. The gravel access is at km 2230 of RN 3, 19 km north of Puerto San Julián. The Coastal Circuit also starts in town and runs along the bay. It has signs, leaflets and a free official audio guide from the province: download it before you set off, because the signal drops along the way.
  • Central zone, via the Península de San Julián Provincial Reserve. The access is at km 2262 of RN 3, 12 km south of town. From there it's 17 km of gravel to the beaches.

In rain or mud, the gravel gets slippery. For road conditions, check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Estado de rutas (Vialidad). National Parks recommends visiting from September to April, partly because the roads are in better shape.

By bus

Long-distance buses running along Route 3 stop at the Puerto San Julián bus terminal (Av. San Martín 1570, tel. (02962) 452072). National Parks publishes the list of companies; check schedules and frequencies at the terminal before you travel. We found no public transport from town to the beaches: you need a car, a bike or a tour booked in town.

By air

National Parks still mentions LADE flights to San Julián's Capitán José Daniel Vázquez airport, but as of November 27, 2026 we found no current scheduled flights. The usual option is to fly to Comodoro Rivadavia or Río Gallegos and continue on Route 3. More options in how to get to Santa Cruz.

A bit of history

San Julián bay was one of the first points of contact between European explorers and Patagonia. National Parks notes that Ferdinand Magellan's expedition entered the bay on March 31, 1520 and stayed seven months; Francis Drake came later and, in the early 19th century, Charles Darwin. According to the same source, the encounters with the Aonikenk or Tehuelche people gave rise to the name “patagones” and, with it, Patagonia.

The park was agreed on September 17, 2010 in a treaty between the national government and the province, signed in Puerto San Julián. Congress approved it with Law 26,817, passed on November 28, 2012.

Things to do in Makenke Marine Park

Explore the beaches of the northern zone

The Coastal Circuit takes you to four beach areas, according to National Parks:

  • Playa La Mina
  • Playa Garganta del Diablo (reached on foot, moderate difficulty)
  • Playa Médanos / Playa Pigafetta
  • Playa Los Pescadores / Playa Punta Cuevas

The beaches are free to access. Swimming is not allowed: the water is deep and cold, and there are no lifeguards or facilities.

Explore the beaches of the central zone

Through the Península de San Julián Reserve you reach two beaches: Playa EmPaSA and Playa Los Instalados. It is the access with the most gravel, so plan the round trip in daylight. In the reserve, National Parks mentions steppe wildlife such as guanacos, rheas, foxes and armadillos.

See the red-legged cormorant colony

The cliffs of Apostadero Makenke and Playa La Mina hold Argentina's main breeding colony of red-legged cormorants: about 900 pairs, according to National Parks, which dive up to 10 meters to feed. From Playa Garganta del Diablo you can watch them flying over the sea. The park also has imperial and rock cormorants. Bring binoculars.

Spot Commerson's dolphins, Peale's dolphins and elephant seals

Commerson's dolphins and Peale's dolphins are common even from the shore. Orcas also appear at sea, although nobody can guarantee you'll see them.

The southern elephant seal is the park's emblem. It is the largest seal: males can reach 6 meters in length and weigh about 5 tonnes. It spends most of the year at sea and comes back to the beaches to breed in summer. The park's coast is also home to South American sea lions and Magellanic penguins.

For birdwatchers, the list includes upland geese, dolphin gulls, plovers, sandpipers, oystercatchers, swans, black-crowned night herons and Chilean flamingos. National Parks publishes a downloadable wildlife-watching calendar for San Julián and Makenke.

Sail to Isla Cormorán and Isla Justicia

The park has no boat trips of its own: you book them with operators in Puerto San Julián who go out into the bay. Opposite the town is Isla Cormorán, a provincial protected area, with a large Magellanic penguin colony. Figures vary by source: the National Parks fact sheet speaks of about 130,000 birds and its overview page of more than 60,000. Isla Justicia has colonies of imperial and rock cormorants. Duration, season and how to book: see the boat-trip section of the Puerto San Julián guide.

Fish from the shore

According to National Parks, sport fishing is open from November 1 to May 1 every year. Local species include tope shark, eagle ray, yellowtail amberjack, sea bream, elephant fish, hake, silverside, bonito, sea catfish, anchovy, Argentine anchovy and flounder. There are no sport-fishing regulations specific to marine areas, but shore gillnets are not allowed. If you need any permit to fish from the shore, ask the park office before you go.

Kayaking and cycling

You can paddle your own boat, as long as you meet the requirements and notifications of the Argentine Coast Guard (Prefectura Naval Argentina). National Parks also mentions kayaking in the bay through the Club Náutico El Delfín or the licensed operator. The Coastal Circuit roads are good for cycling, between the steppe and the sea.

When to visit Makenke Marine Park

The climate is cool temperate, arid to semi-arid and very windy, according to Visit Argentina. Beach access is open all year.

  • September to April: the season National Parks recommends. This is when there are the most Magellanic penguins, Commerson's dolphins, Peale's dolphins, southern right whales, sea lions and birds, and the roads are in better condition.
  • November 1 to May 1: sport-fishing season.
  • Summer: elephant seals return to the beaches to breed.
  • May to August: colder, fewer daylight hours and tougher roads. Check the wildlife calendar before you go.

The park has no opening hours. National Parks recommends going in with good visibility, leaving before sunset because driving those roads at night is very dangerous, and keeping an eye on the tides.

How many days to stay

Visit Argentina recommends 1 to 3 days. With 1 day you can do the Coastal Circuit and a beach in the northern zone. With 2 or 3 days you add the central zone via the peninsula and a boat trip on the bay. Where to sleep and eat: Puerto San Julián directory. There is no accommodation inside the park.

Travel tips

  • Check the tide tables from the Naval Hydrographic Service before going down to the beach. The tidal range is very large.
  • Pack warm layers and a windbreaker. Strong gusts are common and, even in summer, nights are cool.
  • Stock up on water, food and fuel in San Julián. According to National Parks, the park has no drinking water, shops, restaurants, picnic areas, grills, fire pits, jetties or WiFi.
  • Take your trash with you. Leave nothing behind in the park.
  • Keep your distance from wildlife, especially in the breeding season.
  • Don't go in the water. Swimming is not allowed.
  • For questions, the park office is open Monday to Friday from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. at Av. Hernando de Magallanes 335, Puerto San Julián. Phone: 02962 46-0952. Email: pimmakenke@apn.gob.ar.

Frequently asked questions

Do you have to pay to enter Makenke Marine Park?

No. According to Argentina's National Parks Administration, entry is free.

Can you go without a car?

It's hard: we found no public transport to the beaches. You can cycle the Coastal Circuit or book a tour in Puerto San Julián.

Are there penguins in Makenke?

Yes. National Parks mentions a Magellanic penguin colony on the park's coast. The big colony is on Isla Cormorán, opposite the town, and you visit it on a boat trip from Puerto San Julián.

Can you camp or stay overnight inside the park?

National Parks says there is no accommodation inside the park, and no fire pits, grills or drinking water. Accommodation is in Puerto San Julián. If you want to camp in the area, check with the park office first.

How is it different from the Península de San Julián Reserve?

The reserve is a provincial protected area on land. Makenke is a marine park. The road to the beaches in the park's central zone crosses the reserve.
