Home › Articles › How to get to Santa Cruz: flights, buses and roads to travel around the province

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-13

Santa Cruz is huge, sparsely populated and far from almost everything. Río Gallegos, the capital, is 2,535 km by road from the City of Buenos Aires, according to the national government. Between towns there are hundreds of kilometers of steppe. That is why working out how to get to Santa Cruz is half the trip.

Many people arrive through El Calafate and stay in the Andes. But if you want to see the coast, the north or the deep Ruta 40, it pays to combine plane, bus and car. The gateway you choose determines how many kilometers you will drive afterwards. Here you have the airports with commercial flights, how to get around by bus, the national routes and the key distances. Flights, schedules and frequencies change often: always confirm them with each company before you travel.

Where it is and how to plan your way in

Santa Cruz borders Chubut to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the east and Chile to the west and south. For planning, it helps to think of it in three areas:

  • North (Caleta Olivia, Pico Truncado, Las Heras, Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos): the most practical gateway is Comodoro Rivadavia, in Chubut.
  • South and coast (Río Gallegos, Piedra Buena, Puerto Santa Cruz, Puerto San Julián): the natural gateway is Río Gallegos.
  • Andes (El Calafate, El Chaltén, Tres Lagos): the gateway is El Calafate airport.

Getting there by air

According to the national government, Santa Cruz has two international airports: Río Gallegos and El Calafate. Since 2026 there is also the scheduled flight to Perito Moreno.

El Calafate airport (Comandante Armando Tola)

It is 23 km from downtown El Calafate, reached via Provincial Route 11. According to Wikipedia, Aerolíneas Argentinas flies to Aeroparque, Córdoba, Trelew and Ushuaia, and seasonally also to Ezeiza; JetSMART flies to Aeroparque and Ezeiza; Sky has seasonal flights to Santiago de Chile, and Gol announced seasonal flights to São Paulo from November 26, 2026. These routes and frequencies change with the season: confirm them with each airline before booking.

Río Gallegos airport (Piloto Civil Norberto Fernández)

It is 8 km northwest of the city, on National Route 3, km 8, according to the national government. In the schedules published for November 2026, Aerolíneas Argentinas is the only airline with direct flights from Aeroparque, with about 11 flights a week.

Watch out for outdated information: Flybondi launched the Ezeiza–Río Gallegos route on February 3, 2025, with six weekly flights, but suspended it from May 16, 2025. The company said it was evaluating its continuity and, as of November 2026, we found no sign that it has resumed the route.

Perito Moreno airport

This is the big news of 2026. Since July 1, American Jet has linked Buenos Aires with Perito Moreno, with a stop in Comodoro Rivadavia, according to Diario Nuevo Día. The province reported flights on Tuesdays and alternating Wednesdays, with tickets sold on the airline's website. For the northern Andes this is a big change: by land, Perito Moreno is 382 km from Comodoro Rivadavia, according to Interpatagonia. It is a new route: confirm days and times with American Jet before planning your trip. From Perito Moreno you continue to the Cueva de las Manos and to Los Antiguos.

Puerto Deseado and Puerto San Julián

They currently have no scheduled commercial flights. On June 23, 2026, the province and the municipality of Puerto Deseado signed an agreement to upgrade the airfield (perimeter fence, aviation fuel plant and X-ray scanner), and the provincial plan also includes San Julián, according to the Ministry of Production. In November 2026 the municipality of Puerto Deseado opened tenders for the fuel plant and the scanner, but no date has been announced for flights to return, according to Diario Nuevo Día. To get to those towns, see Things to do in Puerto Deseado and the Puerto San Julián guide.

Comodoro Rivadavia, the northern gateway

Although it is in Chubut, Comodoro Rivadavia airport is the most practical option for northern Santa Cruz. From Comodoro, Caleta Olivia is between 68 and 78 km away on paved Route 3: Interpatagonia gives both figures on different pages.

Getting there by bus

According to Los Andes (July 2025), Andesmar connects Santa Cruz with much of the country, with departures from Jujuy, Salta, Tucumán, Córdoba, Buenos Aires, Mendoza and Trelew, among other cities, and reaches Río Gallegos, Caleta Olivia, Puerto San Julián, Puerto Santa Cruz and Piedra Buena. Schedules, travel times and fares: check them on the company's website.

Within the province, Marga Taqsa lists routes such as Río Gallegos–El Calafate, El Calafate–El Chaltén, Río Gallegos–Caleta Olivia and Los Antiguos–Perito Moreno, plus Ushuaia–Río Gallegos. Other regional companies operate at each town's bus terminal; Interpatagonia publishes lists with phone numbers by town, but we could not confirm which are still running: ask at the terminal or the tourist office.

On Ruta 40 there is no single end-to-end ticket: you have to buy by sections, and in high season services fill up fast, according to BAE Negocios. Book ahead.

Getting there by car

According to the national government, the national routes crossing Santa Cruz are 3, 40, 281, 288 and 293, plus many provincial roads. Ruta 3 runs along the coast and Ruta 40 runs through the west, at the foot of the Andes.

Between El Calafate and Río Gallegos you take paved Ruta 40 to Cerrito and then Provincial Route 5, also paved: Interpatagonia gives 280 km for that route and 306 km in its distance table.

Crossing to Tierra del Fuego and Chile

To continue to Ushuaia by land you have to go through Chile. From Río Gallegos you take Route 3 to the Integración Austral border post and, once in Chile, cross the Strait of Magellan on the Primera Angostura ferry, between Punta Delgada and Bahía Azul. Border post and ferry hours: check before you travel at Argentina.gob.ar – Centro de frontera Integración Austral and at TABSA. Details of this stretch are in Ruta 3 in Santa Cruz.

According to the national government, Santa Cruz has thirteen international crossings with Chile, including Integración Austral, Río Jeinimeni (Los Antiguos), Roballos, Dorotea and Laurita–Casas Viejas. Some mountain crossings change their hours or close in winter: before you set off, check their status at Migraciones. A comparison of crossings, hours and documents: Border crossings from Santa Cruz to Chile. More detail: Paso Jeinimeni and Río Turbio and 28 de Noviembre, with the crossings to Puerto Natales.

When to go

High season runs from November to March, with long days and more services in small towns; several flight routes to El Calafate are seasonal only. In winter (June to August) there is less daylight, and snow and ice on some roads. See Climate in Santa Cruz: when to travel, wind and temperatures.

How many days you need

It depends on how much you want to see and how far you drive each day. As a guide: the coast (Río Gallegos, Piedra Buena, San Julián, Puerto Deseado) needs at least 5 days; the northern Andes (Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos, Cueva de las Manos), 4 to 5 days; and if you want to combine the coast and Ruta 40, think 10 days or more.

Travel tips

  • Mix your arrival and departure points. Fly into one airport and out of another: for example, arrive via Comodoro Rivadavia and fly home from El Calafate.
  • Consider renting a car. Many attractions have no public transport.
  • Fill up whenever you can. There are long stretches without services between towns: the fuel stops on each route are in our guides to Ruta 40 and Ruta 3.
  • Check road conditions before you set off, especially in winter, in the roads report published by the province.
  • If you cross into Chile in a rental car, ask the company for authorization to leave the country and check the border formalities.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best airport to fly into Santa Cruz?

It depends on the area. For the glaciers and El Chaltén, El Calafate. For the southern coast, Río Gallegos. For the north, Comodoro Rivadavia (Chubut) or, since 2026, Perito Moreno.

How many kilometers is it by car from Buenos Aires to Río Gallegos?

It is 2,535 km by road, according to the national government. There is no official driving time: it depends on stops, wind and daylight hours, so it is best to split the trip over several days.

Are there direct flights from Buenos Aires to Río Gallegos?

Yes. In November 2026, Aerolíneas Argentinas flies direct from Aeroparque. Flybondi flew from Ezeiza between February and May 2025 and then suspended the route.

Are there flights to Perito Moreno?

Yes. Since July 1, 2026, American Jet has flown between Buenos Aires and Perito Moreno with a stop in Comodoro Rivadavia. Check days and times with the airline.

Do you have to go through Chile to drive from Santa Cruz to Ushuaia?

Yes. There is no continuous road through Argentine territory: you cross at the Integración Austral border post and take the ferry across the Strait of Magellan at Primera Angostura.
