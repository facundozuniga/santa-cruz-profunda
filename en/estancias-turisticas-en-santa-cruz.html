Home › Articles › Tourist estancias in Santa Cruz: where they are and what to do on a Patagonian ranch

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-13

In Santa Cruz, the sheep estancia is the basic unit of the land. For more than a century, wool shaped life on the plateau and along the coast. Today several of those estancias open their gates to visitors, offering lodging, home-cooked meals, horseback rides and a close look at ranch work.

For anyone traveling the province beyond El Calafate, an estancia solves two problems at once. It gives you somewhere to sleep in areas with few towns and works as a base to reach lakes, national parks or penguin colonies that have no other convenient access.

This guide gathers estancias with published, up-to-date information, organized by area. It is not a complete list. Before you travel, always confirm that the estancia is still open to tourism, its season dates and whether you need to book.

What is a tourist estancia

It is a rural property that is still working, usually with sheep, and that also hosts visitors. According to Interpatagonia, the estancias around Gobernador Gregores share sheep farming, home cooking and nearby rivers and lakes that are good for fishing.

What they offer varies a lot. Some have a few rooms in the main house and are run by their owners; others work as fishing lodges or add a hostel and a camping area. Ask how each one works before planning your itinerary.

A good reference is Estancias de Santa Cruz, a private organization that promotes tourist estancias and country inns in southern Patagonia. Its website has listings for La Angostura, La Oriental, Monte Dinero, La Estela (El Chaltén), Cristina (Los Glaciares National Park), La Posta de los Toldos (Patagonia Park) and Río Tarde (Lago Posadas).

In January 2026, the Santa Cruz Ministry of Production and Banco Santa Cruz announced soft credit lines for rural tourism and launched a preliminary, non-binding survey among producers, according to Noticias Ambientales. It targets both those who already host tourists and those who want to start.

Estancias in the center of the province (Gobernador Gregores)

Gobernador Gregores is the gateway to several estancias on the central plateau and in the Andean foothills. Distances are long and some roads are gravel.

La Angostura

It sits in the Chico River valley, surrounded by trees. According to Estancias de Santa Cruz, the Kusanovic family has owned it since 1878; it opens from November to March and has six rooms with private bathrooms. The main house is 4 km from the road. It offers birdwatching, walks and horseback rides (by advance booking only). Interpatagonia adds home-cooked meals with the owners, fishing outings and birdwatching at an inner lagoon. It sells fuel to guests only, there is no cell signal and no bus stops on RP 29.

Laguna Verde

It is a fly-fishing lodge on the southern shore of Lake Strobel, known as Jurassic Lake for its big rainbow trout, according to its official website. Interpatagonia and Welcome Argentina count about 15 small lagoons on the property and place the Barrancoso River, a snowmelt river good for fly fishing, 8 km from the lodge. They also mention 4x4 outings, horseback rides and visits to Tehuelche ruins and rock art about 6,000 years old. Interpatagonia warns that Lake Strobel has strong winds and waves. Catch-and-release is mandatory on the Strobel: see Pesca deportiva en Santa Cruz: temporada 2026/27, permisos y dónde pescar. Season and rates: check with the lodge.

La Oriental

It is inside Perito Moreno National Park, on RP 37 at km 89, and the main house is 8 km west of the park headquarters, according to Estancias de Santa Cruz. It is run by the Lada family. National Parks lists 11 beds in the main house, 8 in the cabin and 8 in the dorms, and the estancia also offers a camping area, home cooking and a transfer service inside the park to the trails.

Welcome Argentina mentions guanacos and condors, horseback rides and drives to Lake Burmeister and the Lácteo River rapids. The organization adds guided excursions inside the park with a licensed guide and 4x4 or fishing outings outside the park, arranged in advance. The season is listed as November to March (Estancias de Santa Cruz) or November to April (Welcome Argentina): confirm dates when booking. There is no cell signal or phone, fuel is sold to guests only and no public transport enters the park.

Estancias like La Angostura and La Oriental make a good base for exploring the area: see Qué hacer en Gobernador Gregores.

Estancias in the northwest (Perito Moreno)

Telken

It is 3 km off Route 40, about 25 km south of Perito Moreno, on the way to the Cave of the Hands, according to Patagonia-Argentina.com, which lists a season from October to April. According to Welcome Argentina, it has operated since 1915 and the family has raised sheep and horses for three generations. It offers walks with the owners, visits to the sheds and stables, short rides through the canyons and shared meals; on multi-day stays they organize 4x4 or horseback outings to the Lake Buenos Aires basalt plateau or the Cave of the Hands.

We could not find an official website or recent information for Telken: call to confirm it is still hosting guests before you count on it. In the same area, La Posta de los Toldos, at the Cañadón Pinturas Portal, is covered in our Patagonia Park guide.

Estancias on the coast and in the south

Monte Dinero (Cabo Vírgenes)

It is the southernmost estancia in mainland Argentina, with 26,000 hectares, according to La Nación (October 2026). It is 115 km from Río Gallegos and 14 km from Cabo Vírgenes, according to its website; the address is RN 40, km 14, because Route 40 crosses the ranch up to its km 0. La Nación mentions a two-and-a-half-hour drive from Río Gallegos via RN 3 and RN 40 (former RP 1). The original house dates from 1880 and the Fenton family has run it for five generations.

According to La Nación, it hosts guests from November to late March in six rooms with shared bathrooms, and Posada Ruta 40, a more affordable option with shared-bathroom rooms, operates on the same ranch. The estancia website gives the posada a season from mid-October to Easter: confirm dates and rates when booking.

The estancia raises about 9,000 Merino sheep. Welcome Argentina explains that shearing, lamb marking, wool classing and droving happen at different times of the year, so ask what you will be able to see on your dates; pre-lambing shearing is in September, according to La Nación. Guests also tour the ranch, the cliffs and a South American sea lion colony.

The Cabo Vírgenes lighthouse has operated since 1904 on land donated by the Fenton family, and next to it is the Al Fin y al Cabo tea house, run by the estancia and open in season from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. The Cabo Vírgenes Provincial Reserve, created in 1986, protects 1,230 ha and a Magellanic penguin colony: La Nación cites about 125,000 breeding pairs and SiFAP about 150,000. In June 2026 a new viewpoint was finished and the trails were reorganized. Full details in Cabo Vírgenes: cómo llegar a la pingüinera, el faro y el km 0.

La María (Puerto San Julián)

It is on the plateau, about 140–150 km from San Julián via provincial routes 25 and 77, both gravel. The municipal website describes it as a 22,000-hectare archaeological site with 87 caves and rock shelters with rock art. Season, bookings and road conditions: check before you travel at Turismo Puerto San Julián (municipal) – ¿Qué visitar?. More in the Puerto San Julián guide.

Other estancias

Travel guides mention other estancias offering tourism in the province, mostly near Río Gallegos, Puerto Deseado and Gobernador Gregores. We could not find up-to-date information confirming they still host visitors, so we left them out. If you know of one that is open, write to us.

When to go

Summer is estancia season: every estancia in this guide that publishes a season opens between October and April, and most from November to March. It is also Magellanic penguin season at Cabo Vírgenes, from September to March according to SiFAP. None publishes a winter season, and snow or mud make gravel roads tricky: before you set out, check the road conditions. More in Clima en Santa Cruz: cuándo viajar, viento y temperaturas.

How many days to stay

  • One night: enough to break up a long stretch of Route 40, as at La Angostura or Telken.
  • Two nights: the minimum for remote estancias like La Oriental or Monte Dinero, where the drive in already takes half a day.
  • Three nights or more: if you want to fish, hike in the national park or go on long horseback rides.

Travel tips

  • Book ahead. Estancias have few beds and some activities, like horseback rides at La Angostura, are by booking only.
  • Confirm how to get there. Several are more than 100 km from the nearest town and part of the road is gravel.
  • Fill up in every town. Some estancias sell fuel only to their guests.
  • Expect no signal. There is no cell signal at La Angostura or La Oriental: let someone know your itinerary.
  • Bring warm layers and a windbreaker, even in summer.
  • Ask about payment methods when booking.
  • Respect the gates: leave them as you found them.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be an experienced rider to go horseback riding?

Not necessarily: Telken, for example, offers short rides. Mention your experience when booking, because some estancias only run rides with advance booking.

Can I visit an estancia without staying overnight?

It depends on each one. The Al Fin y al Cabo tea house, run by Monte Dinero next to the Cabo Vírgenes lighthouse, opens in season from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. For farm activities without lodging, check with each estancia.

Can I get there without a car?

It is difficult. According to Estancias de Santa Cruz, no bus stops on RP 29 at La Angostura or enters Perito Moreno National Park. La Oriental offers transfers inside the park; to get to the estancia itself, ask them.

Which estancias offer fishing?

Laguna Verde is a fly-fishing lodge on Lake Strobel; La Angostura offers fishing outings and La Oriental organizes fishing outside the national park. You always need the current provincial permit.

Which one suits a Route 40 trip?

La Angostura is 4 km from RP 29 (the former Route 40), near Gobernador Gregores, and Telken is 3 km off Route 40 south of Perito Moreno. La Oriental is 100 km inland on RP 37 from the Las Horquetas junction.
