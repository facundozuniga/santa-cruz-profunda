Home › Articles › What to do in El Chaltén beyond Fitz Roy: trails, lakes and plans for windy days

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-11

Almost everyone arrives in El Chaltén with a single photo in mind: Fitz Roy from Laguna de los Tres. It is a great trail, but the weather does not guarantee the peak will show itself on the day you pick.

The town has trails for every level, a lake an hour away by car, boat trips and plans for windy days. Here is what to do in El Chaltén beyond Fitz Roy, with distances and times from official sources and local guides. The short destination page is at El Chaltén.

Where El Chaltén is and how to get there

El Chaltén lies in northwestern Santa Cruz, inside the North Zone of Los Glaciares National Park, at the foot of Fitz Roy (3,405 m, according to the El Chaltén portal and the National Parks leaflet) and Cerro Torre. It was founded in 1985 as a geopolitical move amid the border dispute with Chile over the Lago del Desierto area, according to Welcome Argentina.

The nearest airport is El Calafate (FTE). From there you drive north on National Route 40 and then on the provincial road along Lake Viedma, paved all the way to town. The El Chaltén portal and National Parks call it RP 41; other sources, such as English Wikipedia and Welcome Argentina, call it RP 23.

By bus

According to the El Chaltén portal, in high season (October to April) there are several daily departures from El Calafate and most stop at the airport. From May to September there are sometimes only two a day in each direction. From December to February it is best to book, since the first morning and the afternoon departures sell out. The El Chaltén bus terminal charges a municipal fee. Schedules, fares and the fee amount change: check with each company or the Tourism Office before you travel.

By car

If you come along Route 40 from the north, fill up at every station: between Bajo Caracoles, Gobernador Gregores and Tres Lagos there are very long stretches without gas stations. Turismo Ruta 40 lists El Chaltén among the towns with a gas station, as a detour from Route 40. Road conditions: check before you travel at Vialidad Provincial de Santa Cruz (AGVP) – Parte de transitabilidad de rutas nacionales.

Before you go: the park entry fee

Since October 8, 2026, the National Parks Administration is again charging access at the three North Zone portals: Los Cóndores (at the Visitor Center, before entering town), Cerro Torre–Base Fitz Roy (at the end of Avenida San Martín) and Río Eléctrico (about 16 km away, on RP 41), according to Ahora Calafate.

The rates in force since June 1, 2026 are $50.000 general, $25.000 for Argentine residents, $8.000 for Santa Cruz residents and $15.000 for students. Some people are exempt, such as retirees with an Argentine ID, children up to 6 and local residents. In the North Zone tickets are only available online, on the website or by scanning the QR code at the portal, and can only be paid by credit or debit card: cash is not accepted. Bring ID to prove residence. Current rates: check before you travel at Parques Nacionales – Los Glaciares: tarifas y portales de la Zona Norte (desde junio de 2026).

What to do in El Chaltén beyond Fitz Roy

Almost every trail starts from town. Before heading out, stop at the National Parks Ceferino Fonzo Visitor Center to check trail conditions. We could not find current official opening hours for the center: ask by email at informespnlg@apn.gob.ar or at the Tourism Office.

Laguna Torre

This is the second great classic. The trail leaves town and follows the Fitz Roy River valley to the lagoon, with Cerro Torre behind it if the day is clear. According to the National Parks leaflet it is 10 km and about 3.5 hours one way, with 250 m of elevation gain. The De Agostini campsite is next to the lagoon, and about 2 km further on is the Maestri viewpoint, very exposed to the wind. If you have less time, the Torre viewpoint is 3 km from the trailhead (1 h 15 min).

Loma del Pliegue Tumbado

One of the best panoramic views in the area: Fitz Roy, Cerro Torre, Lake Viedma and the Fitz Roy River valley all at once. It starts at the Visitor Center. According to the El Chaltén portal, it climbs about 1,100 m to a viewpoint at roughly 1,500 m and takes 7 to 8 hours round trip; National Parks estimates 4 hours one way.

The last stretch is marked with yellow markers, has no trees, is very exposed to the wind and can have snow even in summer. If there is snow or mud up top, turn back: it is a steep, rocky area where it is easy to get lost.

Los Cóndores and Las Águilas viewpoints

The ideal plan for your arrival afternoon or a short day. They start at the Visitor Center: according to National Parks, Los Cóndores is 1 km away (about 45 minutes) and has a nice view of town with the mountains behind, and Las Águilas, about 2 km away (1 hour), looks out over Lake Viedma and the steppe.

Chorrillo del Salto

National Parks calls it the easiest trail in the area: a waterfall of more than 20 m in a ñire and lenga forest. You can walk from the end of Avenida San Martín, less than 4 km one way according to the El Chaltén portal, or drive or bike along the road to Lago del Desierto to the parking area and walk about 15 minutes from there. The last stretch is on foot only. Access is through the Cerro Torre–Base Fitz Roy portal, so the park entry fee applies.

Lago del Desierto and the Huemul Glacier

Lago del Desierto is 37 km north of town, on a gravel road along the Río de las Vueltas. From the pier at the south end there are two plans. One is the catamaran trip to the Gendarmería outpost at the north end: Welcome Argentina describes a trip of about 45 minutes. The other is the trail to the Huemul Glacier: almost an hour of steep but well-marked climbing through a beautiful forest to an emerald lagoon at the foot of the glacier. According to the El Chaltén portal, the trail is on private land and charges a separate fee; check before you travel at Portal El Chaltén – Glaciar Huemul (Lago del Desierto).

Operators, schedules and fares for the boat trips change by season: ask at the Tourism Office.

Viedma Glacier

From El Chaltén you can sail across Lake Viedma to the glacier front and, on some tours, walk on the ice with crampons, according to Welcome Argentina. We could not find current official information on the departure point, season or fares: check before you travel.

Rafting, horseback riding and fishing

The province lists rafting, horseback riding and sport fishing among the activities in El Chaltén. Local operators offer them with their own seasons and prices; to fish you need the provincial permit: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (provincia) – Permisos de pesca, temporada 2026/2027.

A day in town

For a windy day, Canal 26 highlights the craft breweries, spit-roasted lamb, craft and regional product shops, and cultural spaces about the first settlers and mountaineering. Opening hours of museums and cultural spaces: ask at the Tourism Office.

When to go to El Chaltén

According to Switchback Travel, a US hiking guide:

  • December to February: high season, with long days, highs of about 15 to 25 °C and lows of about 7 °C. It is also the windiest time, with gusts of up to about 120 km/h in town.
  • March and April: fewer people and better lodging prices, shorter days and more rain, but the wind usually calms down.
  • September to November: temperatures of about 10 to 15 °C and less wind. After very snowy winters, some trails still have snow.

In winter (May to September) there are fewer bus departures, according to the El Chaltén portal, and the road report may require chains. We could not find official information on which services and lodgings open in winter: ask at the Tourism Office.

How many days to stay

With two days you can do Laguna de los Tres and a short plan, such as the viewpoints or Chorrillo del Salto. With four you can add Laguna Torre and Lago del Desierto. Five or six days is ideal: that leaves room to wait for a clear day and add Pliegue Tumbado or a boat trip. These are our suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Registration: it is mandatory for climbing and trekking in remote areas, such as Laguna Toro, according to National Parks.
  • Camping: only at authorized sites, such as Capri, Poincenot and De Agostini.
  • Park rules: no fires (gas stoves only), no pets, no drones and no bikes on the trails.
  • Clothing: bring warm layers, a waterproof jacket, gloves, a hat, sunglasses and sunscreen even if the day starts out nice. National Parks warns that the weather in town is different from the weather in the mountains.
  • Signal: there is little or no cell signal on the trails. Bring a flashlight and plan to be back before dark.
  • Payments: the park entry fee in the North Zone can only be paid by card. For everything else bring cash and another payment option; we could not find official information about ATMs in town.
  • El Chaltén Tourism Office: Perito Moreno 28, Monday to Friday 8 am to 10 pm, tel. +54 9 2966 769216, infotur@elchalten.gob.ar, according to Turismo Santa Cruz.

Frequently asked questions

Do you have to pay to enter the national park in El Chaltén?

Yes. Since October 8, 2026, the National Parks Administration charges access at the Los Cóndores, Cerro Torre–Base Fitz Roy and Río Eléctrico portals. Since June 2026 the general rate is $50.000, $25.000 for Argentine residents and $8.000 for Santa Cruz residents; tickets are bought online or with the QR code at the portal and can only be paid by card.

What can I do if it is cloudy and Fitz Roy is hidden?

Try Chorrillo del Salto, Lago del Desierto with the Huemul Glacier, or the Los Cóndores and Las Águilas viewpoints. If the Torre valley is clear, go to Laguna Torre.

Can you get to Lago del Desierto without a car?

Yes. There are transfers and tours from town along the gravel road, about an hour each way. Ask the Tourism Office about companies and schedules.

How long does it take from El Calafate to El Chaltén?

Between 2 h 40 min and 3 hours for about 215 to 220 km, all paved. From the airport it is about 200 km, 2.5 to 3 hours.
