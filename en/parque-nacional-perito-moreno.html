Home › Articles › Perito Moreno National Park: how to get there, refuges and trails

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-06

First, a clarification that saves confusion: Perito Moreno National Park is not the glacier. The glacier is in Los Glaciares National Park, near El Calafate. This park is in northwestern Santa Cruz, on the border with Chile, and is one of the least visited in the country.

According to the National Parks Administration, it is the wildest park in the Andean Patagonia: steppe, Patagonian forests, glacial lakes in turquoise and emerald tones, and archaeological evidence of human presence going back about 10,000 years. There is no town or grocery store inside. The last stretch is gravel. In return, you get nearly empty trails and a network of refuges, domes and camping areas.

The base with services is Gobernador Gregores, where the park headquarters is. You can also plan the trip from Bajo Caracoles, Lago Posadas or the town of Perito Moreno (the town, not the glacier).

Where it is and how to get there

The main access, according to the National Parks Administration, is via National Route 40 and then Provincial Route 37. There are about 90 km of gravel. Road conditions change with rain and snow: check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Road conditions (Vialidad).

There are no buses to the park. According to the National Parks Administration, the Taqsa bus line reaches Gobernador Gregores, with connections toward Perito Moreno (north) and Piedra Buena (south). Schedules and fares: check with the company. From Gregores you can continue in your own vehicle or with local transporters authorized by the National Parks Administration. The official page lists Eduardo Lada and Transporte “Don Pepe”; the list and fares change: check before you travel at National Parks Administration – PN Perito Moreno: hours and how to get there.

The continuation of Route 41 between Lago Posadas and the park is a high-mountain road: in our Lago Posadas guide we describe it, according to the press, as suitable for 4x4 only in season. Before you take it, check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Road conditions (Vialidad).

A bit of history

The area was created in 1937 (decree of May 11, according to Wikipedia) and is named after Francisco P. Moreno, a pioneer of protected areas in Argentina. The park’s current boundaries were set by law 19.292. In 2013 Estancia El Rincón was added, some 15,000 hectares donated through Conservation Land Trust / Douglas Tompkins, incorporating part of the Río Lácteo valley (Wikipedia records it as the El Rincón wilderness reserve, 15,175 ha, adjacent to the park).

The National Parks Administration mentions archaeological evidence of about 10,000 years of human presence in the area.

What to do in Perito Moreno National Park

The trail network exceeds 100 km, according to the National Parks Administration and Argentina.travel. There are walks of a couple of hours and multi-day treks. The distance, difficulty and duration figures below are from the official activities page.

Belgrano Peninsula Circuit

It is the best-known circuit. The parking lot and trailhead are 10 km from the Onelli information office. There are two variants that share the start and finish:

  • Short circuit: 9.6 km, moderate difficulty, 3 to 4 hours. It crosses the center of the peninsula.
  • Long circuit: 16.8 km, high difficulty, 5 to 6 hours. It follows the west and south shores.

On the peninsula there are four refuges: Caleta Huala (800 m from the start), Archipiélago (2.9 km), Dos Bahías (5.7 km) and Playa Quetro (7.8 km).

Azara Circuit

It is the long trek: 52 km, high difficulty, 4 to 5 days. It starts near Mirador Lago Belgrano and passes the René Negro, Tucúquere, La Angostura and Azara refuges, and the El Arroyo camping area.

Río Lácteo Valley and Laguna de los Témpanos

This is the Cerro San Lorenzo sector, on the former Estancia El Rincón lands. According to the National Parks Administration, the circuit totals 17 km (high difficulty, 2 to 3 days): 800 m to the Gilberto refuge, 11.5 km to the Doug and Kris Tompkins refuge, and 5.2 km more to Laguna de los Témpanos, which often holds ice blocks from the Lácteo glacier.

Day hikes

  • Mirador Lago Belgrano: 8 km, moderate difficulty, 2 to 3 hours.
  • Cerro León: about 3 km of ascent; on clear days it overlooks much of the park. In strong wind, the National Parks Administration advises against going up.
  • Lago Burmeister: 600 m, with a steep slope at the end; watch the wind.
  • Mirador Lago Volcán: 600 m one way, easy.
  • Historic footbridge: 500 m (plus 400 m to the Río Volcán beach), easy; it recalls cattle drives from Estancia El Rincón.
  • A landscape with history: easy self-guided trail from the Onelli Operations Center, with rock art and interpretive signs. Some stretches are cordoned off because of rockfall risk and to protect the paintings.

Wildlife

The huemul is the emblematic species, but sightings are hard. You are more likely to see guanacos, foxes, rheas and condors. The National Parks Administration records 120 bird species. The park and the San Lorenzo Provincial Reserve form an Important Bird Area (IBA). There is a bird observatory at Lagunas del Mié, about 8 km from the Onelli Operations Center, on the way to Mirador Lago Belgrano.

Where to stay

According to the National Parks Administration, park admission is free, but use of refuges, domes and motorhome areas is paid and booked online. Refuge and dome use is shared. Capacities published by the National Parks Administration:

  • Caleta Huala, Archipiélago, Dos Bahías, La Angostura, Azara and Gilberto: 6 people each (in most cases, 1 night).
  • Playa Quetro and René Negro: 3 people (1 night).
  • Tucúquere: 3 people in the refuge, plus 2 domes for 3 people (up to 2 non-consecutive nights according to the official sheet).
  • Doug and Kris Tompkins: 3 people in the refuge, 3 domes for 4 people and 8 tent pitches (up to 2 consecutive nights).
  • Several sites have pitches for tents of up to 4 people.
  • Parking for 3 motorhomes at the Peninsula, Lago Burmeister and Río Lácteo Valley (up to 2 nights in most cases).

The option with a roof and services inside the area is Hostería Estancia La Oriental: 11 beds in the main house, 8 in the cabin and 8 in dorms, according to the National Parks Administration. Opening dates, rates and contact: check before you travel.

How to book

The National Parks Administration says to book by email at reservasperitomoreno@bancodebosques.org or through the online booking system (trekkingperitomoreno.com), which also has the trail map. After booking, you must complete the mountain-activity registration, which is mandatory. Deadlines and availability: check before you travel at National Parks Administration – PN Perito Moreno: plan your visit.

When to go

The park is open from October 1 to April 30. Dates may change with access and internal road conditions. In winter it stays closed: the National Parks Administration says staff work on maintenance, conservation and monitoring. In early spring (October–November) some trails or gravel stretches may still be difficult: ask at headquarters or the Operations Center before you leave.

In summer it can still snow and the wind is strong. For long trails, December to March gives you more daylight. These are our suggestions, not official climate data.

How many days to stay

  • 1 day: one or two short walks (viewpoints, Cerro León or the short peninsula circuit) leaving and returning to Gregores: it is a long day because of the 220 km each way.
  • 2 or 3 days: Belgrano Peninsula or the Río Lácteo valley sleeping in a refuge or dome.
  • 4 to 6 days: Azara circuit (4 to 5 days of walking) plus the trip from Gregores.

These are our suggestions. Argentina.travel also presents the park as a multi-day destination: check before you travel at Argentina.travel – Perito Moreno National Park.

Travel tips

  • Mandatory registration: register your visit at the Clemente Onelli Operations Center / “Ceferino Fonzo” information office, 9 am to 7 pm. You can also register online beforehand, according to the fees page.
  • Arrive in daylight at the reserved campsite or refuge.
  • No store: the park does not sell food. Bring all the food for your stay.
  • Fuel: fill up in Gobernador Gregores or Bajo Caracoles before you enter. Turismo Ruta 40 lists them as fuel stops on or near Route 40; in small settlements stock sometimes runs out.
  • Roads: check the state of RN 40 and RP 37 before you leave: check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Road conditions (Vialidad).
  • Gear: windbreaker, gloves, sunglasses, sunscreen and trekking shoes. On the summit of Cerro León and at Burmeister, wind is the main risk.
  • Cell coverage: we found no official coverage map inside the park. Expect no signal and tell someone your plan before you enter.
  • Official contact: peritomoreno@apn.gob.ar and 02962-409358. Headquarters: Paseo 9 de Julio 610, Gobernador Gregores.
  • Future entrance fee: the general National Parks fee list (in force from June 1, 2026) already includes rates for Perito Moreno, but notes that charging at this park (and others) starts once access-control infrastructure is ready. As of November 6, 2026, the park page still says free admission. Confirm on the day you travel: check before you travel at National Parks Administration – PN Perito Moreno: fees.

Frequently asked questions

Is Perito Moreno National Park the same as the Perito Moreno Glacier?

No. The glacier is in Los Glaciares National Park, near El Calafate. Perito Moreno National Park is in northwestern Santa Cruz, about 220 km from Gobernador Gregores.

How much is the entrance fee?

According to the park’s official page, admission is free as of November 6, 2026. Use of refuges, domes and motorhome areas is paid. The general National Parks fee list already publishes future rates for this park, but notes that charging starts once access-control infrastructure is ready: confirm before you travel.

Can you go without a car?

There are no buses to the park. You can take Taqsa to Gobernador Gregores and from there hire a transporter authorized by the National Parks Administration.

Are the mountain huts free?

No. The National Parks Administration says use of refuges, domes and motorhome areas is paid and must be booked online or by email. Park admission, by contrast, is listed as free.

Is there cell phone coverage?

We found no official coverage information inside the park. Expect no signal and leave your itinerary with someone before you enter.
