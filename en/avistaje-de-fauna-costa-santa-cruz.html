Home › Articles › Wildlife watching on the Santa Cruz coast: penguins, Commerson’s dolphins and sea lions

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-11

The Santa Cruz coast is one of the best places in Argentina to see marine wildlife without the crowds. Between Puerto Deseado and Cabo Vírgenes there are penguin colonies, sea lion haul-outs and bays where Commerson’s dolphins swim right beside the boat.

The main season runs from September–October to March–April, depending on the place: those are the months when penguins come ashore to breed and boat trips operate. Commerson’s dolphins, on the other hand, can be seen year-round in San Julián Bay, according to InterPatagonia.

This guide brings together the four key spots for wildlife watching on the Santa Cruz coast, from north to south: what you can see at each, how to get there and when to go. To plan the whole trip, also see La costa por Ruta 3.

The four spots and how to plan the route

All four spots are on National Route 3 or a short detour from it. You can do them all in one trip or pick one depending on the species you are after.

What to see at each spot

Deseado estuary: dolphins, cormorants and penguins

The Deseado estuary is an old river bed flooded by the sea, protected as a Provincial Reserve. According to SiFAP, some 15 species of sea and shore birds nest there, including four cormorants, and it holds about 40% of Argentina’s red-legged cormorant population. It is explored by boat, on half-day or full-day trips depending on the operator.

Welcome Argentina describes a trip with Commerson’s dolphins around the boat, red-legged and rock cormorants on the cliffs, South American sea lions on Isla Larga and Magellanic penguins on Isla de los Pájaros, where some trips go ashore. The same source gives the season as late September to April; Darwin Expediciones lists its estuary trip from October to Easter, lasting about 2.5 hours.

Penguin Island: the southern rockhopper penguin

This is Puerto Deseado’s highlight. According to Visit Argentina, it is the only place on the Atlantic coast where you can see the southern rockhopper penguin, and National Parks says it is the only colony of the species that tourists can visit on the whole continent. It stands about 55 cm tall and has yellow plumes above its red eyes (Welcome Argentina). Los Vikingos mentions a breeding colony of more than 1,500 pairs, the northernmost of the species.

The island lies about 25 km from Puerto Deseado, in open sea, and the trip takes most of the day: Darwin Expediciones and Los Vikingos list about 6 hours. Besides rockhoppers there are Magellanic penguins, South American sea lions, southern elephant seals and birds such as skuas, petrels and cormorants, according to National Parks. The island has a lighthouse that, according to Welcome Argentina, has been working since 1903, next to the ruins of an old sea lion processing plant.

The island is part of the Isla Pingüino Interjurisdictional Marine Park and can only be reached by boat. According to National Parks, trips run from mid-October to April, depending on sea conditions. As of November 4, 2026, Darwin Expediciones lists its island trip from mid-October to Easter, and Los Vikingos from late October to late March, with a minimum of 8 people. Prices and departure times are not published: check with each operator or with the Puerto Deseado Tourism Office.

San Julián Bay: Commerson’s dolphins year-round

In Puerto San Julián the boat trip around the bay lasts an hour and a quarter on average, according to InterPatagonia. The stars are the Commerson’s dolphins, which leap and dart close to the boat; SiFAP records a population of at least 42 individuals in the bay.

The trip passes Isla Justicia, the islet linked to the 1520 mutiny against Magellan and today a gathering spot for terns, cormorants, oystercatchers and skuas. It then continues to Isla Cormorán, home to a colony of more than 37,000 pairs of Magellanic penguins according to SiFAP (InterPatagonia mentions more than 120,000 penguins). Penguins can be seen from October to April.

The municipal tourism website lists Pinocho Excursiones as the operator in its adventure and sports section. According to InterPatagonia, in January and February there are daily departures every two hours, and trips are cancelled in bad weather. Off-season schedules and prices are not published: check with the operator before you travel. More about the town in the Puerto San Julián guide.

Monte León National Park: penguin colony and sea lion rookery

The park protects 62,169 hectares of steppe and 36 km of coastline, according to the province. The entrance is at km 2400 of National Route 3, 35 km from Piedra Buena and 200 km from Río Gallegos, according to National Parks. What to see:

  • Penguin Colony Trail (Sendero Pingüinera): starts at km 15 of Provincial Route 63 and is a 2.5 km walk across the steppe, of moderate difficulty, to a viewpoint over one of Argentina’s largest Magellanic penguin colonies. National Parks mentions more than 40,000 pairs and Visit Argentina more than 60,000. From October to April it is not suitable for people with reduced mobility.
  • Cabeza del León and Sea Lion Trail: about 400 m, easy, to a viewpoint over a South American sea lion haul-out.
  • Isla Monte León viewpoint: 18.8 km along Provincial Route 63. From there you see the imperial cormorant colony, plus rock cormorants and gulls.

Penguin colony hours according to National Parks: October to April, 9 am to 5 pm; May to September, 10 am to 3 pm. Before heading out on the trails you must register at the Information Center, in the old ranch house. Inside the park you can only drive on Provincial Route 63 (19 km), at a maximum of 40 km/h. As of November 4, 2026, National Parks says entry is free. Sources disagree on staying overnight: Visit Argentina says camping and overnight stays are not allowed, while National Parks mentions a free camping area with no facilities, so confirm with the park office. Trails, services and fees in detail: Qué hacer en Piedra Buena y el Parque Nacional Monte León.

Cabo Vírgenes: a huge colony at the southern tip

At the southern tip of the continent, the Cabo Vírgenes Provincial Reserve protects one of the largest Magellanic penguin colonies in the country. SiFAP speaks of about 150,000 pairs and describes it as the second-largest penguin colony in South America; C5N, of more than 260,000 birds. The reserve was created in 1986 (Provincial Decree 1,806/86) and, according to C5N, lies within the Monte Dinero ranch. The penguins are viewed from a 1,500 m self-guided trail.

From Río Gallegos, take National Route 3 south for about 16 km and then Provincial Route 1, with 123 km of dirt road: 139 km in total, according to SiFAP. There is no fuel and there are no services on the way, according to Welcome Argentina: set off with a full tank. SiFAP says it can be visited year-round, though in winter only by 4x4. Before you go, check the state of Provincial Route 1 in the provincial roads agency’s daily report.

The Cabo Vírgenes lighthouse dates from 1904 and is 26.5 m tall, and in season the “Al fin y al Cabo” tea house is open, according to C5N. We found no official information on an entrance fee to the reserve or on the tea house’s opening dates: check with the Río Gallegos Tourism Office. When to go

  • September: the first Magellanic penguins arrive at Cabo Vírgenes (SiFAP). Some trips start in the Deseado estuary: Los Vikingos lists its season from mid-September.
  • Mid-October to April: Penguin Island season according to National Parks, and penguins at San Julián and Monte León. The best time to combine everything.
  • November and December: chicks hatch. At Cabo Vírgenes, a study published in El Hornero recorded egg-laying in October and hatching between November 13 and early December. We found no official dates for each colony.
  • January and February: in San Julián there are daily departures every 2 hours, according to InterPatagonia.
  • Year-round: Commerson’s dolphins in San Julián Bay (InterPatagonia).
  • May to September: not penguin season, and the Monte León penguin colony opens from 10 am to 3 pm. Puerto Deseado operators do not list winter trips; for San Julián, check with the operator what is running.

How many days to stay

To visit all four spots without rushing, allow 6 to 8 days, including the drives along National Route 3. Visit Argentina recommends 2 to 4 days for Monte León alone and 2 to 3 for Puerto Deseado. For Penguin Island, set aside a full day in Puerto Deseado, plus a spare day in case wind cancels the trip. The San Julián boat trip takes half a day, and Cabo Vírgenes is a full-day trip from Río Gallegos. These are our suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Wind: it is strong all year. Bring a windbreaker, hat and gloves, even in summer.
  • Boat trips: they depend on the weather. Confirm with the operator the day before and build spare days into your itinerary.
  • Keep your distance from wildlife: do not touch or chase the penguins and stay on the trails, because you could step on nests. Penguins peck if they feel threatened, and on Penguin Island skuas swoop low to defend their nests.
  • Tides at Monte León: the range between low and high tide is large. Check the Naval Hydrographic Service tide tables or ask at the Information Center before walking on the beach.
  • Fuel: fill up in every town. There are no services between Río Gallegos and Cabo Vírgenes.

Frequently asked questions

Where can you see the southern rockhopper penguin in Santa Cruz?

On Penguin Island (Isla Pingüino), off Puerto Deseado, only on a guided boat trip. According to National Parks, trips run from mid-October to April, depending on sea conditions.

When can you see Commerson’s dolphins?

In San Julián Bay they can be seen year-round, according to InterPatagonia. In the Deseado estuary, boat trips run from September–October to April, depending on the operator.

Can you visit Monte León without a tour?

Yes. You can drive yourself along National Route 3 to km 2400, register at the Information Center and walk the trails on your own. As of November 4, 2026, entry is free according to National Parks.

Which is the largest penguin colony on the Santa Cruz coast?

Cabo Vírgenes: SiFAP puts it at around 150,000 pairs of Magellanic penguins. It is 139 km from Río Gallegos, largely on gravel and with no services along the way.

When do penguin chicks hatch?

At Cabo Vírgenes, a study published in El Hornero recorded hatching between November 13 and early December. Dates vary by colony and year.
