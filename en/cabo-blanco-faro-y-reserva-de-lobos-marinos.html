Home › Articles › Cabo Blanco: how to get to the lighthouse and the sea lion reserve in Santa Cruz

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-14

Cabo Blanco is a rocky headland at the southern end of the Gulf of San Jorge, on the northern coast of Santa Cruz. It has a red-brick lighthouse more than a hundred years old, a reserve for South American sea lions and fur seals, and the ruins of a salt-mining village that emptied out in the 1940s.

There is no hotel or shop, and according to Wikipedia there is no drinking water, power grid or mobile signal either. You get there on gravel from Puerto Deseado and come back the same way. That is why it is one of the most unusual trips on the Santa Cruz coast: you reach a place where all you hear is the wind, the sea and the sea lions.

Where is Cabo Blanco

The cape is in the Deseado department, 88 km north of Puerto Deseado. It is a set of rocky outcrops joined to the mainland by a tombolo, a strip of low land with salty soils and a bay on each side. According to Wikipedia, the middle headland is the one called Cabo Blanco; the northern one is Pan de Azúcar and the southern one, Tres Isletas.

The base town is Puerto Deseado: that is where you will find lodging, fuel, tour agencies and the last places to buy food. See Things to do in Puerto Deseado.

How to get to Cabo Blanco

According to SiFAP, from Puerto Deseado you take paved route 281 to km 24. There you turn right onto Provincial Route 14 and then Provincial Route 91, both gravel. The total is 88 km.

A few things to keep in mind about the road:

  • According to Wikipedia, the gravel road is regularly maintained, but it is affected by the local weather.
  • In the last few kilometres it turns into a clay track that is a challenge for regular cars. If you can, go in a pickup or 4x4.
  • Drive slowly on gravel: it protects your windshield and tires.
  • It is a dead-end road: you come back the same way.
  • SiFAP asks visitors to check road conditions before setting out. Road conditions: check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Estado de rutas (Vialidad).

If you do not have a vehicle, the option is to book the trip with an agency in Puerto Deseado. Welcome Argentina and InterPatagonia describe it as a half-day or full-day outing, depending on the activity. Prices and departures: ask the agencies or Puerto Deseado Tourism.

A bit of history

According to Wikipedia, the Tehuelche people called the place Yenk Aike, which translates as “lookout of the sea”. Bolas, fish hooks, bone harpoons and arrowheads have been found in the area, a sign that the Indigenous peoples did not only hunt: they also fished.

The current name comes from Magellan’s 1520 expedition: the headlands were covered in white guano and that is how they were recorded on nautical charts. SiFAP explains that even today, seen from the sea, the rock walls look white because of the cormorants’ guano.

In the early 20th century a 900-hectare salt flat inland was mined. The salt was used to preserve meat and hides at the height of the wool era. A small 60 cm gauge railway, about 5.6 km long, carried the salt to a pier on the cape. A village grew around it with a post office, police station, justice of the peace, inns and a school.

The salt works closed around 1930 when, among other reasons, meat refrigeration made salt unprofitable. The village gradually emptied, and the post office, the last thing still running, closed on 17 June 1974. Today only a few buildings and a restored cemetery remain (data from Wikipedia).

Things to do in Cabo Blanco

See the sea lion colony

This is the main reason for the trip. South American sea lions and fur seals rest on the rocks and islets off the cape; according to the wildlife ranger quoted by InterPatagonia, it is one of the largest colonies in the country. They were hunted during the 18th and 19th centuries for their skins and blubber, almost to extinction, and protection allowed them to slowly recover.

They have been protected since 1937. The province created the Strict Nature Reserve in 1977 (Decree 1,561/77), and according to SiFAP its goal is to protect the only breeding area of the South American fur seal. From the headland, a short walk takes you to a rocky balcony above the colonies. Watch from above, without climbing down onto the rocks or approaching the animals.

Birds and other wildlife

SiFAP mentions nests of red-legged and rock cormorants and a large colony of imperial cormorants, as well as gulls, blackish oystercatchers, black-chested buzzard-eagles and rheas. Along the shore there are beds of mussels, ribbed mussels and limpets. According to Wikipedia, Magellanic penguins sometimes appear, although there are no breeding colonies nearby. With luck, you can spot Peale’s dolphins, Commerson’s dolphins, orcas or southern right whales from the coast.

To combine it with other spots on the coast: Wildlife watching on the Santa Cruz coast: penguins, Commerson’s dolphins and sea lions.

See the Cabo Blanco lighthouse

The Navy lighthouse was completed around 1917. According to Wikipedia, the materials were unloaded at the salt pier and carried by the railway and an 80 m cableway; 110,000 trapezoidal bricks were used for the tower. It has run on solar panels since 1993. According to SiFAP, its light stands about 87 m above sea level, with a range of 14 nautical miles.

SiFAP, Welcome Argentina and InterPatagonia say you climb almost 100 steps to the top. However, a 2024 Tiempo Argentino article says the lighthouse can no longer be visited as it could about ten years ago. Before going with that plan, ask Puerto Deseado Tourism whether you can climb it today or only see it from outside.

Explore the village ruins and the salt flat

On the way back you cross the salt flats, a white, flat landscape that contrasts with the plateau. Near the lighthouse there are remains of the old village, railway embankments and the cemetery. Respect the ruins: do not take bricks or pieces, and do not pick up archaeological material.

When to go

According to SiFAP, the reserve is open all year, but because of the access road the recommended season is October to April. The climate series cited by Wikipedia (1951–1970) gives an average temperature of 14.3 °C in January and 4.8 °C in July.

Even in summer, the wind rules. Avoid going if rain or storms are forecast: mud on the final track is the main risk of the road. More about the local climate: Puerto Deseado weather: when to go.

How much time to spend

Cabo Blanco is visited as a half-day or full-day trip from Puerto Deseado. Ideally, give it a whole day, with time to walk and watch wildlife without rushing.

If you are planning a trip to the area, spend 2 or 3 days in Puerto Deseado to combine it with the Deseado estuary and Penguin Island and Bosques Petrificados de Jaramillo National Park.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: leave Puerto Deseado with a full tank.
  • Water and food: there is no drinking water or shops at the cape. Bring everything you will need.
  • No signal: there is no mobile coverage. Tell someone your route and your expected return time.
  • Breakdowns: check your spare tire, jack and tools before you leave.
  • Trash: take back everything you bring. It is a strict nature reserve.
  • Wildlife: keep your distance from sea lions and birds, and do not let dogs run loose.
  • Gear: binoculars, a windbreaker and sunglasses, even in summer.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Cabo Blanco from Puerto Deseado?

88 km according to SiFAP: 24 km of paved road on route 281 and the rest on gravel along provincial routes 14 and 91.

Can you reach Cabo Blanco in a regular car?

The gravel road is maintained, but the last few kilometres are a clay track that is tough on regular cars. If you can, go in a pickup or 4x4, and skip it if it rains.

Can you climb the Cabo Blanco lighthouse?

Travel guides mention almost 100 steps to the top, but a 2024 article says it can no longer be visited as before. Check with Puerto Deseado Tourism before you go.

Is there an entrance fee?

We could not find a published fee. Ask the Consejo Agrario Provincial, which manages the reserve, or Puerto Deseado Tourism.

Can you camp at Cabo Blanco?

SiFAP lists camping among the reserve’s activities. Since it is a strict nature reserve, confirm the current rules with the Consejo Agrario Provincial before you go.
