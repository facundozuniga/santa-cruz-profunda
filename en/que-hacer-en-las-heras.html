Home › Articles › Things to do in Las Heras, Santa Cruz: the steppe town born at the end of the line

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-31

Las Heras is a steppe town in northern Santa Cruz. It has no glaciers or famous mountains. What it has is a very concrete story: it was born at kilometre 283 of a railway that left Puerto Deseado meaning to reach the Andes, and never got any further. The general overview is at Las Heras.

Today it is an oil town of about 24,700 people (24,731 in the 2022 census, the fourth largest in the province according to Wikipedia). Many travellers heading along Provincial Route 43 to Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos or the Cueva de las Manos drive straight through. This guide is for those who want to stop: what to do in Las Heras in half a day or a full day, how to get there, when to go and what to keep in mind in an area of long distances and constant wind.

Where it is and how to get there

Las Heras lies in the Deseado department, about 330 m above sea level. Together with Caleta Olivia and Pico Truncado it forms a triangle of nearby towns that is home to much of the population of the north of the province. The province gives Provincial Route 43 as the access road: it runs through town and links it with Pico Truncado to the east and Perito Moreno to the west.

Sources do not agree to the kilometre. Wikipedia gives 80 km to Pico Truncado, 134 to Caleta Olivia and 214 to Comodoro. The Provincial Roads Department report adds up 83 km of paved road to Pico Truncado (60 to Koluel Kayke and 23 more) and 166 km to Perito Moreno (91 to El Pluma and 75 more); Rutas Patagónicas puts it at 169 km and about 2 h 17 min. From Perito Moreno to Los Antiguos it is another 58 km. Treat them as approximate and check the route on an up-to-date map.

Condition of Route 43

According to the Provincial Roads Department report, the whole stretch of RP 43 between Pico Truncado, Las Heras and Perito Moreno is paved, in good-to-fair condition. In winter there can be ice, snow and closures: in late July 2026, during heavy snowfall, the Las Heras–Perito Moreno stretch had restricted traffic and a temporary closure to clear the road, and the official statement reminded drivers that carrying snow chains is mandatory (Provision 002/18). For the day's conditions, check before you travel at Vialidad Provincial de Santa Cruz (AGVP) – Parte diario de rutas, distrito Las Heras.

By bus and by plane

The “Punta Rieles” bus terminal is at Ramos Mejía and Ameghino, and the Tourist Office works in the same building. The municipal website lists the companies with ticket offices at the terminal; frequencies and timetables change, so check before you travel at the terminal or the Tourist Office. We found no scheduled flights to Las Heras: if you are flying, the option with scheduled flights is Comodoro Rivadavia (Chubut), and from there you continue by road.

A bit of history: from “Punta Rieles” to oil town

Before the town existed, the spot was a meeting point for ranchers who carted their wool to Caleta Olivia, Mazaredo and Puerto Deseado. In 1908 the 600,000-hectare General Las Heras Pastoral Colony was created, according to the history published by the municipality.

In 1909 construction of the Patagonian Railway began from Puerto Deseado; the original plan was to reach Nahuel Huapi. In 1914 track-laying stopped at kilometre 283, and the station and the place became known as “Punta Rieles” (end of the line). On 6 October that year a resolution of the Ministry of Public Works named the station Colonia Las Heras: since a 2017 ordinance, that is the date the town celebrates as its anniversary. The town was officially established by decree of President Hipólito Yrigoyen on 11 July 1921.

  • Other names: before the official one, the place was known as “Rastros de Avestruz”, “Punta Rieles”, “Parada 283” and “Colonia Gral. Las Heras”.
  • 1915: the train starts commercial service along the whole line, with 14 stations.
  • 29 December 1934: oil is struck at a depth of 344 m in a test well in town.
  • 1960: the municipality takes this date as the start of commercially exploitable oil in the area; from the 1970s the oil industry becomes the engine of the economy.
  • 15 January 1978: the Puerto Deseado–Las Heras line is closed.
  • 1995: a fire destroys the historic station; remains and other railway buildings survive.

Today, according to Wikipedia, the economy depends almost entirely on oil and gas.

Things to do in Las Heras

Walk the railway circuit

This is the historic heart of town. At Ramos Mejía and Sarmiento you can see the remains of Colonia Las Heras station, built between 1909 and 1912 mostly of corrugated iron, along with some railway buildings still standing. Nearby is the water tower which, according to the municipality, supplied the steam trains from 1912 until the line closed in 1978.

The Railway Promenade (Paseo Ferroviario), at Paulina Argüello and Av. Argentina, opened in October 2022: it has a scale replica of a locomotive, a restored original carriage, an old clock and a train-themed playground. At the Rotonda del Ferroviario (Av. Isolina Picticar and Paulina Argüello) there is a sculpture of a railway worker, declared historic and cultural heritage. And at Ramos Mejía and O'Higgins a piece of rail from 1912 survives, one of those that marked out the town's first three blocks.

Visit the History Museum

The Las Heras History Museum was created on 11 July 2012 and covers the history of the community. We could not find its address, opening hours or whether it charges admission on official websites: ask at the Tourist Office or the municipal Culture Department before you go.

Stroll past the stone buildings

On San Martín street, between Antiguos Pobladores and Sarmiento, stand the first Post Office (opened in 1938) and the old Civil Registry, both built with stone from local quarries. The old post office building now houses the Domingo Faustino Sarmiento Public Library, founded in 1931, with around 13,000 volumes.

School No. 3 Benjamín Zorrilla, on Gobernador Gregores between Guido Spano and O'Higgins, is the oldest educational institution in town; its own building opened in 1928. On Av. Perito Moreno, between Guido Spano and O'Higgins, there is a vintage fuel pump restored in 2021. On the corner of Av. Perito Moreno and Sarmiento stands the building of Casa “El Sol”, one of the first shops, which opened in 1923 and closed in the 2010s.

Squares and promenades

  • Plaza General San Martín: remodelled and reopened in 2022. Inside is the first public swimming pool, from 1951, declared historic heritage and now turned into a winter garden.
  • Plaza del Bicentenario: opened in 2022 at Av. 9 de Julio and Av. 25 de Mayo, with an 11-metre illuminated pyramid.
  • Plazoleta Nuestro Mate: at Av. Perito Moreno and Guido Spano. It has a solar heater that provides up to 1,000 litres a day of free water at the right temperature for mate, a charging station, free wifi and a 3-metre mate gourd. A handy stop if you are driving.
  • Avenida Isolina Picticar: the municipality bills it as the “First Sustainable Avenue in Argentina”, with solar lighting and a bike lane.

Municipal campground and historic cart

Opposite the Municipal Campground, on Av. Paulina Argüello, there is a historic cart recalling the transport of the early days. Once a year the campground hosts the motorcycle rally of Los Tehuelches Moto Club, and the Steppe Festival is held next door. For the rally date and campground facilities, ask at the Tourist Office.

The National Patagonian Steppe Festival

It started in 2017 and is held every March, because autumn is the season that best shows off the landscape. It has been a National Festival by law since 2019. It brings together craftspeople, local institutions and national, regional and local artists. The pandemic interrupted it and it returned in 2022. For the next edition, check before you travel at Turismo Las Heras – Fiesta Nacional de la Estepa Patagónica.

Nature around town

The province describes Las Heras as surrounded by valleys and canyons, with remains of petrified trees in the area, and suggests sport fishing, trekking and birdwatching. We found no official information on access or distances to those spots: ask at the Tourist Office before heading out.

When to go

The climate is semi-arid steppe: little rain, strong wind and big temperature swings. According to the Weatherbase data cited by Wikipedia, the annual mean temperature is around 10 °C, with a mean of 17 °C in January and −1 °C in July, and about 150 mm of rain a year.

From November to March the days are long and it is easier to fit the stop into the rest of your trip; March adds the Steppe Festival. In winter RP 43 can have ice and snow, with traffic restrictions. These are our suggestions: check before you travel at Vialidad Provincial de Santa Cruz (AGVP) – Parte diario de rutas, distrito Las Heras.

How many days to stay

Half a day is enough for the town circuit. If you want to see the museum, walk past the historic buildings and have a relaxed meal, allow a full day. It works very well as a stop on the way between the coast (Caleta Olivia, Puerto Deseado) and the Andes (Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos, Cueva de las Manos).

Travel tips

  • Fill up here. The municipal tourism website lists three service stations: Vermaz (Av. Simón Bolívar and Pedemonte, listed as “under refurbishment”), Axion (Av. Perito Moreno and Rivadavia) and El Solitario (RP 43 and Av. 9 de Julio). It is about 166 km of steppe to Perito Moreno and we found no official information about fuel stops on the way: leave with a full tank.
  • Accommodation: the municipal tourism website has a list of places to stay and eat. Check availability before you travel.
  • Drive for the wind. Slow down on open stretches and watch out for guanacos and rheas on the road.
  • Drop by the Tourist Office. It is at the “Punta Rieles” terminal (Ramos Mejía and Ameghino). The municipal website gives opening hours of 8 am to 8 pm, phone 297-4789769 and email turismomlhsc@gmail.com; the provincial website gives Monday to Friday, 8 am to 8 pm, and +54 297 497-4300; check before you travel at Turismo Las Heras (Municipalidad) – Inicio: alojamiento, gastronomía y Oficina de Turismo.

To continue your trip: Pico Truncado, Koluel Kayke, Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos and Petrified Forests of Jaramillo.

Frequently asked questions

Is Las Heras worth a stop?

Yes, if you are interested in Patagonian history. It is one of the best places to understand the Puerto Deseado railway and the shift from a wool economy to an oil economy. For mountain scenery, carry on to Perito Moreno and Los Antiguos.

When is the Las Heras anniversary?

On 6 October. On that day in 1914 the station at kilometre 283 was named Colonia Las Heras; a 2017 municipal ordinance adopted that date as the town's anniversary.

Will the train come back to Las Heras?

There is a project under way. In 2024 the province and Trenes Argentinos announced the recovery of the Puerto Deseado–Las Heras branch line, some 283 km long. In October 2026 the province declared the first stage finished: 21 km of track refurbished between Jaramillo and Fitz Roy. There is still no official date for a service to Las Heras.

How do I get to Las Heras?

On Provincial Route 43, which is paved: about 80 km from Pico Truncado and about 166 km from Perito Moreno. From Caleta Olivia it is between 134 and 142 km. If you are flying, the option with scheduled flights is Comodoro Rivadavia, about 214 km away.

Where can I get fuel?

In town: the municipal tourism website lists the Axion, El Solitario and Vermaz service stations (the last one under refurbishment). Leave for Perito Moreno with a full tank, because we found no official information about fuel stops along the way.
