Home › Articles › Penguin Island: how to see the southern rockhopper penguin from Puerto Deseado

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-21

Off Puerto Deseado lies a rocky island with an old lighthouse, the ruins of a processing plant and something that, according to National Parks, you can't visit anywhere else on the continent: a colony of southern rockhopper penguins.

It's the red-eyed penguin with yellow eyebrows that hops between the rocks. The island is also home to Magellanic penguins, sea lions, elephant seals and many South Atlantic birds. It can only be visited on a guided boat excursion: here's how to get there, when to go and what to know before booking.

Where Penguin Island is

The island gives its name to the Isla Pingüino Interjurisdictional Marine Park, which protects more than 159,000 hectares of the Argentine Sea, with several islands and islets. According to National Parks, it's about 25 km by boat south of the port of Puerto Deseado.

The park headquarters are at San Martín 1137, Puerto Deseado, open Monday to Friday from 8 am to 3 pm, phone (0297) 487-0220.

Puerto Deseado is the only departure point for the excursions. These are the National Parks figures:

  • By plane: Comodoro Rivadavia and Río Gallegos have daily flights from Buenos Aires. National Parks suggests checking schedules with Aerolíneas Argentinas and LADE.

A bit of history

The island's lighthouse opened in 1903 and its light can still be seen at night from town, according to National Parks. Tiempo Argentino reports that it is about 22 m tall and first ran on kerosene; today it runs on solar panels.

Near the lighthouse are the remains of an old plant that rendered sea lion blubber. Walking among the ruins helps you understand why the area is protected today.

What to do on Penguin Island

The boat trip

The excursion leaves the port and heads into open sea. Welcome Argentina describes a route past Isla de los Pájaros where Commerson's dolphins and seabirds are often seen. The first part can be rough: guides ask you to stay seated until they say otherwise.

The sea and the wind decide whether the boat goes out. National Parks recommends keeping several days free to make sure you get the trip.

The trail between colonies

On the island you walk a 1.5 km route past colonies of Magellanic and rockhopper penguins, and areas where skuas, kelp and dolphin gulls, steamer ducks and sheldgeese nest.

It's the only area open to visitors and is always walked with a guide. National Parks rates it easy to moderate: it starts flat and open, and gets harder near the rockhopper colony, with narrow rocky stretches and gullies.

The southern rockhopper penguin

It's small: according to Tiempo Argentino it stands 50 to 65 cm tall and weighs up to about 2.5 kg. It has red eyes, a black crest and yellow feathers that sprout like eyebrows. Unlike the Magellanic penguin, it moves by hopping with its feet together.

In 2018, National Parks reported about 1,100 pairs of rockhoppers in the park, a threatened species, and 60,000 pairs of Magellanic penguins in 13 colonies. More recent figures are higher: in 2024 the park superintendent spoke of around 1,600 breeding pairs (Deseado Noticias), and a La Nación article from December 2025 mentions 1,500.

Sea lions and elephant seals

The island has a haul-out of South American sea lions and South American fur seals, sometimes joined by an elephant seal. You see them up close, but always from the trail.

Excursions: duration and operators

According to National Parks, the excursion takes about 6 hours. Other sources describe it as a half-day or full-day trip, so confirm the itinerary when you book.

National Parks lists three licensed boat operators:

  • Darwin Expediciones: (0297) 624-7554
  • Los Vikingos: (0297) 624-4283
  • Puerto Penacho: (0297) 492-1607

Prices, departure days and what each excursion includes (snacks, warm gear) change by season: check directly with each operator. Entry to the park is free according to the official page; what you pay for is the excursion.

When to go

National Parks says the excursions run from mid-October to April, weather permitting. According to what the park superintendent told Deseado Noticias, this is the rockhoppers' calendar:

  • October: they arrive on the island and form pairs.
  • November: nesting activity begins.
  • December: small chicks can already be seen.
  • January to March: the chicks are bigger and can be seen outside the nest.
  • April: the rockhoppers leave the island and spend the winter at sea.

Wind rules all year round. To understand how it affects departures, see Weather in Puerto Deseado: when to go.

How many days to stay

Book at least two nights in Puerto Deseado: one day for the island and a spare one in case the trip is cancelled. With three or four days you can add the Deseado estuary boat trip, Cabo Blanco and the town. More ideas in Things to do in Puerto Deseado.

Travel tips

  • Layers: a windproof, waterproof jacket, hat and gloves, even in summer. The wind is strong on the boat.
  • Sturdy shoes: the last stretch is over uneven rocks.
  • Binoculars: useful for watching nests without getting close.
  • Seasickness: if you're prone to it, check with your doctor beforehand.
  • Keep your distance from wildlife: stay on the trail, avoid sudden movements and follow the guide's instructions.
  • Skuas: they defend their nests and may dive low at you. Stay with the guide.
  • Book ahead: there are only three licensed operators.

Frequently asked questions

Can you visit Penguin Island on your own?

No. According to National Parks, the island can only be reached on the licensed boat excursions, always with a guide.

Is there anywhere else in Argentina to see rockhopper penguins?

There are colonies on Isla de los Estados and the Falkland Islands (Malvinas), but Penguin Island is the only one on the continent that tourists can visit, according to National Parks.

How long does the Penguin Island excursion take?

About 6 hours according to National Parks, including a 1.5 km walk on the island. Confirm the itinerary with the operator.

Is there a park entrance fee?

No: the official page states free access. What you pay for is the excursion; check the price with each operator.

What happens if the trip is cancelled because of wind?

Departures depend on the sea and the wind. Ask when booking whether they reschedule or refund, and keep spare days in Puerto Deseado.
