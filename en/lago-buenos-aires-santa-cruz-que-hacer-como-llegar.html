Home › Articles › Lake Buenos Aires in Santa Cruz: things to do, how to get there and where to fish

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-27

Lake Buenos Aires is the great body of water of northwestern Santa Cruz. Argentina shares it with Chile, where it is called General Carrera. Billiken cites the Tehuelche name Chelenko, which it translates as “stormy waters”, although a study published in the journal Magallania, cited by Wikipedia, questions whether that name actually referred to this lake. What nobody disputes is its temper: when the wind blows, the lake gets as rough as the sea.

On the Argentine side, the gateway is Los Antiguos, on the south shore, and the town of Perito Moreno, 58 km away. From there you can reach the waterfront, the viewpoints, the icy beaches, trout fishing and two scenic roads: RP 43 and RP 41. For everything else in town, see Things to do in Los Antiguos.

Where is Lake Buenos Aires?

The lake lies in the Lago Buenos Aires department, in the far northwest of the province, right against the Andes. The border with Chile is marked by the Jeinimeni River, which flows into the lake just west of Los Antiguos.

  • Area: Wikipedia and Billiken give 1,850 km² in total, about 880 km² of it on the Argentine side; Welcome Argentina and Río Negro speak of 2,240 km². All agree it is one of the largest lakes in South America, and according to Wikipedia it is the largest in Chile.
  • Maximum depth: 590 m, according to Wikipedia and Billiken.
  • Altitude: 217 m above sea level.
  • Origin: glacial. It drains to the Pacific Ocean through Lake Bertrand and the Baker River, in Chile.
  • Name: according to Billiken, it was named by explorer Carlos María Moyano, who reached its shores on 29 October 1880.

How to get to Lake Buenos Aires

The easiest way is on RP 43, a paved road linking Perito Moreno and Los Antiguos along the lake’s south shore. Perito Moreno sits on National Route 40, and the same RP 43 comes in from the Atlantic coast via Pico Truncado.

A bit of history

This was Tehuelche land. According to Río Negro, many chenques (burials covered with stones) have been found around Los Antiguos, and tradition says the Tehuelche came to this valley to spend their last days because of its microclimate.

Wikipedia notes that Argentine, Chilean and European settlers arrived between 1900 and 1925. The La Ascensión ranch was founded on the lakeshore (in 1911 according to La Nación; National Parks gives 1912), where more than 15,000 sheep were once shorn. The 1991 eruption of the Hudson volcano covered the area in ash and hit sheep farming hard; afterwards, Los Antiguos turned to soft fruit and cherries.

Things to do at Lake Buenos Aires

Walk the Los Antiguos waterfront

According to Río Negro, work on the waterfront began in 2009 and turned the town around: it used to face the valley. It has benches facing the lake, a roundabout, a park and the fisherman’s monument. It is the classic place to walk or cycle at sunset.

Climb the viewpoints

The Los Antiguos Tourism Office lists four viewpoints, with distances from the visitor information centre:

  • Mirador Uendeunk (900 m): right in the centre, on Avenida 11 de Julio. According to Río Negro, a stairway leads to the top, next to the Tehuelche Monument, with views over the whole town and the lake.
  • Mirador del Lago Buenos Aires (2.3 km): on the site of the old pier, where coal and firewood arrived from Chile. It has a wooden gallery; fishing is on one side and the beach on the other.
  • Mirador Río Jeinimeni (2.8 km): kilometre zero of Route 41. From here you see Lake Buenos Aires on one side and General Carrera on the other.
  • Mirador del Valle (3.5 km): the highest viewpoint in town, overlooking the farms and the mountain range.

Beaches and water sports

There is a beach by the waterfront, but the glacial water is very cold and, according to the local guide quoted by Río Negro, there are no lifeguards. Going in is more of a challenge than a swim.

Río Negro (2023) reports that windsurfing is done in the windier section and that kayaks and pedal boats are rented in the calmer area. Who is renting this season and when: ask at the tourism office.

Sport fishing

The lake is known for brown and rainbow trout; Welcome Argentina mentions fish of over 5 or 6 kg. The Los Antiguos Tourism Office adds perch (trucha criolla), brook trout and Sakura and Coho salmon in the lake and the Los Antiguos and Jeinimeni rivers. Fly fishing and spinning are the most common methods, and gear can be rented in town, according to Río Negro.

  • 2026/27 season: according to Annex I of the provincial regulations, Lake Buenos Aires is open year-round, with a limit of two fish per angler per day.
  • No-fishing zones: fishing is not allowed within 200 m of river sources and mouths, such as those of the Jeinimeni and Los Antiguos rivers.
  • Los Antiguos River: has its own rules: one fish per day until 31 March 2027 and, from 1 April, fly fishing only with mandatory catch and release.
  • Best times: August to November and March to April, according to the Los Antiguos Tourism Office.

You need the Santa Cruz fishing permit: how to get it step by step. Rules for the rest of the province in Sport fishing in Santa Cruz.

Birdwatching

Black-necked swans and ducks can be seen at the mouth of the Los Antiguos River, according to Welcome Argentina. In town, the Laguna de los Juncos Urban Nature Reserve shelters more than 60 bird species, including flamingos and herons, according to the Tourism Office. Higher up, on the Lake Buenos Aires plateau, lives the hooded grebe, a critically endangered bird endemic to Santa Cruz.

Portal La Ascensión (Patagonia National Park)

17 km from Los Antiguos on RP 43, the old ranch headquarters is now the northern gateway to Patagonia National Park. There is a visitor centre, a short 1.5 km trail to the lakeshore, others climbing to the plateau and a camping area with fire pits. According to National Parks, entry is free and in high season (1 October to Easter) the park is open for entry from 8 am to 8 pm. Full guide: Portal La Ascensión: how to get there and what to do.

Provincial Route 41 (Camino del Zeballos)

It starts at the Jeinimeni viewpoint and heads south to Lago Posadas along 155 km of gravel, following the Jeinimeni River and Mount Zeballos. According to the Los Antiguos Tourism Office, it climbs from 200 to 1,500 m above sea level at El Portezuelo and can become impassable after snow or heavy rain; La Nación (2021) says that, because of the altitude, it is driven between November and April. Fill up in Los Antiguos and check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Estado de rutas (Vialidad). More in Lago Posadas and Route 41.

Cross to Chile Chico

The Río Jeinimeni crossing links Los Antiguos with Chile Chico, across the lake. Argentina’s immigration office lists opening hours of 9 am to 8 pm, but Ruta0, using Gendarmería data, shows different customs hours and a mandatory snow-chains notice on the Chilean side. The crossing can also close due to weather. On the day you cross, check its status on the Ministerio de Seguridad page. Documents, car papers and the SAG declaration: Paso Jeinimeni: how to cross to Chile Chico.

When to go

  • October: cherry blossom season; the Tourism Office calls it the Month of the Cherry Blossom.
  • November: the Tourism Office’s calendar includes the Lake Buenos Aires Festival. For the exact date, ask at the tourism office.
  • Late December to February: cherry harvest, which according to Río Negro starts around 23 or 24 December. The 2027 National Cherry Festival has been announced for 8 to 10 January.
  • November to April: the usual window for Route 41.
  • August to November and March to April: the best fishing periods, according to the Tourism Office.

How many days to stay

  • 1 day: the waterfront, the viewpoints and an afternoon of fishing or kayaking.
  • 2 to 3 days: add Portal La Ascensión and a stretch of Route 41.
  • 4 days or more: combine with the Cave of the Hands, Cañadón Pinturas or the crossing to Chile Chico.

Travel tips

  • Wind: Welcome Argentina warns that the beaches can be calm while there are waves out on the lake. Don’t go out on the water without knowing the lake or without a guide.
  • Fuel: always leave with a full tank, especially if you are heading to Route 41.
  • Warm clothes: even in summer, wind and cold nights are common. Bring a windbreaker, a hat, sunglasses and sunscreen.
  • Cash: bring Argentine pesos and, if you cross, Chilean pesos.

Frequently asked questions

Can you swim in Lake Buenos Aires?

You can go in, but the glacial water is very cold and, according to the local guide, there are no lifeguards in Los Antiguos. Most people stay on the shore.

What is the best base for visiting the lake?

Los Antiguos, because it sits right on the shore. Perito Moreno is 58 km away on paved road and works well if you are continuing along Route 40.

Do I need a permit to fish?

Yes, the Santa Cruz provincial fishing permit. In the 2026/27 season the lake is open year-round, with a limit of two fish per day.

Are Lake Buenos Aires and Lake General Carrera the same lake?

Yes. It is one shared lake: on the Argentine side it is called Buenos Aires and on the Chilean side, General Carrera.
