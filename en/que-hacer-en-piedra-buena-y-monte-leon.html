Home › Articles › Things to do in Piedra Buena and Monte León National Park

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-25

Comandante Luis Piedra Buena is a small town on National Route 3, on the banks of the Santa Cruz River. Many people drive straight through, and that's a shame: it's the base for one of the best getaways on the Santa Cruz coast. The general overview is at Piedra Buena.

35 km to the south lies Monte León National Park, Argentina's first mainland marine national park: cliffs, shingle beaches, a sea lion colony and a Magellanic penguin colony you reach on foot across the steppe. This guide brings together what you need to combine the two: how to get there, what to see in town, what to do in the park, opening hours and the best time to go.

Where it is and how to get there

Piedra Buena is in the Corpen Aike department, on the left bank of the Santa Cruz River and on RN 3. According to the 2022 census it has 9,278 inhabitants. The park entrance is at km 2400 of the same route, at the former Estancia Monte León ranch house.

By car

The whole main route is along Route 3. Inside the park, Provincial Route 63 links RN 3 with the viewpoints, the trails and the beach: 19 km of gravel with a 40 km/h speed limit. Its condition depends on the weather: check before you travel at Parques Nacionales – Monte León: horarios y cómo llegar, or ask at the park office or the Operations Center before going in.

By plane and by bus

The nearest airports are Río Gallegos (200 km from the park) and El Calafate (455 km). From there you continue by bus to Piedra Buena or Puerto Santa Cruz, which have connections to almost the whole country. Bus companies, schedules and frequencies change with the season: check at the bus terminal before you travel. For the last stretch to the park you need a taxi, remís (private car service) or transfer.

By bike

You can ride in on the vehicle roads, within the park's opening hours. Keep in mind the strong, sudden winds of the steppe.

A bit of history

Luis Piedra Buena settled in 1859 on the island that today lies opposite the town. There he built his home and a trading post to trade with the Indigenous peoples, and he named the island Pavón after the battle of 1861. In 1868 his wife, Julia Dufour, came to live with him.

His pilot, Gregorio Ibáñez, settled on the north bank, and for a time the place was known as Paso Ibáñez. The development commission was formed in 1920, and in 1933 the settlement received its current name, made official on August 24, the sailor's birthday. In 1968 the spelling changed from “Piedrabuena” to “Piedra Buena”, although both forms are still used.

Monte León was also a sheep ranch. In 2000, Conservación Patagónica, Kristine Tompkins's organization, bought the land and transferred it to Fundación Vida Silvestre Argentina so it could be donated to National Parks. The park was created by Law 25,945, passed on October 20, 2004.

Things to do in Piedra Buena

Isla Pavón

It sits in the Santa Cruz River, between the main RN 3 bridge and the secondary bridge, very close to downtown. It's the town's classic outing: a tourist complex with camping areas and fire pits sheltered from the wind.

On the island there is a replica of Piedra Buena's house that works as a museum, with objects found among the remains of the original building and period photographs. At the entrance stands the Blakely cannon, from 1850, which was once mounted on Isla de los Estados (Staten Island) and was found in the undergrowth in 1944. For museum hours and admission, check with the Piedra Buena tourism office (Av. Gregorio Ibáñez 215) before you go.

Murals and Patoruzito

The streets have dozens of murals and sculptures about the region's history, plus life-size figures from the comic Patoruzito by Dante Quintero, with the only theme park dedicated to him.

The Santa Cruz River and steelhead fishing

Piedra Buena is the National Steelhead Trout Capital: a trout that is born in the river, grows in the sea and swims back upriver; according to the province it can reach 10 kg. It is fished with fly and spinning gear, and the river also has róbalo (Patagonian blenny) and pejerrey (silverside). Flow is highest from November to March and lowest in September.

The National Steelhead Trout Festival takes place in late March or early April: in 2025 it ran from April 4 to 6. The date of each edition is announced closer to the event; check with Turismo Santa Cruz or the local tourism office.

To fish you need the provincial permit, which you get online. The 2026/2027 season runs from October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027, and the Santa Cruz River is open all year, with a quota of one steelhead per season and mandatory catch-and-release for steelhead from March 15, 2027. The season permit for residents of Argentina costs $40,000 ARS and the daily one $15,000 ARS; retirees, people over 65, children up to 12 and people with disabilities get it free of charge. Rules for each water body, prices for non-residents and other categories: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (provincia) – Permisos de pesca, temporada 2026/2027.

You can also explore the river along its banks or by boat, and it's used for canoeing and other water sports.

Things to do in Monte León National Park

The park protects 62,169 hectares of steppe and 36 km of coastline on the Argentine Sea. Before any hike you must register at the Operations Center (km 2400 of RN 3): registration is mandatory because rain and wind can close roads and trails. Seeing every site takes a full day, and just driving in and out of the area, without stops, takes about 2 hours.

Penguin Colony Trail

It starts at km 15 of RP 63. It's a 2.5 km walk across the steppe, with interpretive signs, to a viewpoint over one of Argentina's largest Magellanic penguin colonies: according to National Parks, more than 40,000 pairs. It's of moderate difficulty and not suitable for people with reduced mobility.

Lion's Head and Sea Lion Trail

A trail of about 400 m over Cabeza del León hill leads to a viewpoint over a South American sea lion haul-out. It's easy and suitable for people with reduced mobility with assistance.

Lion's Head Viewpoint

At km 13.5 of RP 63 a 25 m path, about 10 minutes, leads to the view of the rock formation resembling a lying lion that gives the park its name. It served as a landmark for ships sailing along this coast.

Isla Monte León Viewpoint

At km 18.8 on RP 63. It looks out over the Isla Monte León Provincial Reserve, with a colony of imperial cormorants; you can also see rock shags and kelp and dolphin gulls.

Beach and intertidal zone

At the Juan Quiñonez recreation area, at the end of RP 63, there is 1 km of shingle beach, like the one in the photo. When the tide goes out the intertidal zone is exposed: anemones, starfish, small crustaceans and mollusks. During extraordinary tides you can walk to the foot of the island.

The water is deep and cold, and there are no lifeguards. The difference between low and high tide is large: check the tide times at the Information Center or the park office before heading down to the shore.

Fishing in Monte León

From January 1 to April 30, from 10 am to 6 pm, you can fish from the beach at the Juan Quiñonez recreation area: róbalo, pejerrey and palometa over 30 cm. You need the park's own permit, individual and free, which you get when you register.

Entry is currently free. Resolution 132/2026 has already set a fee for Monte León ($25,000 ARS general, $12,000 ARS Argentine nationals, $8,000 ARS provincial residents and $10,000 ARS students, per day), but it will only be charged once the park has its access-control system. Retirees and pensioners with an Argentine ID, children up to 6 and people with disabilities, among others, are exempt in parks that charge entry. For updates, check before you travel at Parques Nacionales – Monte León: tarifas.

When to go

In our view, the best time is October to April: opening hours are longer and the penguins are in the colony, since they come to nest and molt between October and April. Rain and strong wind can close roads and trails at any time of year. In winter the hours are shorter and it's worth checking the condition of RP 63 before you go.

How many days to stay

Two days are enough: one for the park and another for Isla Pavón, the town and the river. Visit Argentina suggests 2 to 4 days for Monte León; add more if you're coming to fish.

Travel tips

  • Fill up in Piedra Buena or Puerto Santa Cruz before going in: the distances inside the park are long.
  • Bring water, food and a windproof jacket. The park has no drinking water and no café, kiosk, restaurant or store; there are restrooms at the Operations Center and at the Juan Quiñonez recreation area.
  • Juan Quiñonez has fire pits with grills, but check the fire risk with the park before lighting a fire.
  • National Parks lists a free camping area with no services or showers. If you plan to stay overnight, confirm it with the park office first; otherwise there is lodging in Piedra Buena and Puerto Santa Cruz.
  • There's wifi only at the Information Center, and no public phone.
  • Keep your distance from wildlife and stay on the trails.
  • Check the tide table before walking along the coast.

Frequently asked questions

How far is it from Piedra Buena to Monte León?

About 35 km on RN 3 to km 2400, where the Operations Center is. From there you continue on RP 63 toward the coast.

Can you see penguins at Monte León all year round?

No. Magellanic penguins come to the colony to nest and molt between October and April.

How much does it cost to enter Monte León National Park?

Entry is currently free. National Parks has already set a fee that will be charged once the park has access control: check before you travel.

Do you need to register to enter Monte León?

Yes. Registration at the Operations Center, at km 2400 of RN 3, is mandatory before you head out on the trails.
