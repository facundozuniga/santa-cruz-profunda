Home › Articles › Viedma Glacier and Lake Viedma: how to get there, tours and tips from El Chaltén

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-24

The Viedma Glacier is the largest in Los Glaciares National Park: according to Wikipedia, it is bigger than Upsala, the runner-up, and also than Perito Moreno. But it is off the classic El Calafate circuit. You visit it from El Chaltén, 18 km from the port where the boats leave.

If you are going to El Chaltén for the Fitz Roy trails, the Viedma is a great plan for a windy or overcast day: the boat trip does not depend on seeing the mountain, and the ice front is impressive in any weather.

Where is the Viedma Glacier

The glacier is in northwestern Santa Cruz, in the northern sector of Los Glaciares National Park. It flows down from the Southern Patagonian Ice Field between the Huemul and Puntudo peaks and ends at the western tip of Lake Viedma. According to Wikipedia, it covers about 977 km² and is 70 km long, and its front is about 2.5 km wide.

The height of the front varies by source: InterPatagonia mentions walls more than 40 m above the lake, and Wikipedia says about 50 m.

Lake Viedma covers 1,088 km² and is about 80 km long, according to Wikipedia: it is the third largest in the country. It is fed by the glacier and the Río de las Vueltas, and drains through the Río La Leona into Lake Argentino.

How to get to the Viedma Glacier

The glacier front can only be reached by water. Tours leave from Puerto Bahía Túnel, on the lake, 18 km from El Chaltén. According to the El Chaltén portal, the first half of the road is paved and the second half is well-maintained gravel. The road ends at the shore, where you will find the port, the facilities of the operator Patagonia Aventura and a parking area.

You have three ways to get to the port:

  • Your own or a rental car: parking at the port is free, according to the tour information.
  • Taxi or remís from El Chaltén. Fare: ask in town before you go.
  • Operator transfer: it leaves at 10:00 from the office at Av. San Martín 520, in El Chaltén, and returns around 15:00. According to the El Chaltén portal it runs from November to March, and in November 2026 it listed a price of AR$ 30,000 per person.

A bit of history

According to Wikipedia, the Aonikenk called the lake Capar or Copar. Its current name comes from Antonio de Viedma, who in 1782 set out from the Floridablanca fort, near present-day Puerto San Julián, and travelled up the Santa Cruz River and its tributaries. In the late 19th century the area was also explored by Perito Moreno.

The most recent fact is scientific. In July 2022, CONICET reported that a team of its own researchers, the International Center for Earth Sciences and the University of Chile had measured a trench about 900 m deep in the lake, near the glacier front. With that figure, Lake Viedma is the deepest lake in the Americas and the fifth deepest in the world.

That measurement was possible because the glacier has retreated. According to the same report, since 2014 it lost 5.5 km² of ice through a 2 km retreat of its front, which uncovered an unexplored area.

What to do at the Viedma Glacier

Today the trip published with a schedule is the boat trip with a short walk. Ice trekking, for years the star tour, is uncertain (see below). Schedules and names change from one season to the next, so confirm everything when you book.

Boat trip and walk to Iceberg Bay

This is the Aventura Viedma tour, which according to the El Chaltén portal runs all year. The published information says:

  • Duration: half a day. Boarding 10:30, departure 11:00 and back at the port at 14:30.
  • Sailing: 2.5 hours in total.
  • Walk: about 900 m on flat ground, around 20 minutes, to a natural viewpoint over Iceberg Bay (Bahía de los Témpanos), on the Cabo de Hornos peninsula (the information sheet estimates about 45 minutes of walking in total).
  • Glacier front: the boat gets as close as the weather allows and stays about 20 minutes.
  • On board: a snack is included (sandwich, mineral water and a brownie made in El Chaltén) and there is a café.
  • Difficulty: low.

Boat trip price: the portal does not publish it on the information sheet, so ask when you book. The same operator offers a version with food and drink service on board and a full day from El Calafate.

Glacier trekking with crampons

InterPatagonia describes the Viedma Ice Trek: the same boat trip, landing on a rocky promontory facing the glacier and about 2.5 hours of walking on the ice with crampons, among crevasses, sinkholes and frozen ponds. Wikipedia gives a total duration of six hours, and agencies required a minimum age of 12.

Heads up: today it is not guaranteed. The agency MG Tours lists it as “not operating”, and a May 2026 article by Turismo de Bolsillo says it depends heavily on the stability of the ice and on National Parks permits, and that it is usually limited to walking on the rocky promontory without climbing onto the ice. If you want to walk on the glacier, ask beforehand whether it runs in the 2026/27 season.

When to go

The boat trip with a walk runs all year, according to the El Chaltén portal, and the trekking trips used to run from October to April. The operator transfer to the port only runs from November to March. In the summer high season it is best to book ahead.

The wind rules in any month: getting close to the front depends on the weather. When you book, ask what happens if the trip is changed or cancelled. More on the province’s weather: Weather in Santa Cruz: when to travel.

How many days to stay

The Viedma tour takes half a day. From El Chaltén, add it to a stay of several days so you can combine it with the trails to Laguna Torre or Laguna de los Tres: see What to do in El Chaltén besides Fitz Roy.

There is also a full day from El Calafate that combines Route 40, Parador La Leona, the boat trip and a visit to El Chaltén with a walk to the Chorrillo del Salto waterfall. It is handy if you are short on time, but it means more than 400 km round trip in one day.

Travel tips

  • Get to the port with time to spare: if you go on your own, check-in is at 10:30.
  • Dress in layers: on the lake, near the ice, it feels much colder even if it is sunny in town.
  • Windbreaker, hat, gloves and sunglasses: the glare from the ice and the water is strong.
  • Fuel: if you arrive on Route 40 from the north, there are very long stretches without gas stations. See Route 40 in Santa Cruz: fuel, gravel and distances.
  • Be careful with fire: the area has had recent forest fires and the guides stress it during the boat trip.

Town guide: El Chaltén. To the north, on RP 41, you can also visit Lago del Desierto.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Viedma Glacier bigger than Perito Moreno?

Yes. At about 977 km², it is the largest glacier in Los Glaciares National Park, ahead of Upsala, according to Wikipedia.

Can you get to the Viedma Glacier without a tour?

Not to the glacier front: it can only be reached by water, on the boats that leave from Puerto Bahía Túnel, 18 km from El Chaltén.

How long does the Viedma Glacier tour take?

The boat trip with a walk takes about 3.5 hours from port to port (11:00 to 14:30), or about 5 hours if you take the transfer from El Chaltén.

Do you have to pay a National Park entrance fee?

Parques Nacionales charges at the Los Cóndores, Cerro Torre–Base Fitz Roy and Río Eléctrico portals: $50,000 general rate, $25,000 Argentine residents, $8,000 Santa Cruz residents and $15,000 students, since June 1, 2026, online or by QR code and card only. Bahía Túnel is not one of those portals: ask the operator whether the tour has any extra charge.

Can you walk on the Viedma Glacier?

For years the Viedma Ice Trek was offered, with 2.5 hours on the ice and a minimum age of 12. Today it is not guaranteed: check whether it runs in the 2026/27 season before you travel.
