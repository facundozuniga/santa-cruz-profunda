Home › Articles › Camping in Santa Cruz: where to camp beyond El Calafate

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-28

Camping is one of the most direct ways to travel around Santa Cruz. Distances are long, towns are far apart and many of the best landscapes lie inside protected areas with no hotels. A tent or a motorhome lets you sleep right where the trail starts.

But this is no beach camping. There are strong winds, cold nights even in summer and places with no drinking water or phone signal. Some rules have also changed in recent seasons: the national park campsites in El Chaltén, which used to be free, now charge a fee and must be booked.

This guide covers the main places to camp outside El Calafate, the services at each one, how to book and what to bring. If you are just starting to plan, also see the Santa Cruz destinations by region.

Types of campsites you will find

  • Town campgrounds: municipal or private. Good for breaking up a long road trip. Services vary a lot from one to another: ask what the fee includes beforehand.
  • Organized campgrounds inside protected areas: such as those in Parque Patagonia, with toilets and, in some cases, showers, a small shop or space for motorhomes.
  • Primitive campsites: no electricity or shop, with a dry toilet or latrine. Some require a booking.
  • Refuges and domes: in Perito Moreno National Park. They are shared and unequipped: you bring everything.

Where to camp in Santa Cruz

El Chaltén and the Northern Zone of Los Glaciares National Park

According to elchalten.com, El Chaltén has three options: private campgrounds in town, campgrounds outside town to the north and three base camps inside the national park.

The town campgrounds are close to the bus terminal, grocery stores and restaurants. According to the same source, they are the only ones in the area with mobile phone signal.

There are three park campsites:

CampsiteLocationDistance from El Chaltén
Laguna CapriNext to Laguna Capri, on the Fitz Roy trail4 km
PoincenotOn the way to Laguna de los Tres9 km
De AgostiniNext to Laguna Torre9 km

Distances from elchalten.com and the Asociación Amigos del Parque Nacional Los Glaciares.

They can only be reached on foot. They stopped being free in the 2024-2025 season. They are run by the Asociación Amigos del Parque Nacional Los Glaciares, which in November 2026 lists a fee of ARS 30,000 per person per night for adults. Children up to 12, El Chaltén residents and licensed park guides camp free. For the 2026-2027 season they reopened on 9 October, according to the park office.

  • Booking required on the Association's website, paid in advance. You can book up to the day before; the fee is non-refundable and the date cannot be changed. Each campsite is booked separately.
  • What there is: dry toilets and camp hosts. No showers, shop, electricity or signal.
  • Times: check-in from 12:00 and check-out by 11:00. You can enter the campsite until 22:00.
  • No fires: gas stoves only, and they are mandatory. Any rubbish you produce goes back to town with you.

Separate from camping, the park's Northern Zone charges an entry fee at its portals. Fees and exemptions are in our guide to things to do in El Chaltén.

North of town, in the Río de las Vueltas valley, there are private campgrounds. One is at the southern tip of Lago del Desierto, 37 km away. If you don't have a vehicle, you need to book a transfer.

Parque Patagonia: Cañadón Pinturas Portal and Cueva de las Manos Portal

It is the best option for sleeping near the Cueva de las Manos. Parque Patagonia lies on Ruta 40, 54 km south of Perito Moreno, and according to the park the campgrounds are a few kilometres from the highway, on gravel roads passable by any vehicle. In November 2026 the park lists five campgrounds:

Data from Parque Patagonia (Fundación Rewilding Argentina).

  • La Señalada has the best location: from there you can walk to the Cueva de las Manos on a 1 km trail (about 45 minutes, difficult), and it is a few metres from the Interpretation Centre and Planetarium.
  • El Mollar has fenced bays to park motorhomes out of the wind. It is run by the La Posta de los Toldos lodge.
  • Bookings: the park suggests booking campgrounds 15 days ahead by email (destino@parquepatagoniaargentina.org for La Señalada and info@parquepatagoniaargentina.org for Del Río and La Confluencia). Otherwise it is first come, first served. For El Mollar and the yurts, the park lists the WhatsApp number +54 9 2975 00-0145.
  • Hours: the park asks visitors to arrive before 20:00. Connectivity in the area is poor.

Fill up on fuel, water and food before you go. Trails, entry and everything else: Parque Patagonia: Cañadón Pinturas guide.

Perito Moreno National Park

It is the province's most remote park and has a network of refuges, domes and camping pitches. According to Parques Nacionales, entry is free, but the use of refuges, domes and motorhome areas is subject to a fee. Book by email at reservasperitomoreno@bancodebosques.org or through the online booking system. Refuges are shared.

CircuitWhere to stay
Península Lago BelgranoCaleta Huala, Archipiélago, Dos Bahías and Playa Quetro refuges; camping pitches at the last three
AzaraRené Negro, Tucúquere, La Angostura and Azara refuges; El Arroyo camping area
Valle del Río LácteoGilberto refuge; Doug and Kris Tompkins refuge with 3 domes and 8 pitches
Lago BurmeisterCamping area with 3 pitches

Each pitch takes one tent for up to 4 people. Most sites allow one night; some allow two. For motorhomes there are parking areas at Península, Valle del Río Lácteo and Lago Burmeister, each with room for 3 vehicles.

The refuges are unequipped: bring a sleeping bag, cooking gear, a torch and a stove. Fires are banned throughout the park, and stoves are mandatory in camping areas. There is no shop either, and Parques Nacionales advises against drinking from natural streams.

The park is open from 1 October to 30 April and lies 220 km from Gobernador Gregores, including 90 km of gravel. There are no buses to the park: you need your own vehicle or a licensed transport operator. Arrive at your booked refuge or campsite in daylight. Los Antiguos

The official map from the Tourism Office marks the Municipal Campground and Hostel, and Welcome Argentina places it on Ruta Provincial 43, at the entrance to town (tel. 2963-491265). It is a good base for Lago Buenos Aires and the border crossing to Chile Chico. Services, fees and opening season: ask the Los Antiguos Tourism Office. Demand is very high during the National Cherry Festival.

Puerto Deseado

On the northern coast, the Puerto Deseado Tourism Office lists Camping La Costanera, on Avenida Marcelo Lotufo. According to the municipal listing it has shared dorms, a tent area, space for campervans with grey-water dumping, wifi, a hall and a covered barbecue area, and fire pits allowed from 9:00 to 18:00. No pets. Phone: +54 9 297 476-7976. Camping Cañadón de Giménez, on Ruta Nacional 281, is also listed. Services, fees and opening: check before you travel. Things to do in the area: Puerto Deseado guide.

Monte León National Park: check first

Official sources disagree. The Parques Nacionales accommodation page says the park has a free camping area with no services and no showers. Visit Argentina, the country's official tourism website, says camping and overnight stays are not allowed inside the park. Before you turn up with a tent, check with the park office: tel. 02962 498184, monteleon@apn.gob.ar.

There is no drinking water or shop inside the park. If you can't camp, the bases are Comandante Luis Piedra Buena, 35 km away, and Puerto Santa Cruz, 50 km away. More in Things to do in Piedra Buena and Monte León National Park.

When to camp

The main season runs from October–November to April. Perito Moreno National Park is open from 1 October to 30 April, and the El Chaltén campsites reopened on 9 October 2026. For the Tres Lagos area, Turismo Ruta 40 recommends going between November and April, the warmest period. At Lago San Martín, the same source says summer days can reach 25 °C and nights drop to 10–15 °C, with strong winds. Winter brings frost and snow.

The weather changes fast: check the forecast before you set off. More in Weather in Santa Cruz: when to go.

What to bring and tips

  • Stove and gas: fires are banned at the Los Glaciares campsites and throughout Perito Moreno National Park. Elsewhere, follow each campground's rules.
  • Water: primitive campsites may have no drinking water. Bring enough for your whole stay.
  • A wind-ready tent: good stakes, well-tensioned guy lines and the lowest end facing the wind.
  • Warm gear: a cold-rated sleeping bag, sleeping pad and jacket, even in January.
  • Fuel: fill the tank in every town. There are long stretches with no service stations; see the Ruta 40 in Santa Cruz guide.
  • Extra warm gear in the car: in case a breakdown forces you to spend the night on the road.
  • Rubbish: everything that goes into the park comes out with you.
  • Bookings: in high season, book ahead. Several places have no signal to confirm on the spot.
  • Road conditions: check before you travel at Prensa Santa Cruz – Estado de rutas (Vialidad).

Frequently asked questions

Can you camp for free in Santa Cruz?

In some places. In Parque Patagonia, the Del Río and La Confluencia campgrounds are free and open to all. The Los Glaciares National Park campsites in El Chaltén have charged a fee since the 2024-2025 season, and in Perito Moreno National Park the use of refuges, domes and motorhome areas is subject to a fee.

Where can you camp near the Cueva de las Manos?

At the Parque Patagonia campgrounds in the Cañadón Pinturas Portal. From La Señalada you can walk to the cave on a 1 km trail. In the Cueva de las Manos Portal there is the primitive Cañadón Caracoles campsite.

Are there places for motorhomes?

Yes. El Mollar, in Parque Patagonia, has fenced bays for motorhomes and electricity. In Perito Moreno National Park there are parking areas for 3 motorhomes at Península, Valle del Río Lácteo and Lago Burmeister.

Do you need to book the El Chaltén campsites?

Yes. The three national park campsites (Laguna Capri, Poincenot and De Agostini) are booked and paid online on the website of the Asociación Amigos del Parque Nacional Los Glaciares, up to the day before.

Can you camp in Monte León National Park?

Official sources disagree: Parques Nacionales mentions a free camping area with no services, while Visit Argentina says overnight stays are not allowed. Check with the park office before you go.
