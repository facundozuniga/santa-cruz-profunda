Home › Articles › Parador La Leona: history, how to get there and what to do at the classic Route 40 stop

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-26

Between El Calafate and El Chaltén there is one stop almost nobody skips: Parador La Leona. It is a building with tin walls and roof next to the river of the same name, close to where the river leaves Lake Viedma, and it has been welcoming travelers since 1894.

Today it is a café-restaurant, country hotel, souvenir shop and small museum. Drivers use it to break up the trip, have a coffee and stretch their legs. Here is how to get there, what to do at the roadhouse and in the nearby petrified forest, and what to know before you set off.

Where it is and how to get there

The roadhouse sits on National Route 40, on the banks of the La Leona River and a short walk from Lake Viedma, halfway between El Calafate and El Chaltén. From El Calafate you head east about 30 km to the junction with RN 40, then continue north about 80 km. The whole way is paved.

About the road to El Chaltén: Visit Argentina, the country's official tourism guide, calls it provincial route 41 (formerly 23), so on older maps or in other guides you will find it as RP 23. Everything about the town is in Things to do in El Chaltén besides Fitz Roy.

By bus: the Buenos Aires Times describes La Leona as an obligatory stop for buses between El Calafate and El Chaltén. If you travel this way, check with the company whether they stop and for how long.

On a tour: trips to the La Leona petrified forest go through the roadhouse (details below).

A bit of history

The name comes from the river. According to the chronicles cited by La Nación, in 1877 the explorer Francisco Pascasio Moreno, known as Perito Moreno, was attacked by a female puma while camping in the area. In Patagonia a female puma was called a “leona” (lioness), and that is how the river got its name.

The roadhouse was born in 1894, started by a Danish immigrant named Jensen. It was no coincidence: a raft ferry carried settlers and sheep across the river there, and since it took only 200 sheep per trip, the droves had to wait. The ferry was replaced by a bridge in 1974.

In 1910 it was bought by the Petersen family and the German Alfred Brodersen, who added a general store and bar and enlarged the hotel from two to four rooms. It later had other owners; according to La Nación, about two decades ago the Kargauer family bought it and now runs it together with Pablo Quiroz. The province declared it a Historical and Cultural Heritage Site of Santa Cruz.

Figures from southern Patagonian history passed through La Leona: the Salesian priest Alberto De Agostini and expedition members such as Lionel Terray and Guido Magnone, who made the first ascent of Fitz Roy in 1952. According to InterPatagonia and Welcome Argentina, many expeditions used it as their first base to stock up on gear, and the roadhouse was also touched by the tragic events of the Patagonian ranch workers' strike.

Did Butch Cassidy stay here?

It is the question everyone asks. InterPatagonia and Welcome Argentina say that in 1905 Butch Cassidy, the Sundance Kid and Ethel Place stayed almost a month at the roadhouse after robbing a bank in Río Gallegos. La Nación's Lugares magazine, on the other hand, says there is no record or evidence of that visit and that it looks more like a legend. Take it as part of local folklore, not as historical fact.

What to do at La Leona

Coffee, cake and a quick bite

This is the main plan. According to La Nación, the roadhouse serves simple meals, coffee and drinks, and has a grill area for large groups. The homemade cakes are what guides mention most.

Visit the museum and the shop

The current managers set up a museum about the customs of Santa Cruz pioneers, with everyday objects, and a souvenir shop. In some areas you can still see the original adobe walls lined with lenga wood.

Walk by the river and take in the scenery

The La Leona River runs right beside the roadhouse and Lake Viedma is a short walk away. Guides mention short walks and the chance to fish. To fish you need a Santa Cruz fishing permit and must follow the season and rules: see How to get a fishing permit in Santa Cruz.

Tour to the La Leona petrified forest

This is the area's big attraction. Do not confuse it with the Jaramillo Petrified Forests in the north of the province: this site is on Estancia Santa Teresita, at the foot of Cerro Los Hornos, about 10 km of gravel from the roadhouse along the south shore of Lake Viedma.

  • How to visit: according to La Nación (January 2025), only on a private tour, because the land belongs to a ranch. You can leave from El Calafate with transport included or, if you have your own vehicle, meet the group at the roadhouse.
  • Season: October to April. In winter the site is covered in snow.
  • What you will see: a depression of about 800 hectares, with an almost lunar landscape carved by erosion, and petrified trunks that, according to Welcome Argentina, in some cases exceed one meter in diameter.
  • Age: geologist Christian Soto told La Nación that the rock layers in the valley are from the Upper Cretaceous, about 70 million years old.
  • Hike: La Nación describes a circuit of about 5 km and low difficulty, taking around three hours, with the first 20 minutes uphill (Welcome Argentina rates it as moderate).
  • Format: full day from El Calafate. Welcome Argentina's listing gives an 8:30 departure and a 19:00 return, and La Nación mentions transport, the hike and a box lunch.

Prices and departures change by season: check with the agency before you travel. If you find a fossil, leave it where it is: paleontological heritage is protected by Law 25,743.

Estancia La Estela

A short distance to the west is Estancia La Estela, which the Buenos Aires Times recommended as the more complete lodging option in the area.

When to go

The roadhouse is on a paved road that is open all year. The area's high season, driven by El Chaltén, runs from October to April. Visit Argentina points out that from June to September it is very cold and it snows, so in winter drive with extra care and check beforehand that the roadhouse is open. More on the weather in Weather in Santa Cruz: when to travel, wind and temperatures.

How long to stay

  • Roadside stop: 30 minutes to an hour is enough for a coffee, the museum and a stroll by the river.
  • With the petrified forest tour: a full day from El Calafate.
  • To stay overnight: the roadhouse offers lodging. In 2024 La Nación mentioned two rooms in operation; InterPatagonia mentions four bedrooms with private bathrooms and a camping area. Check availability before you go.

Travel tips

  • Fill up in El Calafate. The roadhouse is not among the Route 40 service stations listed by Turismo Ruta 40. Heading north, the nearest ones are in El Chaltén (off the main road) and in Tres Lagos.
  • Watch out for wildlife on the road. The Buenos Aires Times warns that guanacos often cross RN 40.
  • If you go to the petrified forest: hiking shoes, sunscreen, a hat, a windbreaker and water.
  • Contact: in 2024 Lugares magazine published the phone number +54 9 (2966) 27-1695 and the Instagram account @laleonaylaestela.

To continue your trip: Viedma Glacier and Lake Viedma, Tres Lagos and Lake Cardiel and tourist ranches in Santa Cruz.

Frequently asked questions

Do you have to pay to visit Parador La Leona?

No: it is a roadside stop where you go in for a drink or a meal. What you pay for separately is the tour to the La Leona petrified forest, booked with an agency.

Can you stay overnight at La Leona?

Yes, the roadhouse also works as a country hotel. The number of rooms varies by source, so check before you go.

How long does it take from El Calafate to La Leona?

It is about 110 km on paved road: around one hour by car, according to Visit Argentina and the Buenos Aires Times.

Can you visit the La Leona petrified forest on your own?

Not freely: according to La Nación, it can only be visited on a private tour because it is on a ranch. If you drive yourself, you can meet the group at the roadhouse. Tours run from October to April.
