Home › Articles › Ruta 40 in Santa Cruz: sections, fuel and gravel

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-29

National Route 40 in Santa Cruz is the southernmost section of the country's longest road, which runs more than 5,000 km from north to south. It starts at km 0 at Cabo Vírgenes, near Río Gallegos, and leaves the province at the Chubut border, north of Perito Moreno. In between there is steppe, wind, small towns and long distances without services.

Today most of it is paved. According to Turismo Ruta 40 (updated July 2025), between Cabo Vírgenes and the Chubut border the road has 231 km of gravel and 1,219 km of pavement. The real challenge is not so much the road surface: it is fuel, weather and the lack of phone signal between towns. This guide gathers the practical side: sections, distances, gas stations and when to go.

Where it runs

From km 0, in the Cabo Vírgenes Provincial Nature Reserve, the road heads toward the Río Gallegos area, climbs to the coal basin of Río Turbio and 28 de Noviembre, turns north past La Esperanza and the El Calafate junction, and continues through Tres Lagos, Gobernador Gregores, Bajo Caracoles and Perito Moreno.

A word of caution about the south: the August 2026 road report from the Santa Cruz Provincial Roads Agency (AGVP) lists 47 km of gravel between Turbio Viejo and Puente Blanco, plus another 78 km of gravel between Puente Blanco and the RP 54 junction (Bella Vista), a stretch the guide lists as paved. The sources disagree, so before you leave check the AGVP road report, which is updated daily.

The “73 cursed kilometers”: Tres Lagos – Lago Cardiel

This is the most feared section of Ruta 40 in the province. In February 2025, LM Neuquén reported that the Provincial Roads Agency was regraveling half the roadway along these 73 km between Tres Lagos and Gobernador Gregores, and noted that it was not a permanent fix. In the August 2026 AGVP report the section was still listed as gravel: 44 km from Estancia La Lucía and another 29 km to the RP 29 junction (Lago Cardiel). Traveler reports on Ruta0 range from “lots of loose stone” (January 2023) to “very good condition” (January 2025).

In winter it gets seriously tough. On August 4 and 5, 2026, the Provincial Road Safety Agency and Argentina's National Roads Directorate opened the stretch between Tres Lagos and Gobernador Gregores (173 km) to 4x4 vehicles only, with chains mandatory, because of snow and deep ruts.

Alternative: the old route via RP 29

At the Cardiel junction the old Ruta 40 alignment, now RP 29, branches off: according to the Buenos Aires Times, it is about 67 km of gravel to Casa Riera, on the Chico River, bypassing Gobernador Gregores. The paved route through Gregores is longer, but it has services. If you don't know the area or are driving a low car, take the paved route.

Distances from Tres Lagos to Perito Moreno

This is the longest and emptiest stretch of Ruta 40 in Santa Cruz: 519 km according to Turismo Ruta 40, with only one town in between (Gobernador Gregores) and a hamlet with minimal services (Bajo Caracoles). The figures come from that guide's kilometer markers and are approximate.

From El Calafate to Tres Lagos it is about 160 km: Turismo Ruta 40 gives 162 km, and the Buenos Aires Times partial distances (30 km to Ruta 40, about 80 km to La Leona, 20 km to the El Chaltén turnoff and another 34 km to Tres Lagos) add up to 164.

Where to get fuel

This is what matters most. Turismo Ruta 40 lists these towns with gas stations on or near Ruta 40 in Santa Cruz, from south to north:

  • Río Gallegos
  • 28 de Noviembre and Río Turbio (coal basin)
  • Tapi Aike
  • La Esperanza
  • El Calafate (detour) and El Chaltén (detour)
  • Tres Lagos
  • Gobernador Gregores
  • Bajo Caracoles
  • Perito Moreno
  • Off Route 40: Lago Posadas and Los Antiguos
  • Between Gobernador Gregores and Bajo Caracoles there are about 216 km without a gas station. Leave Gregores with a full tank.
  • Bajo Caracoles can run out of fuel. The Buenos Aires Times mentions it, and Turismo Ruta 40 warns that any station can run out until the tanker truck arrives. Don't count on filling up there.
  • Fill up whenever you can. Turismo Ruta 40 recommends filling up in Tres Lagos, again in Gobernador Gregores and again in Bajo Caracoles, without waiting for the low-fuel light.
  • Carry an approved spare fuel can if your car has limited range. The Buenos Aires Times recommends a 20-liter one. With a headwind, real range can be considerably lower.
  • Bring some cash in case card payment fails at a small gas station.

Common gravel detours

  • RP 97 to the Cueva de las Manos: 47 km from Bajo Caracoles (see How to get to the Cueva de las Manos).
  • Bajo Caracoles → Lago Posadas: 75 km of gravel, according to the province.
  • RP 41 Los Antiguos → Lago Posadas: 155 km of mountain gravel, with no services (see Route 41).
  • The detours to Perito Moreno National Park have no gas stations.

What to see along the way

Cabo Vírgenes

At km 0 you will find the lighthouse, built in the early 20th century, and one of the most important Magellanic penguin colonies in the country. It is a little over two hours from Río Gallegos, and the 111 km between Cabo Vírgenes and Ruta 3 are gravel. Season and reserve hours: Cabo Vírgenes.

Cueva de las Manos

A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999, in the canyon of the Pinturas River. According to Visit Argentina, the oldest paintings are about 9,300 years old. It is reached by a gravel detour off Ruta 40, between Bajo Caracoles and Perito Moreno, and visits are guided. For hours and fees, check before you travel at Cueva de las Manos (official site).

Gobernador Gregores and its lakes

It is the only town on the central stretch and the base for Lakes Cardiel and Strobel and Perito Moreno National Park. More in Things to do in Gobernador Gregores.

When to go

  • November to April: the warmest season and the one Turismo Ruta 40 recommends. Long days and less risk of snow.
  • October: the season is already starting in several destinations, but it can be cold and very windy.
  • June to September: snow and ice. There may be closures and sections open only to 4x4 vehicles with chains.

The wind blows all year. Westerly winds average about 50 km/h according to Turismo Ruta 40, with much stronger gusts, especially on the open steppe.

How many days you need

To cross the province without detours, allow at least 3 days: Río Gallegos–El Calafate, El Calafate–Gobernador Gregores and Gobernador Gregores–Perito Moreno. Add 1 or 2 days for each detour, such as El Chaltén, the Cueva de las Manos or Perito Moreno National Park. This is our own suggestion.

Driving tips

  • Check road conditions before you leave in the daily AGVP report. If in doubt along the way, ask at the gas station about the next open one.
  • Watch out for loose animals. The AGVP report flags them on almost every section. Guanacos, rheas and sheep cross without warning, especially at dawn and dusk.
  • In the wind, keep both hands on the wheel and slow down when passing trucks.
  • Carry water, food and warm clothes, even in summer, plus a blanket in case you have to spend the night on the road. In winter, chains and antifreeze.
  • Download offline maps and carry a paper map: there are long stretches with no phone signal.

Driving on gravel

Slow down, avoid braking hard and don't make sudden maneuvers: on loose gravel the car slides more than you'd expect. Keep your distance from the vehicle ahead because of stones and dust. If a vehicle comes the other way, move over to the right and ease off. In rain or snow the gravel can turn to mud; if in doubt, wait or check with Vialidad.

What to carry in the car

  • An inflated spare tire, jack, wheel wrench and, if you can, a puncture repair kit.
  • A flashlight, a car charger and the route downloaded on your phone.
  • Water and food for a day; in winter, extra clothes, a blanket and chains.
  • A windshield protector or, at the very least, distance: gravel kicks up stones.

Frequently asked questions

Is there still gravel on Route 40 in Santa Cruz?

Yes. According to Turismo Ruta 40, about 231 km of gravel remain in the province, in three sections: Cabo Vírgenes–Ruta 3, the Puente Blanco and Turbio Viejo area, and the 72 km between Tres Lagos and the Cardiel junction. The AGVP road report may list more gravel sections, so check it before you set off.

Where can I get fuel between El Calafate and Perito Moreno?

In Tres Lagos, Gobernador Gregores and Bajo Caracoles. Turismo Ruta 40 recommends filling up at all three, because any station can run out of fuel.

What is the longest stretch without fuel?

Between Gobernador Gregores and Bajo Caracoles, about 216 km. Since Bajo Caracoles can run out of fuel, in practice you need enough range to reach Perito Moreno.

Can you drive Ruta 40 in Santa Cruz in a regular car?

In season and with the road dry, yes, driving carefully on the gravel. In winter some sections may be open only to 4x4 vehicles, with chains mandatory.

When is the best time to drive it?

Between November and April, the warmest season. From June to September there is snow and ice, and roads can be closed.
