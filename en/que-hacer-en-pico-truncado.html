Home › Articles › Things to do in Pico Truncado: wind, hydrogen and steppe in northern Santa Cruz

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-29

Pico Truncado sits in the middle of the steppe in northern Santa Cruz, in Deseado Department, 59 km west of Caleta Olivia on Provincial Route 12. Welcome Argentina and Interpatagonia describe it as the third most important town in the province; according to Wikipedia, the 2022 census counted about 25,000 inhabitants. The general overview is at Pico Truncado.

It is a town born of the railway, grown on gas and now billed as the National Hydrogen Capital. For travellers crossing the north of the province it offers a giant dinosaur made of oilfield scrap, a lagoon with flamingos and black-necked swans, a museum in a stone railway hut and steppe landscapes such as the Sierras Blancas.

Where it is and how to get there

Pico Truncado lies on the plateau of north-eastern Santa Cruz, away from the coast. The usual way in is National Route 3 to Caleta Olivia and then 59 km west on Provincial Route 12. From Perito Moreno you drive about 232 km east on Provincial Route 43, according to Interpatagonia. The province lists Route 43 as the access road.

By plane and by bus

The airport with scheduled flights mentioned by Visit Argentina and Interpatagonia is Comodoro Rivadavia (Chubut). From there it is 68 km on Route 3 to Caleta Olivia and then the 59 km of Route 12. The town has a bus terminal with long-distance services: companies, frequencies and timetables change, so check before you travel at the terminal or with the Tourism Office.

A bit of history

According to Welcome Argentina and Interpatagonia, stone tools found in caves in the area show human presence going back about 12,960 years. The present town was born with the Patagonian Railway, which linked Puerto Deseado with Las Heras: for years it was known as “Km 200”, its distance from the Puerto Deseado terminus.

The name comes from a cone-shaped hill with a basalt summit that looks cut off, visible from Provincial Route 12 on the way to Gobernador Gregores. Wikipedia places it 18 km from town.

  • 11 July 1921: President Hipólito Yrigoyen makes the founding official by decree.
  • 1946: gas is discovered underground.
  • 1965: the gas pipeline to Buenos Aires is built.
  • January 1978: the railway stops running.
  • 2005: the Experimental Hydrogen Plant is created.
  • 2014: Congress declares Pico Truncado the National Hydrogen Capital.

Things to do in Pico Truncado

The Bridasaurio

It is the town's best-known image: a life-size dinosaur built from oil-industry scrap, with drums, drill bits, chains, flanges and valves. According to Welcome Argentina it is 17 m long, 3 m wide and 4 m high, and it was made by sculptor Carlos Regazzoni in 1997. It stands where the ring road meets Provincial Route 12.

Laguna de los Patos

It is about 20 blocks from the centre, with signs showing the way. It is surrounded by steppe vegetation, and a young grove is growing at one end. In summer it works as a natural bathing spot. It is the best place to watch birds without leaving town: ducks, upland geese, ruddy-headed geese, flamingos and black-necked swans.

Museo Regional Antiguos Pobladores (regional museum)

It is housed in an old stone track-workers' hut built by the railway company. According to Wikipedia, it was the first National School No. 8 from 1918 to 1950 and has been a museum since 1991. Welcome Argentina says it displays original pieces, reproductions of rock art and photos of Piedra Museo, and gives its address as Gobernador Gregores 414 and Santos Arroyabe. For opening hours and admission, check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Pico Truncado.

Jorge Romanutti wind farm and Hydrogen Plant

Pico Truncado lives with the wind, and puts it to work. The Jorge Romanutti wind farm opened in 1995. According to the bill that declared the town National Hydrogen Capital, it has four 600 kW turbines, with 2.4 MW of installed capacity. Wikipedia gives an average annual wind speed of 10.3 m/s, and Welcome Argentina says the farm covers up to 30% of the town's energy needs.

That energy powers the Experimental Hydrogen Plant, created in 2005 and conceived as a school-factory, with a training module and another for production, storage, laboratory work and prototype testing. It is described as the only one of its kind in Latin America. We found no official information about tourist visits to the plant: if you are interested, ask at the Tourism Office.

Ciudad Sonora (the Sounding City)

Welcome Argentina and Interpatagonia mention Ciudad Sonora near town: sculptures by international artists that make sounds when the wind moves them. We found no up-to-date official information about its exact location or condition, so ask at the Tourism Office before going.

Cerro Pico Truncado, Sierras Blancas and the Deseado River valley

The hill that gives the town its name can be seen from Route 12, on the way to Gobernador Gregores. Further out lie the Deseado River valley and the Sierras Blancas: according to the province, their canyons form a maze about three kilometres long in ochre, white, beige and orange tones, and Visit Argentina mentions walls about thirty metres high that you explore on foot along the valley. We found no official information on access or distances: ask the Tourism Office how to get there and whether you need to cross private land.

The MARA Route and archaeological trips

Along Provincial Route 12 runs the MARA Route (Museo Arqueológico Regional Austral), a circuit the province describes as an open-air museum linking the archaeological, palaeontological and geological heritage of the Deseado Massif. We found no guided-tour operators listed on official websites: ask at the Tourism Office.

  • Piedra Museo: Welcome Argentina places it about 250 km away, south of the Deseado River, and describes it as one of the oldest archaeological sites in the country, with human presence dating back about 13,000 years.
  • Cueva de las Manos: take Route 43 to Route 40 and then head south to the gravel turn-off to the visitor centre. Visits are guided; see how to get to the Cueva de las Manos.
  • Jaramillo Petrified Forests: araucaria trees turned to stone about 150 million years ago. According to the National Parks management plan, it is about 195 km from Pico Truncado via RP 12 and RP 49 (western access, gravel); the recommended access is from Route 3. See our guide to the park.

Do not touch or pick up stone tools or fossils: they are protected by Law 25,743.

The former Hotel Argentino, a recent loss

Some guides still recommend visiting the Hotel Argentino, a corrugated-iron building opened in 1937 that housed the town's first cinema and was declared a Provincial Historic Heritage site. On 5 April 2026 a fire destroyed it completely, according to La Opinión Austral. It can no longer be visited.

Festivals and events

  • Anniversary: 11 July, the date of the official founding.
  • Festival Nacional Austral del Folklore (national southern folk festival): groups from across Patagonia compete for the “Pingüino de Oro” (Golden Penguin). According to the regional press, the 57th edition takes place on 31 October and 1 November 2026, with the national stage from 6 to 8 November and free admission.
  • Festival Nacional Juvenil de Teatro (national youth theatre festival): the province highlights it among the town's cultural events.
  • Travesía del Viento: according to Wikipedia, since 2014 it has brought together kite-sport enthusiasts every September to cover 106 km around the Jaramillo Petrified Forest area.

Dates change from year to year: check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Pico Truncado.

When to go

The climate is cold desert. According to Weatherbase data cited by Wikipedia, the average annual temperature is around 11 °C, with a mean of 17 °C in January and 4 °C in July, and about 162 mm of rain a year. Strong winds blow for much of the year.

For exploring the steppe, the long-day season from November to March is best. In winter you can still visit the town, but gravel-road trips get harder with frost and snow. These are our suggestions.

How many days to stay

Visit Argentina recommends 1 to 3 days. One is enough for the town: Bridasaurio, lagoon, museum and wind farm. If you add Sierras Blancas, the MARA Route or Jaramillo, allow 2 or 3.

Travel tips

  • Clothing: bring warm layers and a windbreaker all year round; temperature changes are sudden.
  • Fuel: fill up before heading into the steppe. There are long stretches without services between towns.
  • Tourism: the Tourism Office is at Gobernador Gregores and Urquiza. According to the provincial website it is open Monday to Friday from 8 am to 2 pm, on +54 297 499-6924 and at turismomuseoPT@gmail.com; check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Pico Truncado.
  • Heritage: do not touch or pick up stone tools or fossils.

To continue your trip: Caleta Olivia, Las Heras, Koluel Kayke, Jaramillo Petrified Forests and Puerto Deseado.

Frequently asked questions

Why is it called Pico Truncado?

After a cone-shaped hill with a basalt summit that looks as if it had been cut off, visible from Provincial Route 12 heading towards Gobernador Gregores. Wikipedia places it 18 km from town.

How do I get to Pico Truncado?

Take National Route 3 to Caleta Olivia and then drive 59 km west on Provincial Route 12. From Perito Moreno, take Route 43. The nearest airport with scheduled flights is Comodoro Rivadavia.

Can you visit the hydrogen plant?

We found no official information about tourist visits. Ask at the Pico Truncado Tourism Office.

Can you still see the Hotel Argentino?

No. The historic building was destroyed by a fire on 5 April 2026, according to La Opinión Austral.

How many days do you need?

One day for the town. If you want to add Sierras Blancas, the MARA Route or the Jaramillo Petrified Forests, allow 2 or 3 days.
