Home › Articles › Things to do in Puerto Santa Cruz

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-29

Puerto Santa Cruz is one of the oldest towns in the province: the province presents it as the oldest city in Patagonia and the first provincial capital. It sits on the south bank of the wide Santa Cruz River estuary, close to the Atlantic. Magellan's expedition passed through in 1520, and the Beagle, with Charles Darwin, in 1834. The general overview is at Puerto Santa Cruz.

Today it's a quiet town of 5,390 people (2022 census). Life revolves around the estuary and the deep-water port of Punta Quilla. It has no big tourist attractions: it has history, water, birds and a lot of silence. If you're driving Route 3 between San Julián and Río Gallegos, the detour is worth it, and if you plan to visit Monte León National Park, it's one of the two possible bases, along with Piedra Buena.

Where it is and how to get there

Puerto Santa Cruz is in the Corpen Aike department, in the southeast of the province, at about 50° south latitude. It isn't on National Route 3: leaving Comandante Luis Piedra Buena, you cross the Santa Cruz River and take Provincial Route 288. According to the National Monuments Commission, it's 36 km from Piedra Buena.

By bus

The town has a bus terminal, and the province says you can get there by bus. Companies, schedules and frequencies change with the season: check at the terminal or the Tourism Office before you travel.

By air

According to the province, the nearest reference airport is Río Gallegos; from there you continue by car or bus.

A bit of history

In 1520, Juan Rodríguez Serrano, captain of the Santiago in Magellan's expedition, reached the estuary and named it Santa Cruz. Magellan later brought the fleet down from San Julián and, according to the National Monuments Commission, stayed until October 18 gathering supplies before searching for the strait. Before leaving, he had an altar built at Punta Reparo.

In April 1834 the Beagle was beached in the river to repair its hull. Captain Robert FitzRoy led 25 men upriver, Charles Darwin among them, dragging the boats for 16 days. They turned back without reaching the source, which turned out to be Lago Argentino. Darwin wrote about the stepped terraces of the valley, the same plateaus you see from the waterfront.

Then came the Anglican mission at Cañadón Misioneros (1862-1863), the Rouquaud colony (1872-1874) and, in 1873, the schooner Chubut under Captain Guillermo Lawrence. In late 1878 Commodore Luis Py's naval division landed to uphold Argentina's claims against Chile: December 1 became Argentina's Navy Fleet Day and the town's founding date, according to the National Monuments Commission. It was the capital of the Santa Cruz National Territory until 1888, when the role passed to Río Gallegos, and in 1943 Decree 12,466 declared it a historic site.

Things to do in Puerto Santa Cruz

Walk the estuary waterfront

It's the essential stroll: good for walking, cycling or drinking mate while looking at the water, and the province highlights its sunrises. Across the water you see the stepped plateaus of the other bank.

Tour the historic sites

You can see them on foot in a morning:

  • Mausoleum of Captain Carlos María Moyano, the first governor of the Santa Cruz territory.
  • Commodore Luis Py monolith, commemorating the reaffirmation of Argentine sovereignty in Patagonia.
  • The great cross and the Centennial Monument, facing the estuary, honoring the region's founding figures and pioneers, and the monument to Commander Luis Piedra Buena.
  • Exaltación de la Santa Cruz church, the town's first reinforced-concrete building, still intact.

Visit the Carlos Borgialli Regional Museum

It's at Av. Piedra Buena 234 and has rooms on fauna and flora, archaeology, paleontology and local history, from the Indigenous peoples to colonization and the Navy's presence. Phone according to Interpatagonia: +54 2962-498301. Hours and admission: ask at the museum or the Tourism Office before you go.

Fish in the estuary

The estuary is a sport-fishing area for large róbalo and pejerrey. There are access points to the shore and, according to Welcome Argentina, fishing trips and boat rentals: ask the Tourism Office who's currently operating. You need the provincial permit to fish; seasons and rules: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (provincia) – Permisos de pesca, temporada 2026/2027.

Kayaking and water sports

The province lists kayaking among the local activities, and the estuary has access points for water sports. Check the wind and the tide before heading out.

Birdwatching on Isla Leones

Isla Leones, in the estuary, is good for birdwatching, with many species passing through and others breeding there. You reach it by crossing on an excursion: ask about departures and season at the Tourism Office.

See the port of Punta Quilla

It's the area's deep-water port, at the mouth of the river, about 17 km south of town. Ocean-going ships call here, and it's a good viewpoint over the mouth of the estuary.

Go to Monte León National Park

It's about 50 km away, with the entrance at km 2400 of RN 3, and the park headquarters are in town (Juan Williams 361). It has one of the largest Magellanic penguin colonies in the country, with more than 40,000 pairs according to Parques Nacionales, a sea lion colony, the Cabeza del León and shingle beaches. Without a car you'll need a taxi, remís or transfer. Trails, hours and fees in Things to do in Piedra Buena and Monte León National Park; road conditions: check before you travel at Parques Nacionales – Monte León: horarios y cómo llegar.

When to go

The climate is cold semi-arid. Using the historical data compiled by Wikipedia (1901-1960), the mean annual high is around 14.3 °C, the low around 3.7 °C, and it rains about 182 mm a year. In our view, the best season runs from October to April: long days, milder temperatures and penguins at Monte León, which according to Parques Nacionales arrive at the colony between October and April. Winter is cold with short days, but you can still explore the town. The wind blows all year: calm days on the estuary are gold for fishing or boating.

How many days to stay

  • Half a day: waterfront, historic sites and museum, as a stop on Route 3.
  • One day: add Punta Quilla and a fishing or estuary outing.
  • Two or three days: use the town as a base for Monte León and Piedra Buena. Visit Argentina suggests 2 to 4 days to get to know the park well.

Travel tips

  • Head to Monte León with a full tank, water and food: the park has no store or drinking water.
  • Bring a windbreaker, a hat and sunscreen, even in summer.
  • To visit Isla Leones or fish from a boat, arrange it in advance: options are limited.
  • Check the tide times: in the estuary they change the landscape and the shore access a lot.
  • Tourism Office: Av. Piedra Buena and San Martín; according to the province, open Monday to Friday from 8 am to 8 pm.

Frequently asked questions

Is Puerto Santa Cruz on Route 3?

No. From Comandante Luis Piedra Buena you cross the Santa Cruz River and take Provincial Route 288; it's about 36 km according to the National Monuments Commission.

Is it the same as Santa Cruz Province?

No. Puerto Santa Cruz is a town in Santa Cruz Province. The provincial capital is Río Gallegos.

Can you visit Monte León from Puerto Santa Cruz?

Yes. The park is about 50 km away according to Parques Nacionales, and its headquarters are in town. It's best to go by car; otherwise, by taxi, remís (private car service) or transfer.

What can you fish in the Santa Cruz estuary?

Mainly róbalo (Patagonian blenny) and pejerrey (silverside). You need the provincial fishing permit: check the current regulations before you go.

Why is it important in Patagonian history?

Magellan passed through in 1520 and Darwin in 1834. It was the capital of the Santa Cruz National Territory until 1888 and has been a national historic site since 1943.
