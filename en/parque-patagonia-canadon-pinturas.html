Home › Articles › Patagonia Park: a guide to Cañadón Pinturas, its trails and how to get there

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-31

In northwestern Santa Cruz, south of Perito Moreno, the steppe breaks into canyons with red, ochre and yellow walls. This is where you find the Cañadón Pinturas Gateway of Patagonia Park, in the same valley as the Cueva de las Manos. The park's general overview is at Patagonia Park.

The gateway has self-guided trails, campsites, a lodge, a planetarium and a stargazing observatory. You have to register at the Visitor Center before hiking, and you pay the entry fee right there.

What Patagonia Park is

The names are easy to mix up, so here they are in order:

  • Patagonia Park is the conservation area that the Rewilding Argentina Foundation has been building since 2011. According to the foundation, it covers about 180,000 hectares.
  • Patagonia National Park is the national section, run by the National Parks Administration. It was created by Law 27,081, passed on December 16, 2014, with about 52,811 hectares. Rewilding says it now exceeds 106,000.
  • Cueva de las Manos Provincial Park protects the archaeological site. It was created in 2019 with 600 hectares that Rewilding donated to the province.

You explore the park through four gateways, each with a visitor center: Cañadón Pinturas and Cueva de las Manos, run by Patagonia Park, and La Ascensión and El Sauco, which are sectors of the National Park. Across the border, Chile has its own Patagonia National Park, in the Chacabuco Valley: it is a different park with a different access.

Where it is and how to get there

The Cañadón Pinturas Gateway is on Ruta Nacional 40, 54 km south of Perito Moreno. Any vehicle can get in, but all internal roads are gravel.

Without a car

According to the park, Chaltén Travel buses stop at the gateway: the stop is called “Parque Patagonia (Perito Moreno - Santa Cruz - Argentina)”. From the entrance to your lodging inside the park you can request a transfer, arranged in advance with the park. Buses also reach Perito Moreno, and you can arrange a transfer from there. Schedules and frequencies: check before you travel at Parque Patagonia – Cómo llegar.

Entry fees and hours

Entry to the Cañadón Pinturas and Cueva de las Manos gateways costs a single fee of AR$6,000, according to the park. It is free for Santa Cruz residents, retirees and children under 12, with the corresponding ID. You pay by card or bank transfer when you register at the Visitor Center. Activities inside are not charged separately unless you hire a guide; visits to the archaeological sites do have a fee.

The Cueva de las Manos Gateway is open from 9 am to 7 pm; outside those hours there is self-registration at the Visitor Center. If you are staying overnight in the park, you are asked to arrive before 8 pm. In the National Park sectors, the National Parks Administration lists 8 am to 8 pm in high season (from October 1 until after Easter) and 10 am to 5 pm in low season. Fees and updates: check before you travel at Parque Patagonia – Recomendaciones y preguntas frecuentes.

Tierra de Colores

It is the best known: a canyon of pink, ochre and yellow slopes, like the one in the photo above. It is short, so it fits easily into any itinerary.

Cueva de las Manos

The rock paintings are more than 9,000 years old, and the site has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999. Visits are guided and paid separately. Access, hours and tips in our guide on how to get to the Cueva de las Manos.

Alero Charcamata

It is a full-day excursion, only with a guide and a prior booking. After crossing a stream you reach a cave with an 80 m wide mouth and 40 m high, holding a large number and variety of rock paintings. You can book it, for example, at the La Posta de los Toldos lodge.

Interpretation Center, Planetarium and observatory

The Interpretation Center and Planetarium opened in 2023, according to Rewilding. It covers the region's geology, the evolution of its species and the biodiversity crisis, and it has a café. It is a few meters from La Señalada campsite. The Star Observatory is free to use: you reach it by a path that starts behind the La Posta de los Toldos lodge.

Wildlife

Wildlife watching is one of the big draws: guanacos, lesser rheas, condors and, with luck, pumas. The park describes itself as the only puma-watching destination in Argentine Patagonia and offers outings with specialized guides.

Cueva de las Manos Gateway (Caracoles Canyon)

It opened in 2024 and gives vehicle access to Cueva de las Manos Provincial Park. Besides the archaeological site, it has:

  • Trails: Tierras Blancas (7 km, 2 h, hard), Balcón Sur (8 km, 2 h, hard) and the Huella Cañadón Caracoles (12 km, easy but long).
  • Climbing: 80 routes of different grades, free to use.
  • Mirador El Cóndor: on the rim of the Pinturas Canyon, with parking, tables and benches. Free.
  • Visitor Center: mandatory registration, coffee, tea and simple sandwiches.

In the canyon you can also see orange mountain viscachas, recently reintroduced, black-chested buzzard-eagles and condors.

National Park sectors: La Ascensión and El Sauco

La Ascensión

It sits on Lake Buenos Aires, 17 km from Los Antiguos. You have to check in at the Visitor Center, where park rangers register you. The old La Ascensión ranch house, founded in 1912, still has its shearing shed, corrals and matera (the ranch hands' common room). Trails lead to the lakeshore, Cerro Negro (575 m above sea level), Cerro de la Calle, Laguna COA and Roca Guacha. The most demanding one climbs to the Lago Buenos Aires Plateau, habitat of the hooded grebe, the park's flagship bird. For camping there is Área Caburé, with fire pits, grills, running water and toilets, and Área El Rincón, on the way to the plateau, where only camping stoves are allowed.

El Sauco

It is in the Río Blanco valley, on RP 103. It has the Río Blanco Viewpoint, overlooking Sierra Colorada, Monte Zeballos and Cerro San Lorenzo, and the easy Vuelta al Valle trail. Los Choiques campsite is free. Fires are not allowed there: only camping stoves.

Where to stay at the Cañadón Pinturas Gateway

  • Hostería La Posta de los Toldos: nine rooms with private bathrooms, a restaurant and a wood-fired kitchen in an old outpost. Bookings: reservas@lapostadelostoldos.com.
  • Camping El Mollar: paid and suitable for motorhomes, with individual windbreak enclosures, a fire pit, a shared building with a wood stove and bathrooms with showers. Run by the lodge.
  • Camping La Señalada: paid, with twelve windbreaks for tents, bathrooms and showers. It has a country store with packed meals, drinks and regional products, open 8 am to 9 pm, and yurts for up to four people.
  • Camping Al Río (del Río): free, in the Pinturas River valley, with bathrooms and four tent platforms.
  • Camping La Confluencia: free, with bathrooms and six tent platforms. Reachable only on foot.

At the Cueva de las Manos Gateway there is the rustic Cañadón Caracoles campsite, with 10 platforms, a dry toilet and non-potable water, no showers. For the campsites, the park suggests booking 15 days ahead; otherwise it is first come, first served. The lodge, the yurts and El Mollar require a prior booking. Contacts and rates: check before you travel at Parque Patagonia – Dónde dormir (hostería, yurtas y campings). Outside the park, see the Perito Moreno directory and the one for Los Antiguos.

When to go

According to the park, the best time is November to March: long days, between 10 and 24 °C, and nights that can drop to 4 °C. Strong wind is common at any time of year. Spring brings flowers, and in winter you can track wildlife by its footprints in the snow.

How many days to stay

  • 1 day: Tierra de Colores in the morning and Cueva de las Manos in the afternoon.
  • 2 or 3 days: add the Koi trail, a night of camping, the planetarium and the observatory.
  • One week: combine Cañadón Pinturas with La Ascensión, Los Antiguos, Perito Moreno and Ruta 41.

These are our own suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: leave with a full tank and fill up whenever you can. In some cases, the park recommends carrying an extra jerry can.
  • Cash: bring Argentine pesos. Many places have no card terminal and don't exchange foreign currency.
  • Supplies: the nearest supermarket is in Perito Moreno. Bring enough water and food.
  • Signal: there is no phone signal or data on the roads. There is wifi at the visitor centers, La Posta de los Toldos, El Mollar and La Señalada campsites, the Interpretation Center and the Cueva de las Manos Gateway.
  • Gravel: don't exceed 60 km/h and carry a spare tire.
  • Clothing: warm layers, a windbreaker, shoes for rocky ground, a hat, sunscreen and sunglasses.
  • Rules: no pets, fires only in designated places, and watch wildlife from a distance, never chasing or feeding it.

To continue your trip: Lago Posadas and Ruta 41, Gobernador Gregores and northern Ruta 40.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to enter Patagonia Park?

According to the park, entry to the Cañadón Pinturas and Cueva de las Manos gateways costs a single fee of AR$6,000, free for Santa Cruz residents, retirees and children under 12. Visits to the archaeological sites are paid separately. Check the current fee before you travel.

Where is the Cañadón Pinturas Gateway?

On Ruta Nacional 40, 54 km south of Perito Moreno and 75 km north of Bajo Caracoles.

Can you go without a car?

Yes. According to the park, Chaltén Travel buses stop at the gateway, and from there you can request a transfer to your lodging, arranged in advance. You can also take a bus to Perito Moreno and book a transfer.

Do you need a guide?

Not for the self-guided trails. You do for Alero Charcamata, and Cueva de las Manos is visited with a guide.

Are Patagonia Park and Patagonia National Park the same thing?

Not exactly. Patagonia National Park is the part run by the National Parks Administration (La Ascensión and El Sauco). Patagonia Park is the wider conservation area, which includes the Cañadón Pinturas and Cueva de las Manos gateways.
