Home › Articles › Border crossings from Santa Cruz to Chile: which ones there are, where they are and how to cross

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-13

Santa Cruz shares its entire western border and its southern tip with Chile, and many trips through the province end up on the other side: Torres del Paine from Río Turbio, the Carretera Austral from Los Antiguos or Punta Arenas from Río Gallegos. Choosing the right crossing changes the route, the type of road and how long you wait.

According to the national government, the province has thirteen international crossings with Chile. They don't all work the same way: some have fixed hours and handle trucks and buses, others are gravel or seasonal, and one is crossed on foot and by boat. Here are the ones travelers use most, from south to north, with what official sources publish.

Before you cross

Gendarmería reports the status of each crossing (open or closed) on the Ministry of Security's international crossings site. Check it on the day you travel, because a crossing can close due to snow, wind or roadworks. Migraciones notes that its hours are those of its own offices and recommends also checking the neighboring country's hours, which may differ: check before you travel at Migraciones – Pasos internacionales y horarios.

Documents

For the Integración Austral border center, the national government requires:

  • Adults: a valid DNI or passport in good condition. Proof of a pending DNI or passport renewal isn't accepted.
  • Minors traveling with both parents: the minor's DNI showing both parents' details on the back, an original or certified birth certificate, or a family booklet.
  • Minors traveling with only one parent, alone or with third parties: a travel authorization (before a notary, a judge or Migraciones) or, where applicable, a death certificate.
  • Everything in original or certified copy.

For any crossing into Chile, the official page of recommendations for crossing into Chile states that the digital DNI (card or light-blue booklet, latest issue) or a passport are accepted, and that the handwritten DNI and proof of a pending application are not. Foreigners need a valid passport and, depending on nationality, a visa.

If you cross by car

ARCA (formerly AFIP) and the national government require a valid driver's license, insurance with international coverage for Chile, the green card or title if you're the owner, and the blue card or the owner's authorization (form OM-2261 certified at Customs or a notarized power of attorney) if you're not. The license plate must be etched at least on the windshield and rear window. If the car is rented, the company must authorize the crossing in writing. Details are in the Paso Jeinimeni guide.

Health and agricultural controls

At Integración Austral, SENASA carries out checks on the Argentine side. To enter Chile you must fill out the SAG sworn declaration and declare any product of animal or plant origin, whatever the quantity: anything not authorized is confiscated and failing to declare can lead to a fine. Fresh fruit, meat and dairy are exactly what you should finish before crossing. On the way back, SENASA also checks what you bring into Argentina.

Integración Austral (Monte Aymond)

It links Río Gallegos with Punta Arenas via National Route 3 on the Argentine side and CH-255 on the Chilean side. It sits at the southern tip of the province, at 164 m above sea level and about 70 km from Río Gallegos. It's a border center open to all kinds of traffic (cars, freight and long-distance buses), with Migraciones, Customs, Gendarmería and SENASA.

It's also the overland route to Tierra del Fuego: there's no continuous road through Argentine territory, so you cross here and, once in Chile, take the Primera Angostura ferry.

Hours: according to the border center's page, it opens at 8 am, exit from both countries closes at 9:45 pm and entry at 10 pm. Migraciones publishes 8 am to 10 pm for the crossing and 8 am to 5 pm for country-to-country transit.

Laurita – Casas Viejas

It links Río Turbio with Puerto Natales. On the Argentine side you reach it via National Route 293 and on the Chilean side via Route 9. According to Interpatagonia, it's 19 km from Río Turbio, at 239 m above sea level, and Puerto Natales is 14 km from the border.

It was closed from October 1, 2023 for paving works and fully reopened on January 8, 2025, according to Billiken, which gives hours of 8 am to 8 pm. Casas Viejas isn't on Migraciones' list of hours, so confirm hours and status on the day you cross on the international crossings site.

Dorotea

It's the other option between Río Turbio and Puerto Natales. It's 14 km from Río Turbio and at 618 m above sea level; on the Chilean side, CH-250 reaches Puerto Natales in 27 km, according to Interpatagonia.

Hours: Migraciones publishes 8 am to 5 pm. In late October 2026, Gendarmería reported hours of 5 am to 6:30 pm on the Argentine side and Chile's road authority asked drivers to carry chains, according to Ruta0's summary. Since sources don't match, check the status on the day.

Cancha Carrera

According to Interpatagonia, it's about 47 km north of Río Turbio, only opens in summer, from December to March, and leads to the Cerro Castillo area, the gateway to Torres del Paine. Opening dates for the 2026-2027 season, hours and road conditions: check before you travel at Migraciones – Pasos internacionales y horarios.

Lago del Desierto – Candelario Mancilla (on foot)

It isn't a crossing for vehicles. It's the backpacker and cyclist crossing between El Chaltén and Villa O'Higgins, the southern end of Chile's Carretera Austral.

  1. El Chaltén – Punta Sur: 37 km on Provincial Route 41 to the Punta Sur pier on Lago del Desierto.
  2. Boat across Lago del Desierto to Punta Norte, where you complete Argentine immigration.
  3. On foot to the border and Candelario Mancilla: according to the Green Trails travel account, about 5 km on a trail to Chile and another 15 km on a road to the Chilean post at Candelario Mancilla.
  4. Boat across Lake O'Higgins to Villa O'Higgins. Chilean operators publish schedules and fares each season.

Receptivo Chaltén runs the Lago del Desierto boat from November 1 to March 31, every day except Wednesdays, and asks for a valid passport (elsewhere on its site it mentions passport or DNI). Sailings can be rescheduled or canceled due to weather, and on the Chilean side some travelers have waited several days at Candelario Mancilla because of the wind: leave spare days. Schedules and fares: check before you travel at Receptivo Chaltén – Cruce internacional por Lago del Desierto.

Roballos

From Lago Posadas, RP 41 continues north to Paso Roballos, which crosses to Cochrane, in Chile. On the Chilean side, Route X-83 runs through the Chacabuco Valley of Chile's Patagonia National Park, according to Ruta de los Parques. According to the road authority's report, it's an improved gravel road that can be cut by snow in winter.

It's remote, with little traffic. Roballos isn't on Migraciones' list of hours: for hours and status, check before you travel at Argentina.gob.ar – Pasos internacionales: Paso Roballos (estado y contacto) (published phone: (2963) 432033). More about the road: Lago Posadas and Route 41.

Río Jeinimeni (Los Antiguos – Chile Chico)

It links Los Antiguos with Chile Chico, on the shores of Lake Buenos Aires (General Carrera on the Chilean side). It's the natural exit from northern Santa Cruz to the Carretera Austral and the Marble Caves. Migraciones publishes hours of 9 am to 8 pm.

Which crossing suits your trip

  • Río Gallegos to Punta Arenas or Tierra del Fuego: Integración Austral.
  • Río Turbio to Puerto Natales: Dorotea or Casas Viejas.
  • El Calafate to Torres del Paine: Cancha Carrera when it's open (summer); the rest of the year, Dorotea or Casas Viejas.
  • El Chaltén to the Carretera Austral without a car: Lago del Desierto, in season.
  • Northern Santa Cruz to the Carretera Austral: Jeinimeni, from Los Antiguos.
  • Lago Posadas to the Chacabuco Valley: Roballos.

Travel tips

  • Arrive well before closing time: paperwork on both sides can take a while, especially in high season.
  • Keep in mind the time difference with Chile, which changes depending on the time of year.
  • Fill up before you cross.
  • On gravel or seasonal crossings, check Gendarmería's status before setting off and have a plan B.
  • Keep the receipts you're given at the border, especially those for the car: you'll need them on the way back.

Frequently asked questions

Can I cross into Chile with just my DNI?

Yes, if you're Argentine and your DNI is valid and in good condition (digital DNI, latest issue) or you have a valid passport. Proof of a pending application isn't accepted. For the walking crossing at Lago del Desierto, the boat operator asks for a passport.

Which Santa Cruz crossings have hours published by Migraciones?

Integración Austral (8 am to 10 pm), Jeinimeni (9 am to 8 pm) and Dorotea (8 am to 5 pm). Any crossing can close due to weather: check Gendarmería's status on the day.

Can you walk from El Chaltén to Chile?

Yes, via Lago del Desierto to Candelario Mancilla, combining boats and about 20 km on foot. The Argentine boat runs from November 1 to March 31; leave spare days because of the wind.

Do you have to go through Chile to reach Tierra del Fuego?

Yes. From Río Gallegos you cross at Integración Austral and, in Chile, take the Primera Angostura ferry.
