Home › Articles › How to get to the Cueva de las Manos

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-26

The Cueva de las Manos is the most famous archaeological site in Santa Cruz. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999 and lies in the Río Pinturas canyon, in the province's northwest, far from any city. Today it is a 600-hectare Provincial Park run from Perito Moreno, bordering the Cañadón Pinturas Portal of Parque Patagonia. Getting there isn't hard, but there is gravel, long distances and few fuel stations, and visits are always guided. Here's everything worth knowing.

Where it is

According to the Perito Moreno municipal website, the site is 114 km south of the town. All access roads branch off National Route 40, on the stretch between Perito Moreno and the hamlet of Bajo Caracoles.

Access options

The municipal website lists three access points on RN 40. For road conditions, the official site refers you to the Provincial Roads Department (Vialidad Provincial).

1. Via RP 97 from Bajo Caracoles (the most used)

From Perito Moreno you head south on paved RN 40 to the hamlet of Bajo Caracoles, about 130 km away. At the junction with RP 97 you turn north. Turismo Ruta 40 describes the road like this: after about 11 km, Cerro Poivre, a volcanic hill shaped like a cap, appears on your left; 38 km further on, a hairpin bend marks the start of a descent of about 120 m to the entrance, along 1.5 km of cliffside road. In total it's 47 km of gravel, which the municipal website rates as fair and fine for low-clearance cars; allow at least an hour. Note: the Bajo Caracoles fuel pump is 2.6 km south of the junction.

2. Via RP 101

The turn-off leaves RN 40 about 80 km south of Perito Moreno and has 28 km of gravel. The municipal website describes it as in poor condition and for 4x4s, and the official site as a “scenic, winding road with steep grades”. Check its condition before choosing it.

3. On foot from the Cañadón Pinturas Portal

The portal is on RN 40, 54 km south of Perito Moreno. From there the Bajada de los Toldos, the traditional footpath, heads down into the canyon along an old guanaco track, crosses the Río Pinturas on a metal footbridge and climbs a steep staircase to the provincial park's visitor centre, where you register, pay the entrance fee and wait for the next group. It's self-guided and waymarked.

Parque Patagonia rates it as a difficult trail, 1.7 km long and about an hour. La Nación described it as a 4 km round trip taking an hour and a half, not counting the visit. Rewilding Argentina, which upgraded it in 2021 with gentler slopes and steps, reckons 45 minutes to the cave.

Entry to the Parque Patagonia portals has its own fee, separate from the cave ticket: according to its website, AR$6,000, free for Santa Cruz residents, retirees and children under 12 with documents. Fees change: check before you travel at Parque Patagonia Argentina (Fundación Rewilding Argentina).

Distances

According to Turismo Ruta 40, from the Cueva: Bajo Caracoles 47 km, Perito Moreno 118 km, Lago Posadas 120 km and Los Antiguos 176 km. From Perito Moreno, the full trip takes around 2 h 45 min.

Opening hours, visits and tickets

The official site lists: high season (October to April) 9 am to 7 pm and low season (May to September) 10 am to 5 pm, every day. Visits are only with a site guide: according to the municipal website, groups of up to 20 people leave every hour, and times may change due to the weather. Of the various sectors of archaeological value, only one is open to the public. Tiempo Argentino reported in 2022 that helmets are handed out for the tour.

Visits are charged and, according to the municipal website, tickets can be bought on the park's website. Prices change often: check before you travel at Cueva de las Manos (sitio oficial).

Fuel and services

There's fuel in Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos, Bajo Caracoles and Lago Posadas, according to Turismo Ruta 40, which also warns that it isn't always available. The rule is to fill up wherever you can. Parque Patagonia recommends sorting out fuel, cash and shopping in Perito Moreno. The site has Wi-Fi, toilets and a rest area to wait for your visit. Camping is not allowed, and the municipal website recommends bringing food and drink for the day.

Without a car

Small-vehicle tours leave from Perito Moreno and Los Antiguos. According to Parque Patagonia, there are flights to Perito Moreno from Buenos Aires and Comodoro Rivadavia, and buses that reach the Cañadón Pinturas Portal or Perito Moreno: check before you travel at Parque Patagonia – Cómo llegar.

What you'll see

The paintings cover cliff faces and rock shelters on the south side of the canyon. The municipal website dates them to between 9,300 and 1,300 years before present and counts at least 800 hand stencils, in ochres, blacks, reds and violets. Almost all are left hands: Wikipedia, citing rock art studies, records 829 left hands and 31 right hands.

  • Hand stencils: made by placing a hand on the rock and blowing pigment around it, using a hollow bone as a spray tube.
  • Hunting scenes with guanacos in motion, pregnant female guanacos and encircling hunts.
  • Other animals, such as rheas and armadillos, rhea footprints and geometric figures.

The pigments were minerals: iron oxides for red and purple, kaolin for white, natrojarosite for yellow and manganese oxide for black. Until 2021 the paintings were seen behind 2 m bars; Rewilding Argentina replaced them with a 90 cm railing, so today you see them up close. Touching them is forbidden.

A bit of history

The Salesian priest Alberto María De Agostini was the first to mention the site, in 1941, and published the first photos in his book Los Andes del Sur. Carlos Gradin arrived in 1964 and years later led the archaeological research with Carlos Aschero and Ana M. Aguerre. It was declared a National Historic Monument in 1993 and a World Heritage Site in 1999.

For almost 40 years the Perito Moreno municipality was in charge of looking after the site, even though the land was still privately owned. The Provincial Park was formed in 2018, and in July 2020 the province accepted the donation of 600 hectares from the Rewilding Argentina Foundation, which had bought Estancia Los Toldos in late 2015.

Trails and viewpoints of the Cañadón Pinturas Portal

If you have time, the portal adds more than fifty kilometres of self-guided trails. A few of them, with Parque Patagonia's figures:

  • Los Balcones: 1.2 km, 20 minutes, easy. It links viewpoints along the north wall, with cliffs up to 300 m high.
  • Tierra de Colores: 2.6 km, 1 hour, moderate.
  • Koi: 4.8 km, 1 h 45, moderate, up to the top of a mesa.
  • La Guanaca: 6 km, 1 h 45, moderate, up Cerro Amarillo. On clear days you can see Cerro San Lorenzo.

With luck you'll see guanacos, rheas, condors and orange-toothed mountain viscachas (chinchillones anaranjados), which have been reintroduced to the area. From the portal you can also visit the Alero Charcamata, with a guide and a booking (more in Things to do in Perito Moreno).

When to go

The park is open all year, and in high season you get longer visiting hours. Parque Patagonia considers summer, from November to March, the best time, with long, mild days (10 to 24 °C). In winter the weather can change opening times and the state of the gravel roads: check road conditions before you set off.

Where to stay

Camping is not allowed in the provincial park. The nearest options are in Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos and at the Cañadón Pinturas Portal itself, which according to Parque Patagonia offers:

  • Hostería La Posta de los Toldos: nine rooms with private bathrooms and a restaurant. It also runs the El Mollar campsite, which charges a fee and has fenced pitches where you can park.
  • La Señalada campsite, which charges a fee, with windbreaks and a country store.
  • Del Río and La Confluencia campsites, free to use; La Confluencia can only be reached on foot.

For the campsites they suggest booking 15 days ahead; otherwise it's first come, first served. For bookings and fees, check before you travel at Parque Patagonia – Portal Cañadón Pinturas (senderos, campings y hostería).

Possible itineraries

  • Round trip from Perito Moreno: a full day, with the guided tour in the middle.
  • From Los Antiguos: also a full day; leave early, as it's 176 km each way.
  • With a night at the portal: walk down to the cave one day and hike the Cañadón Pinturas Portal trails the next.
  • Traveling south on Route 40: Perito Moreno → Cañadón Pinturas Portal → Cueva (via RP 97) → Bajo Caracoles for the night, or continue to Lago Posadas.

These are our own suggestions.

Tips

  • Leave early: in strong wind the gravel gets slow. Drive slowly and check your spare tyre.
  • Bring warm clothes even in summer, plus water and something to eat.
  • Check road conditions with Vialidad Provincial, especially in winter.
  • Don't touch the paintings or leave the walkways.
  • If you collect stamps: Tiempo Argentino reported in 2022 that you can ask, free of charge, for the Cueva de las Manos stamp in your passport.

To plan the whole trip: Northern Route 40, Perito Moreno and Route 40 in Santa Cruz: fuel, gravel and distances.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel road is there to the Cueva de las Manos?

Via RP 97 from Bajo Caracoles it's 47 km of gravel in fair condition, fine for low-clearance cars. Via RP 101 it's 28 km, in poor condition and for 4x4s, according to the municipal website.

What are the Cueva de las Manos opening hours?

According to the official site, October to April 9 am to 7 pm and May to September 10 am to 5 pm, every day. Confirm before you travel.

Can you visit without a guide?

No. The site can only be visited with a guide, in groups of up to 20 people that leave every hour. What is self-guided is the Bajada de los Toldos trail, which takes you on foot to the visitor centre.

Can you walk there?

Yes. The Bajada de los Toldos trail, rated difficult, starts at the Cañadón Pinturas Portal of Parque Patagonia, on RN 40 54 km south of Perito Moreno; it crosses the Río Pinturas and climbs up to the visitor centre.

Do you need to book?

The municipal website says tickets can be bought on the park's website, and Parque Patagonia notes that visits to archaeological sites must be booked with each operator. It's best to sort it out before you leave, especially in high season.

Can you camp?

Not in the provincial park. You can at the campsites of the Cañadón Pinturas Portal, such as La Señalada, El Mollar, Del Río and La Confluencia.

Where can I get fuel?

In Perito Moreno, Los Antiguos, Bajo Caracoles or Lago Posadas. Fill up wherever you can.
