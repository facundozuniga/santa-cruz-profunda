Home › Articles › Lago Strobel (Jurassic Lake): how to get there, fishing for giant trout and the hooded grebe plateau

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-24

In the middle of Santa Cruz there is a lake that few locals know and that many visiting anglers have on their bucket list. It is Lago Strobel, which the fly-fishing world calls “Jurassic Lake” because of the size of its rainbow trout.

It lies about 115 km from Gobernador Gregores, on a plateau with no towns, and you reach it on ranch tracks that are only passable by 4x4. There are no shops. In return, it has trout weighing several kilos and a plateau with more than 1,500 lagoons where the hooded grebe, a critically endangered bird, breeds.

Where Lago Strobel is

The lake sits on the Lago Strobel plateau, part of the Patagonian plateau, in the Río Chico department in the centre of the province. According to Hispanopedia (Wikipedia content), it lies 715 m above sea level and covers about 65 km².

It is a closed basin: water flows in but there is no outlet. Centuries ago it belonged to the basin of the Chico River, a tributary of the Santa Cruz River, but that outlet is dry today. Its main tributary is the Barrancoso River, which rises near Cerro Dos Cuernos, according to Interpatagonia and Welcome Argentina.

The closest town is Gobernador Gregores. The land around the lake is split among several sheep ranches, and some of them host anglers.

How to get to Lago Strobel

With your own vehicle

Interpatagonia and Welcome Argentina describe leaving Gobernador Gregores north on provincial route 25, with the Cara Riera sign as the first landmark. You then pass the Las Horquetas area and head south across the so-called Meseta del Viento (Wind Plateau), which hides the lake. Within the first 60 km the road crosses the Chico River valley, with groves of trees and a few campsites.

The last stretch is not a public road to the lake: the roads in the area were built to reach the ranches, not the lake. Sources agree it can only be done by 4x4, and Billiken warns that the tracks can become impassable after rain. Interpatagonia recommends checking road conditions before you set off. For provincial roads, check before you travel at AGVP Santa Cruz – daily provincial road report.

With a fishing lodge

Most visitors arrive on a fishing package that includes transfers. In 2020 Fly Fisherman described a lodge at the mouth of the Barrancoso with its own airstrip and weekly charter flights from Comodoro Rivadavia, of about one hour; in the early years, it said, the trip meant about five hours by road from El Calafate. On the south shore of the lake there is also the Estancia Laguna Verde lodge (more in Tourist estancias in Santa Cruz). Check transfers, season and rates with each lodge.

A bit of history

The name honours the Jesuit Matías Strobel. In 1745, together with José Cardiel and José Quiroga, he set out from San Julián Bay and travelled about 120 km west: it was the first recorded European exploration of the interior of that part of southern Patagonia. The nearby Cardiel and Quiroga lakes are named after his companions. If that history interests you, see Things to do in Puerto San Julián.

Human presence goes back much further: the area has rock paintings and engravings that, according to Hispanopedia and Billiken, may be up to 9,000 years old. There are also remains of petrified forests on its shores.

The trout story is recent. According to Fly Fisherman, in 1994 a ranch owner released rainbow trout into the Barrancoso River, planning to farm them for sale. The fish reached the lake, found plenty of food and grew at an unusual rate. In 2005 the first group of visiting anglers arrived, and the nickname “Jurassic Lake”, coined at the height of the Jurassic Park films, stuck for good.

Things to do at Lago Strobel

Rainbow trout fishing

It is why almost everyone comes. According to Fly Fisherman, the trout average 3.5 to 5.5 kg, and in peak season fish over 9 kg are caught nearly every week. Revista Aire Libre reported in 2021, at the end of the season, fish of 8 to 10 kg and many between 4 and 8 kg.

  • You fish by wading. According to Fly Fisherman, the plateau wind makes boats unworkable. On top of that, provincial regulations ban navigation with combustion engines or mechanical propulsion on Lago Strobel.
  • The mouth of the Barrancoso concentrates fish and is the most sought-after spot. Access to each sector depends on the ranch or lodge where you stay.
  • Heavy gear. Fly Fisherman and Aire Libre mention 7 and 8 weight rods for the lake and 6 weight for the river, and heavy-wire hooks, because the trout straighten thin ones.
  • Spinning. According to Aire Libre, in the bays away from the river mouth people also fish with spoons.

2026/27 fishing rules

You need a Santa Cruz fishing permit: see How to get a fishing permit in Santa Cruz, step by step. Under the 2026/2027 regulations (Disposición 241/26), Lago Strobel is open all year, with mandatory catch and release and single hooks with or without barbs, and you must disinfect and wash your gear at the end of the day. On the Barrancoso River release is mandatory along its whole course, the season runs from 1 October 2026 to 3 May 2027 and a daily disinfection certificate is required. Barrancoso River

It is the lake's only significant tributary and its main spawning ground. Aire Libre describes it as a narrow, shallow river with lots of rocks, drops of 30 to 70 cm and small pools where you fish, with silvery trout running up from the lake.

The plateau of lagoons and the hooded grebe

The Strobel plateau has more than 1,500 lagoons, according to a study published in the journal El Hornero (2009). That survey recorded 18 waterbird species, six of them breeding, including the hooded grebe, silvery grebe, flying steamer duck, crested duck, upland goose and white-winged coot.

The hooded grebe is endemic to southern Patagonia and breeds in the lagoons of Santa Cruz's high plateaus. The IUCN lists it as critically endangered. Kini Roesler, director of the Hooded Grebe Project, told Mongabay that the Lago Strobel plateau is the most important of the plateaus for the species. If you are into birdwatching, remember that the colonies are very sensitive: do not approach the nesting lagoons.

When to go

The climate is dry, cold and very windy all year. According to Fly Fisherman, the peak spawning on the Barrancoso is in October and November, and activity continues until late April or early May, when snow and ice arrive. The 2026/27 Barrancoso season (1 October to 3 May) matches that window. In winter the plateau tracks get harder: check conditions before you set off. More on the weather in Weather in Santa Cruz: when to travel, wind and temperatures.

How many days to stay

A day trip from Gobernador Gregores is possible if you have a 4x4 and the tracks are in good shape, but it is about 230 km round trip over rough terrain. If you fish with a lodge, the length of the programme depends on each operator: ask when you book.

Travel tips

  • Fill up in Gobernador Gregores. It is the last service station before the plateau; there are no services at the lake.
  • Tell someone your route. It is an area with no towns and very few vehicles.
  • Bring water, food and warm clothes even in summer: the plateau is cold and windy all year.
  • Respect gates and fields. The land is private: ask permission to cross or camp.
  • Tyres and spare in good condition. Loose stones and tracks are hard on tyres.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Lago Strobel called Jurassic Lake?

Because of the size of its trout. According to Fly Fisherman, the name was coined by the first visiting anglers, who arrived in 2005, at the height of the Jurassic Park films.

Can you get to Lake Strobel without a 4x4?

No. Sources agree that the last stretch, on ranch tracks, can only be done in a four-wheel drive, and even then it depends on the state of the tracks after rain.

Can I keep the trout from Lago Strobel?

No. Under the 2026/2027 fishing regulations, catch and release is mandatory in Lago Strobel and the Barrancoso River.

Is there accommodation near the lake?

Only at ranches (estancias) and fishing lodges in the area. If you want to sleep in a town, the closest is Gobernador Gregores, about 115 km away.

Are the trout in Lago Strobel native?

No. Rainbow trout were introduced in 1994. According to Aves Argentinas, trout stocked in the plateau lagoons eat the invertebrates and change the water, and they are one of the threats to the hooded grebe.
