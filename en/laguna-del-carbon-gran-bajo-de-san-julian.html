Home › Articles › Laguna del Carbón and the Gran Bajo de San Julián: how to get to the lowest point in the Americas

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-28

48 km from Puerto San Julián, the Santa Cruz plateau suddenly drops away. That is where the Gran Bajo de San Julián begins, a huge depression with no outlet to the sea. At its deepest point lies Laguna del Carbón, about 105 meters below sea level: the lowest point in all of the Americas. The town overview is at Puerto San Julián.

This is not a place set up for tourism. There is no tourist infrastructure, and much of the depression lies on private ranches. You can see it easily from Ruta 3; getting down to the bottom is another story. In this guide we explain what you can see, from where, and who to talk to before you set out.

Where the Gran Bajo de San Julián is

The Gran Bajo lies in east-central Santa Cruz, in the Corpen Aike and Magallanes departments. According to Argentina's Geological and Mining Survey (SEGEMAR), it stretches roughly between 49°20' and 49°40' south latitude and between 68°00' and 68°45' west longitude. Wikipedia places it between San Julián Bay and the Chico River.

It is an endorheic basin: the water that flows in has no outlet to the sea and leaves only by evaporation. Because of its size, SEGEMAR describes it as the largest closed depression in the country. Wikipedia and the press give it an area of about 2,900 km².

Laguna del Carbón occupies the lowest sector. According to Wikipedia, it is about 5 km long and 2 km wide, with an area of about 9 km². The most often cited elevation is 105 m below sea level (SEGEMAR and the National Geographic Institute, which gives it as an approximate value); the San Julián municipality says 107 m and the press, between 105 and 107 m depending on the time of year. SEGEMAR ranks it as the fifth deepest continental depression in the world; the municipality, as the seventh.

How to get to Laguna del Carbón

The logical base is Puerto San Julián, on Ruta Nacional 3. From there you head south on the same paved highway.

The viewpoint on Ruta 3

This is the easiest way to see the Gran Bajo. The municipal tourism website says that after 48 km south on Ruta 3 you reach a natural viewpoint overlooking the depression. SEGEMAR places it at km 2,302, a few meters from the entrance to the gas pipeline compressor plant, looking west. We found no official information on where to park: if you stop, pull well off the road and be careful.

Going down to the bottom

SEGEMAR describes the access from km 2,302.5 of RN 3, along the road to the Gasoducto Austral compressor plant. From there, local dirt tracks in fair condition connect outposts and ranches, most of them uninhabited.

That same 2008 report noted that access to the Gran Bajo was closed by the landowners and by TGS, the company that runs the compressor plant. Recent press reports still say the route is unmarked, crosses private land, and that you must notify the local Tourism Office before setting out. We found no up-to-date official information on whether access to the bottom is open today: check before you travel.

Before you go: San Julián Tourism

Puerto San Julián Tourism Office: Av. San Martín 1570. Phone +54 2962 454396 / +54 2962 414221, email centur@sanjulian.gov.ar. According to the provincial website it is open Monday to Friday from 8 am to 8 pm; check before you travel at Turismo Santa Cruz – Puerto San Julián.

We did not find any operators offering trips to the Gran Bajo on the official tourism websites. Ask the Tourism Office whether any option is authorized this season.

A bit of geology

The Gran Bajo is not a crater. According to SEGEMAR, it was shaped mainly by the wind. Over the last five million years, Patagonian gusts swept away the fine materials of the Monte León Formation, very crumbly marine rocks. The gravel and sand plains around them held up better. The result was a relief inversion: where there used to be high ground, there is now a hollow.

The walls and floor of the depression tell a long story:

  • Middle Jurassic (about 165 to 155 million years ago): ignimbrites, lavas and tuffs of the Bahía Laura Group, exposed in the deepest parts.
  • Paleogene (Eocene to Oligocene): fine sediments with remains of leaves and stems.
  • Early Oligocene (about 33 to 30 million years ago): the marine San Julián Formation, with coquinas packed with oysters, snails, sea urchins and corals. It forms the almost continuous cliff, up to 30 m high, that rings the depression.
  • Late Oligocene to Early Miocene (about 25 to 22 million years ago): the Monte León Formation, also marine, with very fossil-rich layers.

Inside the depression there is also a Jurassic petrified forest, with remains of several species of gymnosperms and some ferns. Near the lagoon stands the abandoned Raspuzzi outpost, and inside the depression SEGEMAR mentions a second panoramic spot, the “sillón de Raspuzzi”, overlooking the Laguna del Carbón area.

Natural Monument

The Santa Cruz Legislature declared it a Natural Monument by a resolution of October 28, 2004. In 2008 SEGEMAR warned that there were still no effective protection measures and proposed ranger stations and an interpretation room. We found no official information that rangers or a visitor center exist today, and recent press describes the site as having no tourist infrastructure.

What to do in the Gran Bajo de San Julián

Look at the depression from the rim

This is the most accessible experience. From the plateau you see the land falling away, the low hills and, in the distance, the white of the salt flats. In low light, early in the morning or at sunset, the relief stands out more.

Reach Laguna del Carbón

If you get permission, the floor of the depression shows you the brackish lagoon and the salt crusts left by evaporation. The press recommends a 4x4 and GPS, and SEGEMAR warns that the internal tracks are mostly impassable for two-wheel-drive vehicles.

Look at the fossils, but leave them

The cliffs have visible marine fossils. Fossils are part of the paleontological heritage protected by Law 25,743: do not take or move them. Take photos and leave them where they are.

Combine it with Puerto San Julián

The viewpoint fits easily into a day in San Julián: the Nao Victoria, the Coastal Circuit and the boat trip on the bay. See Things to do in Puerto San Julián.

When to go

The climate is arid, with persistent wind and big temperature swings between day and night. The viewpoint on Ruta 3 can be visited year-round. If you plan to drive the tracks, avoid days after recent rain: the lagoon beds have mudflats (guadales) of fine sediment, and it is best to check the condition of the internal tracks beforehand.

How much time to spend

Driving to the viewpoint and back to San Julián is about 100 km of paved road: half a day is enough. If you get permission to go down to the lagoon, plan a full day, because the track section is slow. These are our suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Fuel: leave Puerto San Julián with a full tank; there are no services in the depression.
  • Water, food and warm clothes: bring everything, even in summer. The wind chills you fast.
  • No signal: according to the regional press, there is no cell signal at the site. Tell someone your route and expected return time, and download offline maps.
  • Private land: respect gates and fences. If you open a gate, close it.
  • Tracks: do not leave the tracks; the salty soil and vegetation are fragile.
  • No 4x4: stay on the highway and at the viewpoint.

To continue your trip: Puerto San Julián, Puerto Santa Cruz, Piedra Buena and Monte León and The coast along Ruta 3.

Frequently asked questions

Is it really the lowest point in the Americas?

Yes. Laguna del Carbón lies about 105 m below sea level, according to SEGEMAR (Argentina's geological survey) and the National Geographic Institute. That is lower than Badwater, in Death Valley (California), at 86 m below sea level.

Where is Laguna del Carbón?

In the Gran Bajo de San Julián, in east-central Santa Cruz. The viewpoint and the access road are on Ruta Nacional 3, 48 km south of Puerto San Julián.

Can you visit Laguna del Carbón on your own?

Not always. Access to the floor of the depression crosses private ranches, and SEGEMAR reported it was closed by the landowners and by the gas pipeline company. Check first with the Puerto San Julián Tourism Office.

Are there organized tours?

We did not find any operators listed on the official tourism websites. Ask the Puerto San Julián Tourism Office whether any trip is authorized.

Can you see anything from Ruta 3?

Yes. 48 km south of San Julián there is a natural viewpoint where you can see the depression and, in the distance, the white of the salt flats.
