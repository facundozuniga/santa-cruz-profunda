Home › Articles › What to do in Río Turbio and 28 de Noviembre: coal, snow, forests and condors

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-10-31

In the far southwest of Santa Cruz, right against Chile, lie Río Turbio and 28 de Noviembre. Together they make up the Coal Basin (Cuenca Carbonífera), and Río Turbio is Argentina’s National Coal Capital. This is not postcard Patagonia: there are mine tunnels, chimneys and miners’ neighborhoods, but also lenga forests, snow in winter and one of the largest groups of Andean condors in the country.

The two towns are about 12 km apart. According to the 2022 census, Río Turbio had 11,670 inhabitants and 28 de Noviembre, 9,481. Puerto Natales, the gateway to Torres del Paine, is about 30 km away. That is why many people drive through without stopping. This guide is here to make you stop. The short profiles are at Río Turbio and 28 de Noviembre.

Where they are and how to get there

Both are in the Güer Aike department. Río Turbio sits at an average of 352 m above sea level, according to Wikipedia. You get there on Ruta Nacional 40 from Río Gallegos or El Calafate, or from Chile through the border crossings near Puerto Natales. If you fly, the province points to the airports of Río Gallegos and El Calafate, and from there you continue by car or bus.

Border crossings to Chile

  • Paso Dorotea: 14 km from Río Turbio, at 618 m. On the Chilean side, route CH-250 reaches Puerto Natales in 27 km (Interpatagonia). In late October 2026, Gendarmería listed opening hours of 05:00 to 18:30 on the Argentine side and Chile’s road authority required carrying chains, according to Ruta0’s summary.
  • Paso Casas Viejas: 19 km from Río Turbio, at 239 m. Puerto Natales is 14 km from the border (Interpatagonia).
  • Paso Cancha Carrera: 47 km north of Río Turbio. According to Interpatagonia, it only opens in summer, from December to March, and leads to the Cerro Castillo area, the access to Torres del Paine.

Hours and openings change with the season and the weather: check before you travel at Migraciones – Pasos internacionales y horarios. Documents and requirements for crossing: check before you travel at Argentina.gob.ar – Recomendaciones para cruzar a Chile.

By bus

Interpatagonia lists offices of several long-distance bus companies in Río Turbio, with services to Río Gallegos, El Calafate and Puerto Natales. We did not find up-to-date official frequencies or timetables: ask each company or the Tourism Office.

A bit of history

On February 22, 1887, navy lieutenant Agustín del Castillo found coal in the area, according to Wikipedia. Mining only arrived in the 1940s, with a clear goal: to stop importing coal from England. YPF created a mineral coal division and in 1943 Yacimientos Carboníferos Fiscales (YCF) set up two camps in Río Turbio, says Interpatagonia. The town takes December 14, 1942 as its founding date.

In 1951 the 285 km, 750 mm gauge railway opened, carrying coal to Punta Loyola, about 20 km from Río Gallegos. According to English Wikipedia, the service was deactivated in 2009 and only occasional trains have run since. You can still see the tracks along the road.

28 de Noviembre was founded in 1959, when the old national territory had already become a province. Its name recalls the date the provincial constitution was enacted, and the founding charter is buried in the middle of Plaza Martín Miguel de Güemes, according to Wikipedia.

Mining history has its harsh side too. On June 14, 2004, a fire and collapse in Mina 5 killed 14 miners. The tragedy is remembered every June 14.

What to do in Río Turbio

Mina 1 complex

Mina 1 was the first mine tunnel in the area. At first the work was done by hand and the coal traveled by truck to Río Gallegos. Today it is a historic and recreational complex at Paraje Mina Uno, about 5 km from downtown, at the base of Valdelén, according to Interpatagonia, which lists the phone number +54 2902 421191. Interpatagonia and Welcome Argentina describe going in with a helmet and a headlamp to see what working in the dark was like. We did not find up-to-date official hours or prices: ask the Río Turbio Tourism Office before you go.

Visiting the working mine

Every December 4, Saint Barbara’s Day, patron saint of miners, YCRT usually organizes a visit inside the active mine, according to La Opinión Austral. In a previous edition, the call had a limit of 400 people, prior registration by email with ID and a photo, ages 16 to 60, and restrictions for people with high blood pressure, claustrophobia, asthma or mobility problems; phones and backpacks are not allowed inside (Editorial RN). One traveler’s account describes about 2 hours inside and nearly 1,000 m of tunnel.

Note: in 2025 YCRT cancelled all Saint Barbara activities because of a labor dispute (Diario Nuevo Día). As of late October 2026 we had not found a call for this year: ask YCRT or the Tourism Office.

Mining museum

The “Dr. Anatol Kowaljow” mining school museum is among the attractions listed by the province. According to Wikipedia, it began in 1978 as a training center for mine workers and was later turned into a museum, with tools, machinery and archives of coal mining. We did not find an official address or hours: ask the Tourism Office.

Bosque de Duendes

It is a sculpture park opened in 2003 in the ravine of the Santa Flavia stream, on RP 20, about 4 km from downtown on the way to Mina 1. According to LM Neuquén (July 2026), it has around 50 sculptures carved with chainsaws into standing dead lenga trees: elves, native wildlife and mining characters, such as “Tonel”, the miners’ dog. The route is just over 3 km; the main trail adds up to about 6 km round trip, and there are loops of under 2 km. Difficulty is low, with boardwalks and bridges, and entry is free all year.

The Tourism Office now and then runs guided walks called “Entre Duendes y Leyendas” (Among elves and legends). We did not find a published schedule: ask at Tourism.

Valdelén ski area

It is about 5 km from town, in the Sierra Dorotea. It is run by the Club Andino Río Turbio, founded in 1976, which turned 50 in 2026. It has 100 skiable hectares among lenga and ñire trees, a T-bar lift and capacity for 500 to 600 people a day, according to the newspaper Río Negro. You can do alpine and Nordic skiing and snowboarding, and there are lessons, equipment rental, snowshoes, sleds, a zip line and snowmobile rides of about 45 minutes (Diario Nuevo Día). From the top you can see the Torres del Paine.

It is a cheap ski area: in July 2026 a day pass cost $60,000 for non-members and $30,000 for members, according to LM Neuquén. It does not sell passes in advance because the season depends on the snow: in 2023 it opened on June 20 and stayed open until late August, and in 2026 it was open at least until August 17. Current dates and prices: valdelen.com.

San José dam, Siete Cascadas and rural tourism

The Dique San José Urban Reserve and the waterfall walk are among the attractions highlighted by the province. Welcome Argentina says that in summer there are outdoor activities and birdwatching at the dam and at the Siete Cascadas (Seven Waterfalls). We did not find official directions to the waterfalls: ask at Tourism how to get there.

According to Welcome Argentina and Interpatagonia, some estancias in the area welcome visitors to take part in sheep marking, shearing and herding. We did not find an official list of which ones open or in which season: ask the Tourism Office.

What to do in 28 de Noviembre

Condor watching

It is the town’s big attraction, and the condor is its emblem. In the April 18, 2025 census, coordinated by the Asociación Somos Huellas Patagónicas, 134 Andean condors were counted within 80 km, a record for the area. The main points were the Glencross (48 birds) and Stag River (45) estancias, Cerro Mirador and the Sierra Baguales, according to LM Neuquén.

The province recommends the Mirador del Cóndor, a viewpoint that shows the shift from steppe to the Andean foothills and is a spot for seeing condors. Since 2010 a provincial law has named 28 de Noviembre the Provincial Capital of the Andean Condor. On July 10, 2025 the Senate unanimously approved naming it National Capital, and the bill went to the Chamber of Deputies; as of late October 2026 we had not found that it was passed. For directions to the viewpoint or guided outings, ask the 28 de Noviembre Tourism Office.

Fishing at Puente Blanco

Sport fishing is a classic very close to town, at Puente Blanco, where old-time anglers say fish of up to 14 kg were caught, according to the province. You need the provincial permit to fish: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (provincia) – Permisos de pesca, temporada 2026/2027.

Around town

Downtown is Plaza Martín Miguel de Güemes, where the founding charter is buried. All around there are forests and steppe, and the Andean foothills are a few kilometers away, great for trekking and birdwatching.

When to go

  • Winter (June to August): snow season at Valdelén, if there is snow. The Provincial Snow Festival takes place in August: in 2026 it was on Sunday the 16th, with slalom, music and a torchlight descent. Santa Cruz’s winter school break in 2026 ran from July 13 to 24.
  • Autumn (March to May): the lengas turn red, according to Welcome Argentina. It is a lovely time for the Bosque de Duendes.
  • Summer (December to February): long days and outdoor outings. Every December 4, Saint Barbara’s Day, the National Coal Festival is held, according to Interpatagonia. Confirm the program with the municipality before you travel.

It is cold all year. According to Wikipedia, the average annual temperature is 5.4 °C, with a record high of 28 °C, a record low of −30 °C and heavy snowfall in winter.

How many days to stay

  • One full day: Mina 1, the Bosque de Duendes and a drive around 28 de Noviembre.
  • In winter: add one or two days to ski at Valdelén.
  • If you are going to Torres del Paine: it works as a one-night stopover.

These are our own suggestions.

Travel tips

  • Warm clothes: bring warm clothing in any month. Wind and cold are constant.
  • Winter: carry chains and check the condition of RN 40 and the border crossings before you leave.
  • Crossing to Chile: valid ID and, if you drive, the car’s papers. Requirements: check before you travel at Argentina.gob.ar – Recomendaciones para cruzar a Chile.
  • Fuel: we did not find official information on service stations along the El Calafate–La Esperanza–Río Turbio stretch. Fill up in every town and leave with a full tank.
  • Accommodation: according to LM Neuquén, the area has about 280 beds and they fill up during the winter break. Book ahead.
  • Río Turbio Tourism Office: Gobernador Lista 288, Monday to Friday 7 am to 6 pm, tel. +54 2902 421950, direccionturismorioturbio@gmail.com, according to Turismo Santa Cruz.
  • 28 de Noviembre Tourism Office: Antártida Argentina 105, Monday to Friday 8 am to 7 pm, tel. +54 9 2966 542350, turismo28denoviembre@gmail.com, according to Turismo Santa Cruz.

To keep traveling: Río Gallegos, El Calafate, Cabo Vírgenes and our guide to Ruta 40 in Santa Cruz.

Frequently asked questions

What to do in Río Turbio in one day?

Start at the Mina 1 complex, walk a short loop of the Bosque de Duendes (they are on the same road) and finish in 28 de Noviembre, about 12 km away. Check Mina 1 opening hours with the Tourism Office first.

Can you go inside an active coal mine in Río Turbio?

Only on specific dates: YCRT usually opens the mine to the public every December 4, Saint Barbara’s Day, with prior registration and limited places. In 2025 the visit was cancelled due to a labor dispute, so ask YCRT whether it is happening this year. Mina 1 can be visited as a historic site.

How far is Río Turbio from Puerto Natales?

About 30 km. You cross at Paso Dorotea or Paso Casas Viejas; check the crossing’s hours and conditions before you leave.

When is there snow at Valdelén?

It depends on each winter: the ski area does not sell passes in advance. In 2023 it opened on June 20 and stayed open until late August; in 2026 it was open at least until August 17, with the Provincial Snow Festival.

Where to see condors in 28 de Noviembre?

The province recommends the Mirador del Cóndor. The April 2025 census counted 134 condors within 80 km of town. For access and guided outings, ask the 28 de Noviembre Tourism Office.
