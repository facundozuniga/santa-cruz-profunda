Home › Articles › Route 3 in Santa Cruz: stretches, distances and stops

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-14

National Route 3 crosses Santa Cruz from north to south along the Atlantic side. It enters the province near Caleta Olivia and leaves it at the Integración Austral border crossing in Monte Aymond, heading toward Chile and Tierra del Fuego. In between there are more than 700 km of steppe, small towns and detours to some of the best spots on the Patagonian coast.

It is the road for those driving down to Ushuaia and the one that links Caleta Olivia, Puerto San Julián, Piedra Buena and Río Gallegos. If Route 40 is the mountain road, Route 3 is the coastal one: more pavement and more towns, but also long empty stretches. The short itinerary is in La costa por Ruta 3; here is the detail on stretches, distances and fuel.

Where it starts and where it ends

According to Route 3 kilometre markers on Wikipedia, Caleta Olivia is between km 1908 and 1911. The access to Puerto San Julián is at km 2252, Comandante Luis Piedra Buena at km 2372 and Río Gallegos between km 2607 and 2609. The border with Chile is at km 2674.

On the other side of the border the road continues on Chilean route CH-255 to the Magellan Strait crossing at Primera Angostura. According to Wikipedia, the ferry takes about 20 minutes to cross the 4.65 km strait. Once on the island, you re-enter Argentina at another crossing (San Sebastián).

If you drive straight through, Caleta Olivia–Río Gallegos is a full day at the wheel. It makes sense to split it in two, overnighting in San Julián or Piedra Buena. Exact timing depends on your pace, the wind and roadworks: allow a buffer.

Road conditions

The Santa Cruz stretch of Route 3 is all asphalt, but it is not even. The National Highways report published by Ruta0 (data from 3 November 2026) marked several Santa Cruz sections as “passable with caution”:

  • Caleta Olivia – Fitz Roy: potholes and loose animals.
  • Junction of RN 3 and RN 281 – Tres Cerros: potholes and loose animals.
  • Other stretches of southern Patagonia show a deformed surface or works depending on the date of the report.

This changes often: before you leave, check the updated status on Ruta0 (which takes data from National Highways) and the AGVP report.

Stops along the route

Caleta Olivia

It is the gateway from Chubut, with full services. City guide: Qué hacer en Caleta Olivia.

Fitz Roy and Jaramillo

They are two small towns that grew up with the old railway. At Fitz Roy is the turnoff to Puerto Deseado. At about km 2,074 of Route 3 the RP 49 turnoff leads to Bosques Petrificados de Jaramillo National Park: about 50 km of gravel according to National Parks and the province. Gravel conditions change with rain: check before you travel on National Parks – Bosques Petrificados de Jaramillo: hours and how to get there.

Tres Cerros

It is a hamlet in Deseado department, between Fitz Roy and San Julián. On Route 3 (km 2114 according to the operator) there is an ENER+ YPF station: its website lists it as a fuel stop 143 km from San Julián and 291 km from Comodoro Rivadavia. Food, shop and lodging tied to the station: check on the spot before counting on them, because roadside services change.

Puerto San Julián

It is 345 km from Caleta Olivia according to InterPatagonia, with access at km 2252. Magellan’s expedition wintered here in 1520. It has a bay, a coastal circuit and is a good place to overnight. Full guide: Guía de Puerto San Julián.

Comandante Luis Piedra Buena

It sits on the bank of the Santa Cruz River, at km 2372. Wikipedia records 9,278 inhabitants in the 2022 Census. Isla Pavón is the classic outing. Guide: Qué hacer en Piedra Buena y el Parque Nacional Monte León.

Monte León National Park

The entrance and the Operations Center are at km 2400 of Route 3, 35 km south of Piedra Buena and 200 km north of Río Gallegos, according to National Parks. Inside the park you drive on RP 63 (19 km). National Parks speaks of more than 40,000 Magellanic penguin pairs and Visit Argentina of more than 60,000. There are lookouts (Cabeza de León, Isla Monte León) and coastal trails.

Park hours according to National Parks: October to April, 9 am–8 pm; May to September, 10 am–5 pm. The penguin colony: 9 am–5 pm (Oct–Apr) and 10 am–3 pm (May–Sep). On rainy days RP 63 can become impassable and coastal access closed: ask at the Intendancy or the Operations Center. As of 5 November 2026 entry is listed as free on National Parks. On camping, sources disagree (Visit Argentina says you cannot overnight; National Parks mentions a free camping area without services): confirm with the Intendancy.

Río Gallegos

It is the provincial capital and the last large city before the border. South on the same Route 3 is the turnoff to Laguna Azul Provincial Reserve; from Río Gallegos you can also drive the 139 km to Cabo Vírgenes (SiFAP: RN 3 + RP 1, with gravel and no services along the way according to Welcome Argentina). City guide: Qué hacer en Río Gallegos.

Crossing to Chile via Monte Aymond

The Integración Austral Border Center, according to argentina.gob.ar, opens at 8. Exits from both countries close at 21:45 and entries at 22:00. Adults: valid DNI or passport; a pending-document certificate is not accepted. Minors need extra paperwork depending on who they travel with (details on the border center page).

If you cross by car, the national government requires a green or blue vehicle card, insurance with international coverage and the plate number etched on the windows; if you are not the owner and do not have a blue card, a notarized authorization from the owner. Details: border procedures.

Monte Aymond (the hamlet) is 66–67 km from Río Gallegos according to Ruta0 and Wikipedia, and about 8 km from the border complex. On the Chilean side CH-255 continues to Primera Angostura, where TABSA runs the Punta Delgada–Bahía Azul ferry. Regional press reported that, from 1 March 2026, TABSA publishes sailings from 8:30 to 23:00, with the last sailing at 22:00 from Punta Delgada and at 23:00 from Bahía Azul. Hours and fares change: check before you travel on TABSA – Transbordadora Austral Broom (Primera Angostura and other crossings).

Leave yourself a buffer so you do not miss the border or ferry hours.

When to drive it

  • October to April: longer days and penguin season at Monte León (National Parks) and along much of the coast. At Cabo Vírgenes, SiFAP marks penguins from September to March.
  • Winter: fewer daylight hours; there can be ice or snow on the pavement, especially near Río Gallegos and the border. Check the road report.
  • Year-round: Route 3 is used; the west wind is a constant and on open stretches it pushes from the side.

How many days to allow

  • Passing through, without detours: 2 days from Caleta Olivia to the border, overnighting in San Julián or Piedra Buena.
  • With the coast: 4 or 5 days to add Puerto Deseado, Monte León and Cabo Vírgenes.
  • Combined with Route 40: near Piedra Buena / Puerto Santa Cruz, RN 288 heads west (Tres Lagos and Route 40). The AGVP daily report for the Piedra Buena district distinguishes paved and gravel stretches on that road; conditions change with rain and snow: check before you travel on Santa Cruz Provincial Highways (AGVP) – Piedra Buena district daily report. You can also go up to El Calafate from Río Gallegos.

These are our suggestions, not official figures.

Travel tips

  • Refuel whenever you can. Between Fitz Roy and San Julián the documented intermediate stop is Tres Cerros (YPF, km 2114 according to ENER+); in Fitz Roy the same operator lists another YPF at km 2009. In small towns stock sometimes runs out.
  • Loose animals. Road reports flag them on almost every stretch. Avoid driving at night.
  • Works and single-lane sections. With active patching there may be one lane, a traffic light or a flagger. Allow extra time.
  • Lodging. In high season it is wise to book ahead or start looking early.
  • Mobile signal. We did not find an official coverage map for the Fitz Roy–San Julián stretch: expect long stretches without signal and download offline maps.

Frequently asked questions

Is Route 3 paved in Santa Cruz?

Yes. The whole Santa Cruz stretch is paved, though several sections have potholes, a deformed surface and patching works. Check the latest conditions before you leave.

How far is it from Caleta Olivia to Río Gallegos?

About 705 km on Route 3, according to InterPatagonia. It is a full day of driving; it makes sense to split it in two.

Where is it best to overnight?

Puerto San Julián and Comandante Luis Piedra Buena are the two most comfortable options for splitting the trip between Caleta Olivia and Río Gallegos.

Is the detour to Monte León worth it?

Yes, if you have at least half a day. The entrance is on Route 3 itself (km 2400), 35 km from Piedra Buena according to National Parks.

What documents do I need to cross to Chile at Monte Aymond?

Valid national ID (DNI) or passport (no pending-document certificate). By car: green or blue vehicle card, international insurance and the plate number etched on the windows. Check the border and TABSA ferry hours on the day you travel.
