Home › Articles › Sport fishing in Santa Cruz: 2026/27 season, permits and where to fish

By Santa Cruz Profunda, from Puerto San Julián · Updated 2026-11-09

Santa Cruz has some of the rivers and lakes most sought after by fly anglers. The Gallegos River is known for its sea-run brown trout, which go down to the sea and come back up the river. Lake Strobel is famous for its big rainbow trout. And the Santa Cruz River adds steelhead, chinook salmon and róbalo (Patagonian blennie).

The general 2026/2027 season runs from 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027, but each water has its own dates, limits and methods: the Gallegos River, for example, only opened on 1 November. Here is how to get the permit, what it costs, the general rules and what you can do on the best-known waters, according to the current regulations (Disposición No. 241/26) and the province’s official fishing portal. Dates and fees change every season: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (province) – Fishing permits, 2026/2027 season.

Who regulates fishing in Santa Cruz

The authority is the State Secretariat of Fisheries and Aquaculture, and enforcement is handled by the Directorate of Inland Fisheries with its inspectors and fishing wardens. For this season the province approved new General Inland Sport Fishing Regulations through Disposición No. 241/26, dated 22 September 2026 and in force since 1 October, according to the Ministry of Production and the text published in the Official Gazette.

If you plan to fish inside a national park

Inside national parks the provincial regulations do not apply; the Patagonian Inland Sport Fishing Regulations do, the same ones used by Neuquén, Río Negro and Chubut, with a specific annex for the Southern National Parks (AICACYP). Under that annex you can only fish the listed waters.

In Los Glaciares National Park, according to the National Parks Administration, fishing runs from 1 November to 1 May and is allowed on Lake Anita, Lake Argentino, the Caterina River, Lake Roca and Lake Viedma. The annex published by AICACYP adds rules per water: in the park sector of Lake Argentino you may fish only from shore, and fishing is banned in Seno de Mayo and Bahía del Viento Norte, near the Perito Moreno Glacier; on Lake Roca catch and release is mandatory; fishing is banned on the lower stretch of the De las Vueltas River inside the park; and felt-soled wading boots are not allowed. Outside the park, on those same lakes, provincial rules apply.

To fish in the park you need the National Parks fishing permit, which is separate from the provincial one. For the process, the park office answers questions at informespnlg@apn.gob.ar. Monte León National Park has its own free permit for shore fishing: we cover it in Things to do in Piedra Buena and Monte León National Park.

Lago del Desierto, north of El Chaltén, is a provincial reserve, not part of the national park: it appears in the provincial annex (see below).

How to get a fishing permit

The provincial permit is issued online through the SIPESQ system, on the pesca.santacruz.gob.ar portal. It is personal, named and non-transferable: carry it printed or on your phone, together with your ID, and show it when an inspector asks. Minors get it through their father, mother or guardian.

According to the official portal, the permit is paid by bank transfer in pesos, attaching the receipt at the end of the application; transfers in foreign currency are not accepted.

Trolling permit

Trolling needs an additional permit: ARS 20,000 a day, ARS 30,000 a week or ARS 40,000 for the season. It does not replace the regular permit: you need both.

General rules worth knowing

  • Daylight only. The fishing day runs from sunrise to sunset as published by the Naval Hydrographic Service for the nearest town. Night fishing is banned throughout the province.
  • One lure, one hook. As a general rule, a single artificial lure with one single or double hook. If it comes with more hooks, remove them or make them unusable. And only one rigged outfit at a time.
  • Release in rivers. In rivers, streams and within 200 m of where they leave or enter lakes, catch and release is mandatory, except as provided in Annex I, using micro-barbed or barbless (crimped) hooks.
  • Possession. The most you can hold is the daily limit for that water. In mandatory catch-and-release areas you cannot keep or carry any fish.
  • No lead. Lead sinkers, weights and lead-headed lures are banned.
  • No motors on rivers. On lakes and reservoirs you may fish from a motorboat under the conditions of the regulations; on rivers motor navigation is banned, except as provided in Annex I.
  • Protected native species. Peladillas, native catfish, lampreys and puyenes may never be killed anywhere in the province: always release them.

What about waters not listed in the annex?

Annex I of the 2026/2027 regulations makes it clear at the very start: on waters not on the list the general rules apply. In other words, fishing there is not banned; the general rules above apply. Note the difference: in national parks it is the other way round, and you can only fish the listed waters.

The regulations also recommend not wading or stepping on fine-gravel beds in rivers and streams until 1 January 2027, so as not to damage spawning redds, and suggest that, where you may keep fish, you take only one small fish (under 30 cm) and release the big ones.

Didymo: clean everything

The invasive alga didymo (Didymosphenia geminata) travels on boots, waders, lines and boats. The regulations require you to enter the province with new or disinfected gear and to disinfect when you change basins. The official method: remove any algae and mud, soak in bleach or hot water (over 60 °C) and dry in the sun for 48 hours before using again. Some waters, such as the Santa Cruz River, have fixed disinfection stations, and on others, such as the Barrancoso River, you must carry the daily disinfection certificate.

Fines

Fines are calculated in units worth the same as the residents’ season permit (ARS 40,000) and range from 5 to 200 units. According to the official portal: fishing without a permit, ARS 400,000; during a closed season, ARS 800,000; with banned gear or chumming, ARS 200,000; selling fish caught for sport, ARS 4,000,000. Full table: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (province) – Fishing permits, 2026/2027 season.

Where to fish: the best-known waters

Annex I for the 2026/2027 season lists dozens of lakes, rivers, streams and lagoons. These are the most popular. Before you go, check the entry for your water: check before you travel at Pesca Santa Cruz (province) – Waters of Santa Cruz, Annex I 2026/2027 season.

Río Gallegos

  • Season: 1 November 2026 to 30 May 2027.
  • Rules: mandatory catch and release on both banks, from the headwaters to the estuary.
  • Exception: from 1 to 28 February 2027 you may keep one sea-run brown trout a day.
  • Closed area: 200 m upstream and downstream of the Güer Aike bridge, on both banks.

According to Fish & Fly, the river is nearly 300 km long, rises from the Penitente and Rubens rivers and is shallow and easy to wade. The same magazine mentions fish of over 25 pounds (about 11 kg). The Penitente and Rubens are open from 1 November 2026 to 3 May 2027, with mandatory catch and release.

Access: we found no official list of public access points or freely accessible stretches of bank. Much of the river runs through estancias, and some lodges have their own beats: Fish & Fly describes more than 40 km of private water at Las Buitreras. Fly Dreamers mentions the Güer Aike bridge on RN 3 as a reference, along with roads that follow the river towards El Calafate and Río Turbio. Do not enter private land without the owner’s permission, and ask the Directorate of Inland Fisheries, which has offices in Río Gallegos. Base for the trip: Río Gallegos.

Its tributary, the Gallegos Chico, is open from 1 November 2026 to 3 May 2027, fly only with mandatory catch and release. It is fully closed from the Puente Nuevo on Route 40 down to its mouth.

Santa Cruz River

  • Open all year.
  • Steelhead: one fish per season; from 15 March 2027, mandatory catch and release.
  • General daily limit: two fish, one perch and one brown trout.
  • Chinook salmon: 1 August to 30 November 2027, one fish a day, any lure with one single or double hook.
  • Róbalo: all year, up to 10 per day, minimum size 30 cm and no lead. It is the only species you may keep under that method.
  • Natural bait (with some exceptions) and lead weights are banned. Disinfection at fixed stations is mandatory.
  • Trolling: only for registered fishing guides, on the stretches set by the regulations.

Bank fishing: the regulations allow it on both banks from the river access at the Monumento Héroes de Malvinas, near Bella Vista street, in Comandante Luis Piedra Buena, up to the Interconectado Provincial power-line crossing. It includes the shore of Isla Pavón, from the RN 3 bridge to the spot known as La Noria, which is marked with signs. More on steelhead and Isla Pavón in Things to do in Piedra Buena and Monte León National Park; for the estuary and róbalo downstream, Things to do in Puerto Santa Cruz.

Lake Strobel

  • Open all year.
  • Mandatory catch and release, with a single hook, barbed or barbless.
  • Navigation with combustion engines or mechanical propulsion is banned.
  • Disinfect and wash your gear at the end of the day.

The Barrancoso River, which flows into the lake, has strict catch and release along its whole course, from 1 October 2026 to 3 May 2027, and requires the daily disinfection certificate.

Why can’t you keep anything? In the grounds of Disposición 241/26, the province cites a study by IPEEC-CENPAT-CONICET on the rainbow trout population of Lake Strobel and the Barrancoso, which recommends banning the killing of fish to ensure the population’s survival.

Turismo Ruta 40 says it is nicknamed “Lago Jurásico” (Jurassic Lake) because of the size of its trout. For reference, Revista Aire Libre reported rainbows of 4 to 8 kg in 2021, and some of 8 to 10 kg, around the mouth of the Barrancoso: those are catches from one trip, not an average.

Getting there: the nearest town is Gobernador Gregores. Interpatagonia and Turismo Ruta 40 put the lake about 115 km from town (other guides say around 130 km). Interpatagonia describes heading north on RP 25, past the Casa Riera sign, and then south from Las Horquetas, across the Meseta del Viento. There is no official road: you get there on estancia tracks, really just ruts, which can become impassable after rain, and you need a 4x4. Check the state of the access before you set out and fill up in Gobernador Gregores. More in Things to do in Gobernador Gregores.

Plateau and Andean lakes

  • Lakes Buenos Aires, Cardiel, Ghio, Posadas, Pueyrredón, San Martín and Viedma: open all year, limit of two fish per angler and a 200 m no-fishing zone at inflows and outflows.
  • Lake Argentino: all year, limit of two, trolling allowed subject to Coast Guard (Prefectura) requirements. It is shared with the National Parks Administration: different rules apply in the park sector (see above).

For Cardiel and the plateau, see Things to do in Tres Lagos and Lake Cardiel; for Posadas and Pueyrredón, Lago Posadas and Ruta 41.

Los Antiguos River

Open all year in two phases. From 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, one fish a day. From 1 April to 30 September 2027, fly only with mandatory catch and release using a single hook. Base: Things to do in Los Antiguos.

Lago del Desierto

  • Season: 1 October 2026 to 3 May 2027.
  • Limit: two fish.
  • Boat fishing by rowing or electric motor only, fly fishing or trolling (with the additional permit).
  • No fires. Disinfect and wash your gear at the end of the day.

It is about 37 km from El Chaltén: What to do in El Chaltén beyond Fitz Roy.

When to go

The general season covers the whole year, but several rivers under special rules, such as the Gallegos, open in November and close in May, and most rivers and streams close on 3 May 2027.

  • Sea-run brown trout on the Gallegos River: February is the only month when you may keep a fish. For the fishing itself, guides and outfitters agree on summer and autumn: Fly Fishing Patagonia considers January and February the key months for the biggest fish, with March and April more consistent in numbers and a late run that usually arrives at the end of March. Fly Dreamers puts the best results from January to March.
  • Chinook on the Santa Cruz River: the window in the regulations is 1 August to 30 November 2027. Note: a new season starts on 1 October 2027, with its own regulations and fees.
  • Lakes: most are open all year, but winter on the plateau is harsh and many access roads are gravel or dirt tracks.

Travel tips

  • Get the permit online before you leave and keep it on your phone or printed, together with your ID.
  • Read the entry for your water in Annex I: dates, limits and methods change from one river to the next.
  • Bring wind and cold-weather gear even in summer: on the plateau and the coast the wind is strong almost all the time and the weather changes fast.
  • Many lakes are far from everything: leave with a full tank, water and food. For RN 40, see Route 40 in Santa Cruz: fuel, gravel and distances.
  • Disinfect your gear every time you change basins.
  • Enquiries: the Directorate of Inland Fisheries answers on +54 2966 429462, Monday to Friday from 8 to 16, and at pesca-continental@santacruz.gob.ar (Avellaneda 801, Río Gallegos).

Frequently asked questions

When does the fishing season start in Santa Cruz?

The general 2026/2027 season started on 1 October 2026 and ends on 30 September 2027. Rivers under special rules, such as the Gallegos, opened on 1 November.

How much is a fishing permit in Santa Cruz?

According to the official portal, for residents of Argentina: ARS 15,000 a day, ARS 25,000 a week and ARS 40,000 for the season. For non-residents: ARS 90,000 a day, ARS 300,000 a week and ARS 450,000 for the season. Fees may be updated: confirm them at pesca.santacruz.gob.ar.

Is the provincial permit valid in Los Glaciares National Park?

No. Inside national parks the Patagonian regulations of the National Parks Administration apply and you need their permit. Questions about the process: informespnlg@apn.gob.ar.

Can I keep the trout?

It depends on the water. In rivers and streams the general rule is mandatory catch and release. On lakes such as Buenos Aires or Cardiel the limit is two fish. On Lake Strobel you cannot keep any.

Can I fish a water that is not listed in the annex?

Yes: according to Annex I of the 2026/2027 regulations, the general rules apply to waters not listed. National parks are different: you can only fish the listed waters.
